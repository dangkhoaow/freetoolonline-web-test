When to Play a Retro FPS in Your Browser

Last reviewed: 2026-08-07

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

Retro FPS Online runs a complete classic-style first-person shooter - the freely licensed game Freedoom - inside a browser tab.

Nothing is installed and no account is created, which makes it the right pick for some situations and the wrong one for others. This page covers which is which.


When it fits

Playing FPS in the browser fits when you want the classic run-and-gun feel without touching the system: a quick session on a computer where you cannot (or do not want to) install games, revisiting the 1990s style legally - Freedoom is a complete free game, not a demo or a pirated copy - or having a shooter that keeps working offline once its one-time download is cached in the browser. Saves stay in the browser between visits, and an export file moves them to another computer.


When it does not fit

Playing a retro FPS in your browser does not fit a phone or tablet without a keyboard: the game is keyboard-first - arrows move, Ctrl fires, Space opens doors - and the page says so instead of promising touch controls.


What to expect on first use

What to expect on first use of Retro FPS Online is a one-time campaign download stated on each card before anything is fetched - about 11 MB per campaign - with later visits starting from local storage. Campaign 1 is four episodes (36 levels); Campaign 2 is 32 levels in one continuous map set.
