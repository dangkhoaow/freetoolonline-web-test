Uluru 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-06

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Uluru as a single sandstone monolith rising abruptly from the flat central-Australian desert plain, its flanks marked by near-vertical rock grain and shallow ridge-and-groove weathering rather than the layered banding or pillar fields seen on the other landform explorers.

Uluru stands about 348 m above the surrounding plain (roughly 863 m above sea level at its summit) and measures about 9.4 km around its base. The sandstone was deposited roughly 550 million years ago and later tilted nearly vertical, which is why its grain runs steeply up the rock face instead of lying in flat horizontal layers. Uluru is famous for appearing to change color through the day, most strikingly glowing deep red-orange at sunset as low-angle light scatters off iron oxide in the rock surface. The domed rock formations of Kata Tjuta lie about 25 km to the west and are visible from Uluru's summit approach on a clear day.

  • Toggle the sunset-glow color shift on the rock face
  • Toggle the Kata Tjuta marker to the west
  • Read height, circumference, and rock-age figures in the panel
  • See a single rising monolith, not a bowl, pillar field, or gorge
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

Everything renders on your device with WebGL. The 3D engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is cached.

This is an educational approximation - rock grain texture, weathering, and sunset lighting are procedural teaching cues, not survey geology.
