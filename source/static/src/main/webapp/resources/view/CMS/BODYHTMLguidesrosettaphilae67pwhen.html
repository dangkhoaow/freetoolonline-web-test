When to Use the Rosetta + Philae 67P 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-11

Open the Rosetta + Philae 67P 3D Explorer when you want a fast, real-dates answer to how the first-ever landing on a comet actually went - including the part where it did not go as planned - and how the mission itself ended.


Sessions this explorer fits

Students and teachers explaining why real space missions plan for failure recovery, readers who want the true story behind "first comet landing" instead of a headline without the harpoon-failure detail, and anyone curious why a comet has two lobes or what happens to it near the Sun all get a direct answer here.


When 67P's landing timeline is the wrong page for your question

Skip this explorer if your question is really about how any comet swings around the Sun in the first place - the shape of that long looping path, perihelion and aphelion, and why the tail grows and shrinks - because that general orbital-mechanics story lives on the Comet Orbit 3D Explorer rather than in 67P's specific landing timeline. It is also the wrong page if you came to study how a spacecraft grabs a physical sample off a small body and flies it home, since Rosetta dropped a lander to stay on 67P and never returned material - that touch-and-go sampling technique is shown on the Hayabusa2 Ryugu Sample Return 3D Explorer instead. Once you know this is the right page, the step-by-step guide walks you through the controls.
