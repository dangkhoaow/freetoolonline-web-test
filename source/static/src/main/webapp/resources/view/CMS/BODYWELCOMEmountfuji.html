Mount Fuji 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-28

Author: FreeToolOnline Geographic Team

Rotate around Mount Fuji, Japan's tallest peak at 3,776 meters, and toggle two features: the snow-capped summit (visible roughly November through May) and the perpetual cloud bands that often encircle the mountain's lower slopes.


Key facts

  • Summit: 3,776 meters above sea level, a nearly perfect symmetric cone at the crater rim.
  • Location: Honshu, Japan, near Tokyo - visible from about 100-130 kilometers away on clear days.
  • Volcanic status: active stratovolcano, dormant since its last eruption in 1707-1708 (the Hoei era eruption, lasting 49 days).
  • Fuji Five Lakes at the base: Kawaguchiko, Yamanakako, Saiko, Shojiko, and Motosuko.
  • Heritage: designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013, reflecting its profound cultural significance in Japanese tradition and aesthetics.

How the scene is built

After the procedural cone loads, this explorer fetches real elevation data - Terrarium terrain tiles from Mapzen and AWS Open Data, derived from NASA/USGS digital elevation models - plus true-color Sentinel-2 satellite imagery of the volcano, and swaps them in over the same footprint. The radial cone shape and summit crater you see are then measured terrain, not a procedural sketch.


Privacy and browser support

The scene runs entirely in your browser with a site-vendored 3D build - nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server. If WebGL is unavailable on your device, the page tells you so and still renders the facts panel, so the key figures remain readable.
