Depth: how this labeled map differs from a generic spiral

The labeled map differs from the generic Galaxy 3D Simulator in one deliberate way - every structure here is named, not just suggested. Where the Galaxy 3D Simulator regenerates an unlabeled spiral for mood and motion, this scene fixes the disk at about 100,000 light-years across and plants a yellow Sun marker on an orbit ring roughly 26,000 light-years out from the core. The Sun, Sagittarius, Orion (Local), Perseus, and Center focus buttons each snap the facts panel to that feature, so you can jump straight to a named arm instead of hunting across a blank spiral. Press Play spin and the disk turns slowly while the arm labels stay upright and readable, a pacing choice tuned for a classroom rather than a screensaver. The panel also carries the numbers a generic spiral leaves out - diameter, Sun radius, the three teaching arms, and a galactic year of about 230 million years (commonly cited in the 225 to 250 range). Reach for this labeled map when you need to place the Sun in a named structure, and use Galaxy 3D Simulator when an unlabeled spiral is all the scene calls for.
