Denali 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Denali as a schematic Alaska Range massif: the 6,190 m summit rising from a low-elevation base, a translucent band marking its record base-to-peak vertical rise, and the Kahiltna Glacier tongue flowing down one flank.

Denali - "the tall one" or "the great one" in Koyukon Athabascan, officially renamed from Mount McKinley in 2015 - stands in the Alaska Range within Denali National Park and Preserve, Alaska. Because its base sits near sea level rather than atop a high plateau, Denali rises roughly 5,500 m from base to summit in about 20 km of horizontal distance - a greater base-to-peak vertical rise than any other mountain fully above sea level, including Mount Everest, which climbs "only" about 3,650 m above its own high Tibetan Plateau base. Walter Harper, a member of the Stuck-Karstens-Tatum expedition, became the first person confirmed to reach the true summit on 1913-06-07.

Orbiting Denali, toggling the rise band, and switching the glacier on and off all happen on your own device: the schematic terrain is drawn with WebGL and the vendored three.js engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is cached, so returning to spin the peak or re-read the figures needs no re-download and no upload.

This is an educational approximation - the ridge shape, glacier tongue, and rise band are schematic teaching cues, not survey terrain.
