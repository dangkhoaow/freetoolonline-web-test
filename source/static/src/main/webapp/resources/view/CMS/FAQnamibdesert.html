Frequently Asked Questions

Where is the Namib Desert?

Along Namibia's Atlantic coast, stretching roughly 2,000 km into southern Angola and northwestern South Africa, and 50 to 160 km wide.

Is the Namib really the oldest desert in the world?

It is widely described that way - arid to semi-arid conditions here are estimated to have persisted for at least 55 to 80 million years.

How tall are the dunes?

Dune 7 near Walvis Bay reaches about 383 m, and "Big Daddy" near Sossusvlei reaches about 325 m, among the tallest dunes on Earth.

Why do some dunes look more red than others?

Dune sand is coated in iron oxide. Younger dunes near the coast read pale apricot, while older dunes further inland read deep burnt orange to red as the coating oxidizes further with age.

Where does the desert get water?

From a persistent Atlantic fog belt driven by the cold offshore Benguela current, which rolls inland most mornings and is the desert's main source of moisture.

What is the Skeleton Coast?

The fog-fed northern strip of the Namib, named for the shipwrecks and whale and seal bones exposed on its beaches.

What is welwitschia?

A slow-growing, fog-dependent plant found only in this desert. Some individuals are estimated to be over 1,000 years old, surviving on fog moisture alone.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the dune shapes, color gradient, fog bank, and plant markers are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
