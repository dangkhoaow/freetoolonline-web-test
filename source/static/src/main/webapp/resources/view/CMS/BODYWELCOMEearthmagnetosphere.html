Earth Magnetosphere 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-09

Earth's magnetosphere is a teaching schematic here - dipole field lines, a compressed sunward magnetopause, and optional solar-wind particles - not an MHD model.

Surface field intensity is about 25-65 uT. The magnetic dipole tilts about 11 deg from Earth's spin axis. Solar wind compresses the sunward magnetopause to roughly 6-10 Earth radii (Re) - Quiet about 10 Re and Compressed about 6 Re presets match that published range.

Adjust tilt and magnetopause standoff, or hide the solar-wind particle stream. The facts panel lists the same figures.

  • Dipole field-line cage with adjustable about 11 deg tilt
  • Orange magnetopause wire shell at 6-12 Re (teaching standoff)
  • Quiet about 10 Re and Compressed about 6 Re presets
  • Optional solar-wind particles from the Sunward side
  • Surface field about 25-65 uT in the facts table
  • Complements aurora (emission curtains) and solar-wind-heliosphere (AU-scale bubble)
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

Students see why the day side is squashed; teachers demo magnetopause standoff without a blackboard sketch; curious readers connect field tilt to the aurora oval's magnetic frame.

Which Space 3D explorer covers which scale

This explorer owns the near-Earth field and boundary: dipole tilt about 11 deg and a sunward magnetopause about 6-10 Re. Two siblings cover the scales it deliberately leaves out - Aurora 3D for what the precipitating particles look like at 80-300 km, and Solar Wind Heliosphere 3D for the AU-scale bubble the solar wind carves.

The dipole field-line cage, magnetopause shell, and optional solar-wind stream all draw on your device with WebGL. The three.js engine downloads once (about 0.7 MB) and then stays cached, so every tilt and magnetopause standoff you adjust is recomputed locally - none of the magnetosphere scene is uploaded or sent to a server.

This is an educational dipole + boundary schematic - not an MHD magnetosphere model, not a live solar-wind or IMF feed, and not a Van Allen belt particle solver.
