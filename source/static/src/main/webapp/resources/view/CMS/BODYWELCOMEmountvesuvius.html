Mount Vesuvius 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-01

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Mount Vesuvius rising about 9 km east of Naples, Italy - the only volcano on the European mainland to have erupted within the last hundred years. The modern cone grew inside the horseshoe-shaped remnant of an older, larger volcano called Monte Somma, whose ancient caldera rim survives as a ridge open toward the south, where it collapsed long ago.

Vesuvius is best known for its 79 AD eruption, which buried the Roman towns of Pompeii and Herculaneum in ash and pyroclastic material, preserving them for centuries. The volcano has erupted many times since, most recently in March 1944. Today, a "Red Zone" evacuation plan covers roughly 600,000 people living around its base, making it one of the most closely monitored volcanoes in the world.

  • Toggle the Monte Somma ridge to see the ancient caldera rim that partially encircles the modern cone
  • Toggle the hazard zone to see a schematic ring approximating today's evacuation area around the base
