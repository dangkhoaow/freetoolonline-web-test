Why long 4K and H.265 clips stall in the browser

Browser ffmpeg runs inside a WebAssembly sandbox that most builds cap at 2 GB of addressable memory, and only the newer memory64 builds lift that ceiling to roughly 4 GB. A single 12-minute 4K clip encoded in H.265 with a 5.1 audio track has to hold both the decoded frames and the output buffer in that same 2 GB window, so it routinely runs past the limit and the progress bar freezes near 0 percent. As a rough working line, files under about 500 MB convert comfortably in the tab, while anything above that is where you either trim the source first or switch to a local ffmpeg pass. The fastest way to shrink a long recording before you upload is to cut it into two or three shorter segments and convert them one at a time, since each segment gets its own fresh sandbox. When the file is genuinely large - a full studio master rather than a phone clip - a desktop ffmpeg run has no sandbox ceiling at all and finishes in one pass, which is why the size threshold, not the format, is the real decision point.
