This browser explorer shows Ha Long Bay in cross-section: a dense field of steep gray-tan limestone islands rising straight from an emerald water plane, most too sheer to hold more than a cap of green tropical vegetation near the top, with a marker at the bay's tallest island in the scene standing in for Sung Sot (Surprise) Cave.

Ha Long Bay sits in the Gulf of Tonkin off Quang Ninh province in northeastern Vietnam. The name translates to "descending dragon" and is tied to a local folk legend of dragons sent to defend the coast, whose thrashing tails are said to have carved the bay's channels. The limestone islands were shaped over roughly the last 20 million years as tropical rain and sea water dissolved the rock along cracks and fault lines, leaving thousands of steep-sided towers standing apart in the water rather than one connected coastline. The bay has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1994, and its core protected zone covers about 1,553 sq km holding an estimated 1,600-2,000 islands and islets, about 775 of which are named.
