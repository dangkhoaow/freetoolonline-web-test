Frequently Asked Questions

What do the A, B, C, M, and X letters mean?

They are GOES flare classes based on the Sun's peak soft X-ray flux in the 1-8 angstrom band: A is below 1e-7 W/m^2, B is 1e-7 to below 1e-6, C is 1e-6 to below 1e-5, M is 1e-5 to below 1e-4, and X is 1e-4 W/m^2 or higher, with no formal upper cap.

What does the number after the letter mean?

Each letter class subdivides linearly from 1.0 to 9.9. An M5 flare is 5x10^-5 W/m^2; an X1 flare is 1x10^-4 W/m^2; an X20 flare is 2x10^-3 W/m^2.

What is the NOAA Radio Blackout (R) scale?

It is NOAA's 5-level scale for how a flare of a given class disrupts high-frequency radio on Earth's sunlit side: R1 (Minor) starts at M1, R2 (Moderate) at M5, R3 (Strong) at X1, R4 (Severe) at X10, and R5 (Extreme) at X20. Classes A through C carry no Radio Blackout rating.

At what class does NOAA issue an alert?

NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center issues an alert once a flare reaches M5 (R2 - Moderate).

What is the biggest solar flare ever recorded?

The largest confirmed flare on record is the X28 flare of 4 November 2003, part of the "Halloween storms". The GOES sensor saturated near its peak, so the true value may have been even higher.

Is the flare animation showing the real brightness and speed?

No. The flare loop's size and glow, the particle-burst intensity, and the Earth companion's reach arc are scaled for visibility, not rendered from a radiative-transfer or magnetic-reconnection model. The flux ranges and R-scale figures in the facts panel are the real NOAA SWPC ones.

How is this different from the Coronal Mass Ejection page?

A coronal mass ejection is the plasma cloud a flare can throw outward, tracked as it travels; this page is the X-ray brightness classification scale for the flare itself, which can happen with or without an accompanying ejection.
