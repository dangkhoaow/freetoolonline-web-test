Waitomo Caves 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-07

This browser explorer shows the Waitomo Glowworm Cave as a winding limestone passage carved by an underground stream, its ceiling scattered with small points of bioluminescent glow - a cave archetype distinct from the layer-stack of Mammoth Cave and the open doline-lit passage of Son Doong Cave already shown on the other cave explorers.

Waitomo Caves sits near Otorohanga in the Waikato region of New Zealand's North Island. It was explored in 1887 by local Maori chief Tane Tinorau, guided by English surveyor Fred Mace, who rafted along the underground Waitomo Stream and found a ceiling glittering with tiny lights. The cave was carved through limestone laid down under an ancient sea roughly 30 million years ago, later uplifted and eroded by the stream that still flows through it today. The lights come from Arachnocampa luminosa, a glowworm species found only in New Zealand: the larvae glow to lure flying insects into sticky silk threads they dangle from the ceiling. Tane Tinorau and his wife Huti opened the cave to visitors in 1889, and their descendants still co-manage tours of the Glowworm Grotto by boat today.
