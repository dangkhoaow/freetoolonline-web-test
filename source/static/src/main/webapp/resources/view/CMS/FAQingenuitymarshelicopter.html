Frequently Asked Questions

When did Ingenuity make its first flight?

On 2021-04-19, climbing about 3 m (10 ft) and hovering about 30 seconds - the first powered, controlled flight by an aircraft on another planet.

How fast does Ingenuity's rotor spin?

About 2,400 rpm by original design, later raised to about 2,700 rpm as the Martian atmosphere thinned seasonally. It was ground-tested, not flown, to 2,800 rpm.

Why does it need to spin so much faster than an Earth helicopter?

Mars's atmosphere is only about 1% as dense as Earth's at the surface, so the rotors must spin roughly five times faster than a typical Earth helicopter's 400-500 rpm main rotor to generate enough lift.

How many flights did Ingenuity complete?

72 flights total, far beyond its original 5-flight, 30-Martian-day technology-demonstration plan, logging more than 2 hours of cumulative flight time.

How did Ingenuity's mission end?

On its final flight (Flight 72, 2024-01-18) it reached about 12 m (40 ft), hovered about 4.5 seconds, then lost contact with Perseverance about 1 m above the ground while descending. Images later showed rotor-blade damage; NASA declared the mission over on 2024-01-25.

How did Ingenuity get to Mars?

It rode to Mars attached to the belly of the Perseverance rover, landing with it in Jezero Crater on 2021-02-18. Perseverance also relayed Ingenuity's data to Earth.

Is the on-screen terrain and helicopter to scale?

No - the terrain, rover, and helicopter models are procedural shapes for teaching purposes, not a terrain-accurate reconstruction of Jezero Crater. Only the flight numbers, dates, altitudes, and rpm figures in the facts panel are real published figures.

Where do these figures come from?

NASA JPL's Ingenuity mission pages, NASA's "6 Things to Know About NASA's Ingenuity Mars Helicopter," and NASA JPL's mission-end announcement.
