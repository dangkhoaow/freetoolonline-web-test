Frequently asked questions

Will the JPG copies take more space than the HEIC originals?

Usually, yes. HEIC stores a photo in noticeably less space than a JPG of similar visual quality, so a folder of 100 converted photos tends to grow. If storage matters, use a lower quality setting for photos you only view on a screen and keep a higher one for photos you may print.

Should I delete the HEIC originals after converting?

Keep them until you have opened a few of the JPGs in the app or website that needed them. JPG is a lossy format, so a JPG cannot be turned back into the original HEIC quality, and Live Photo motion and depth data are not carried over. Once the JPGs work where you need them, removing the originals is only a storage decision.

Can I convert some photos now and the rest later?

Yes. Each batch is separate, so you can convert the photos in several smaller sets and put all the JPGs in one folder afterwards. Splitting a large batch is also the usual fix when a browser tab runs short of memory partway through.
