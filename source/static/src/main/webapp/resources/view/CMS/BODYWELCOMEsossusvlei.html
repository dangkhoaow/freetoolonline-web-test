Sossusvlei 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-09

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Sossusvlei as a field of star-shaped dunes rising from the Namib Desert floor, their radiating ridges built by wind blowing from several directions at once. At the foot of the tallest dune sits Deadvlei, a white clay pan holding the blackened skeletons of camel thorn trees that died centuries ago but never rotted away.

Sossusvlei sits inside Namib-Naukluft National Park in Namibia, part of the Namib Sand Sea UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Namib is considered the world's oldest desert, hyper-arid for roughly 55 to 80 million years, and some parts of it receive well under 10 mm of rain a year. The deep red-orange color of the older dunes comes from a coating of iron oxide - the redder the sand, the longer it has been oxidizing in place.

  • Toggle iron-oxide color bands to contrast younger, paler sand against the deep red of older dune crests
  • Toggle the Deadvlei tree markers on the white clay pan
  • Read dune-height, desert-age, and tree-age figures in the panel
  • See the star-dune shape formed by multi-directional wind
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload
