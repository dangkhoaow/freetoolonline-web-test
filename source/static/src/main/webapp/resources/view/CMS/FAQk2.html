Frequently Asked Questions

How tall is K2?

Its summit sits at 8,611 m (28,251 ft) above sea level, in the Karakoram range on the China-Pakistan border - the second-highest peak on Earth after Mount Everest (8,849 m).

Why is K2 called the "Savage Mountain"?

K2 has a historically very high fatality-to-summit ratio, roughly one death for every four successful summits over its climbing history, though the ratio has improved with modern equipment and weather forecasting. Its steep, abrupt rise from the glacier base also makes it technically harder to climb than the taller Everest.

What is the Abruzzi Spur?

The ridge on K2's southeast side that carried the first successful summit team to the top in 1954. It remains the most-climbed route on the mountain today.

What is the Bottleneck?

A narrow couloir at about 8,200 m directly beneath a huge overhanging ice serac - one of the most dangerous single sections on the route. The 2008 K2 disaster, in which 11 climbers died in one incident, happened here.

When was K2 first climbed?

July 31, 1954, by Lino Lacedelli and Achille Compagnoni, on an Italian expedition led by Ardito Desio, via the Abruzzi Spur.

When was K2 first climbed in winter?

January 16, 2021, when a team of ten Nepali climbers led by Nirmal Purja and Mingma Gyalje Sherpa reached the summit together - the last of the world's 8,000 m peaks to fall to a winter ascent.

Why is it called "K2" instead of a local name?

It was the second peak surveyed in the Karakoram range by T.G. Montgomerie in 1856. Unlike most major peaks, it never acquired a widely used local name recognized internationally, so the survey designation stuck.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the pyramid shape, route line, and serac position are schematic teaching cues, not survey terrain.
