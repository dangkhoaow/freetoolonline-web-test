Vatnajokull 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Vatnajokull as a broad ice dome draped over irregular ground, with two active volcanoes hidden beneath the ice and a calving front feeding icebergs into the Jokulsarlon lagoon at the southern edge. Toggle the lagoon and the subglacial volcano markers.

Vatnajokull is Europe's largest ice cap by volume, covering about 7,700 sq km of south-east Iceland - roughly 8% of the country's land area. Beneath the ice sit two of Iceland's most active volcanoes, Grimsvotn and Bardarbunga, whose heat periodically melts pathways through hundreds of metres of ice above them. At the ice cap's southern edge, the Breidamerkurjokull outlet glacier has retreated since the 1930s and 1940s, leaving behind the Jokulsarlon lagoon, where calved icebergs drift before washing out to the nearby Diamond Beach.

The whole scene - the ice dome, the subglacial terrain beneath it, the calving front, and the Jokulsarlon lagoon - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to see the dome's broad shield shape, and toggle the volcano markers to see where the heat sources sit beneath the ice. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the ice-dome relief, subglacial terrain, and lagoon geometry are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
