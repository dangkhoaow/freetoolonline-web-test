Serengeti Plains 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-28

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Serengeti Plains as an almost flat grassland broken by ancient granite kopjes rising abruptly from the grass - the stage for the wildebeest Great Migration. Toggle the grass color between the golden dry season and the green wet season, and the marker tracing the migration's roughly circular annual route.

Serengeti National Park covers about 14,750 sq km of northern Tanzania, and the greater Serengeti ecosystem it belongs to spans roughly 30,000 sq km across Tanzania and into Kenya's Maasai Mara. The plains sit at an average elevation of roughly 1,500-1,850 m. Each year about 1.5 million wildebeest, along with hundreds of thousands of zebra and gazelle, follow the rains in a large roughly circular route across the ecosystem - the Great Migration. Scattered across the grass are granite kopjes, ancient Precambrian rock outcrops among the oldest exposed rock in the region, which provide rare shade, lookout points, and shelter for wildlife on the otherwise open plains. Serengeti National Park was established in 1951 and the wider area has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site (natural) since 1981.

The whole scene - the rolling grass, the scattered granite kopjes, the seasonal color shift, and the migration trail - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to see how the kopjes break up the flat plain, and toggle the season to see the grass shift from golden dry-season tones to green wet-season tones. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the plain, kopjes, grass-color seasonality, and migration trail are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
