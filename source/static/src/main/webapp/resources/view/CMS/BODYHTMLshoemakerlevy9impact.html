About this scene

The facts panel fills when the scene loads. Scrub the progress slider to move through the real impact timeline.

Published figures (NASA/JPL and Hubble Space Telescope observations, cross-checked 2026-08-11): Jupiter's own gravity tore Comet Shoemaker-Levy 9 apart on 7 July 1992, and 11 of its named fragments (of at least 21 total) struck the planet's southern hemisphere between 16 and 22 July 1994 at about 60 km/s. Fragment G's 18 July impact alone released energy estimated at about 6 million megatons of TNT and left a dark scar whose outer ring spans about 12,000 km - comparable to Earth's diameter.

Drag the progress slider (or press Play) to watch the fragment train approach and strike Jupiter one impact at a time, and press "Show Earth-size overlay" to compare Fragment G's real scar size directly against Earth.
