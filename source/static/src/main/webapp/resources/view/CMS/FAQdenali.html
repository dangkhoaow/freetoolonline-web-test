Frequently Asked Questions

How tall is Denali?

6,190 m / 20,310 ft above sea level, per the 2015 USGS survey - the highest peak in North America.

Why does Denali have a bigger base-to-peak rise than Everest?

Denali's base sits near sea level, so it climbs about 5,500 m from base to summit. Everest's base already sits on the high Tibetan Plateau, so it climbs only about 3,650 m above its own base even though its summit is higher above sea level.

Is Denali the same mountain as Mount McKinley?

Yes - the peak was officially renamed Denali in 2015, restoring the Koyukon Athabascan name meaning "the tall one" or "the great one".

Who climbed Denali first?

Walter Harper, a member of the Stuck-Karstens-Tatum expedition, became the first person confirmed to reach the true summit on 1913-06-07.

What is the Kahiltna Glacier?

Denali's largest glacier, about 70 km long, flowing down the mountain's southwest side - the standard approach route for most climbing expeditions starts on it.

Is this a terrain simulation?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the ridge shape, glacier tongue, and rise band are schematic teaching cues, not survey terrain.
