The live wildfire map, official agency incident pages, full GIS fire dashboards, and mobile alert apps mostly read the same federal incident data. They differ in what you trade for depth: install time, page weight, notifications, and privacy. The table below is the short answer.

When this page wins

You want the national or state picture in seconds: how many fires, which are big, how contained, and how far from you - with nothing installed and nothing tracked. The dot map plus the largest-first list answers that faster than loading a tile-based dashboard, and the built-in NWCG size-class explainer decodes the jargon on the spot.


When an alternative wins

Evacuation and life-safety alerts: a mobile alert app or official local channels - this page has no notifications and the federal feed lags agency reporting by hours. Perimeter shapes and satellite hotspot layers: an agency GIS dashboard; this page deliberately plots point locations on a coordinate grid with no basemap tiles. Deep detail on one fire (closures, evacuation zones, daily updates): that fire's official incident page.


Same data, different packaging

All of these ultimately trace back to US interagency reporting (NIFC WFIGS and the agencies that file into it), so acreage and containment numbers should broadly agree everywhere. What you are really choosing is packaging: speed and privacy here, layers and depth in a dashboard, alerts in an app. To see how this page works in practice, start with the step-by-step walkthrough or check when it is the right choice.
