Matterhorn 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-12

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Matterhorn as a classic glacial "horn": four steep triangular faces meeting at a sharp point, each face separated from its neighbors by a sharp ridge. The summit sits at 4,478 m on the border between Zermatt, Switzerland (to the northeast) and Breuil-Cervinia, Italy (to the south), in the Pennine Alps.

A horn peak like this forms when glaciers erode a mountain mass from several sides at once, cutting back toward the center until only the sharp ridges and concave faces remain. The four ridges have names still used by climbers today: the Hoernli ridge (northeast, the standard Swiss route), the Zmutt ridge (northwest), the Lion or Italian ridge (southwest), and the Furggen ridge (southeast). Toggle the Hoernli ridge marker to see the standard climbing route traced up the northeast ridge, with a small marker near the Hoernli Hut.
