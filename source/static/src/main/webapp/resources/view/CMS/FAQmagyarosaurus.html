Frequently Asked Questions

How big was Magyarosaurus?

Detailed skeletal studies put adult body length as small as about 2.16 to 2.82 m and mass around 660 to 972 kg, making it one of the smallest known adult sauropods. Broader estimates for the genus go up to about 6 m - sources disagree because only partial skeletons are known. Turn on the human figure in the viewer to see the scale against a 1.8 m person.

Was it a baby dinosaur, not a small species?

No. Bone-microstructure analysis shows these individuals had stopped growing, confirming they were fully grown adults rather than juveniles. The small size is a real trait of the species, called insular dwarfism.

Why was Magyarosaurus so small?

It lived on Hateg Island, an isolated landmass in the Late Cretaceous in what is now Romania. Limited food and living space on islands often drives large animals to evolve smaller body sizes over generations - the same pressure that produced other dwarfed animals sharing the island, such as the hadrosaur Telmatosaurus.

When and where did Magyarosaurus live?

In the Late Cretaceous, about 71 to 66 million years ago, on Hateg Island in what is now Transylvania, Romania. Franz Nopcsa first described it in 1915 from fossils his sister found in 1895; Friedrich von Huene placed it in its own genus, Magyarosaurus, in 1932.

Is the model scientifically accurate?

The proportions follow published figures, but the skin color and soft-tissue outline are an artistic reconstruction - fossils preserve bone, not soft tissue or color. This model is not a fossil-accurate skeleton. The length, weight, and age figures shown are real published values from the sources described on this page.

Do I need to install anything to view it?

No. The model renders in your browser with WebGL - no app, no account, and nothing about your visit is sent to a server. The 3D engine loads once and is then cached.
