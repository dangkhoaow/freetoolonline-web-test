How to Check a Vehicle for Open Recalls - Step by Step

Last reviewed: 2026-08-10

The vehicle recall lookup answers one question: does this vehicle have open US safety recalls? You enter a 17-character VIN or the year, make, and model; the page pulls the matching NHTSA campaigns and lists them newest first. Nothing to install, no account - here is each step.


Step 1 - open the page

Go to the vehicle recall lookup. An example lookup renders the moment the page loads, so you can see what results look like before typing anything - the layout you see for the example is exactly what your own query returns.


Step 2 - pick a mode and enter the vehicle

The fastest path is the vehicle mode: pick the model year from the dropdown, then type the make and model - both fields offer suggestion lists as you type. If you have the VIN (the 17-character plate at the base of the windshield or on the driver-door jamb), switch to the VIN mode instead: the page decodes the year, make, and model with the official NHTSA vPIC service first, shows you what it decoded, then pulls the matching campaigns.


Step 3 - read the campaign cards

Each campaign card shows the campaign number, report date, affected component, the problem, the safety risk, and the fix, newest first. High-severity flags render as badges straight from the NHTSA data: do-not-drive (park it), park-outside (fire risk), and over-the-air update. A no-recalls result states plainly that NHTSA lists no active campaign for the query, with a link to confirm at nhtsa.gov/recalls.


Step 4 - act on a match

Recall repairs are performed free of charge by the manufacturer's authorized dealers. Call a dealer for your brand, mention the campaign number from the card, and book the repair. If a badge says do-not-drive or park-outside, follow it before the appointment, not after. Keep in mind the results are model-level campaigns - whether a specific car already had its repair completed is confirmed by the dealer or the nhtsa.gov VIN search.
