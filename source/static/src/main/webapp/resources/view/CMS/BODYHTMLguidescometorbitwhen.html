Open the Comet Orbit 3D Explorer when you need a Halley-like eccentric ellipse with anti-sunward ion and dust tails - scrub perihelion to aphelion, toggle each tail on its own, and read P about 76 yr, q 0.586 AU, Q 35.1 AU, and e 0.967 in the panel. Everything renders on your own device: no install, no account.


The controls, in the order you will use them

The Comet Orbit 3D Explorer starts as soon as the page loads. Drag to orbit the Sun and the path, scroll to zoom, and press Play orbit. Look for the eccentric ellipse with the Sun near one focus, then drag Orbit progress toward perihelion so the tails lengthen - both cones stay roughly anti-sunward through the sweep. Click Ion tail and Dust tail to hide or show each stream on its own, and use fullscreen so the controls stay visible while you orbit the view.


A five-minute classroom demo

A five-minute classroom demo starts when you open Comet Orbit 3D Explorer, scrub near perihelion, and pause so students see ion (blue) and dust (yellow) tails stretch away from the Sun - faster than a flat textbook ellipse alone.


Halley numbers in one panel

Halley numbers in one panel stay visible while you read period about 76 yr, perihelion 0.586 AU, aphelion 35.1 AU, and eccentricity 0.967, plus an approximate heliocentric distance readout on the compressed orbit. Orbit distances are shortened for one-screen fit; the panel keeps the published figures.

Solar System 3D Explorer is heliocentric planets for our neighborhood; Asteroid Belt 3D Explorer covers the main belt, not ion and dust tails. A textbook can print one static ellipse - this page adds scrub, play, and independent ion/dust toggles in a browser-only 3D view.


Sessions Comet Orbit 3D Explorer does not fit

Sessions Comet Orbit 3D Explorer does not fit are planet-only tours, main-belt Kirkwood gaps, or Moon tidal locking. Use Solar System 3D Explorer, Asteroid Belt 3D Explorer, or Tidal Locking 3D Explorer instead. The comet page needs WebGL, is not an N-body or outgassing chemistry model, and will not give live ephemeris for Halley's next return.
