Frequently Asked Questions

How big was Tyrannotitan?

Published length estimates range from about 11.6 m to as much as 12.2 m in some studies, with weight estimates of about 6 to 7.4 tonnes - making it one of the largest theropods known. Turn on the human figure in the viewer to see the scale against a 1.8 m person.

Is Tyrannotitan related to Giganotosaurus?

Yes. Tyrannotitan belongs to the tribe Giganotosaurini within the carcharodontosaurid family, making it a close relative of the already-shipped Giganotosaurus and Mapusaurus 3D viewers. All three are giant Patagonian predators from a similar size class, but Tyrannotitan is the geologically oldest of the three by several million years.

Why is so little known about its skeleton?

The holotype (specimen MPEF-PV 1156) includes partial jaws and teeth, a run of vertebrae, ribs, a shoulder fragment, a humerus and ulna, part of the hip, and a nearly complete femur and fibula - but no complete skull or full skeleton has ever been found. Size and shape estimates are scaled from those bones and from more completely known relatives.

When and where did Tyrannotitan live?

In the Early Cretaceous, usually cited as the Albian stage (roughly 113 to 100.5 million years ago), though some sources place it slightly earlier in the Aptian. Its fossils come from the Cerro Castano Member of the Cerro Barcino Formation near La Juanita Farm, 28 km northeast of Paso de Indios, Chubut Province, Argentina. Fernando Novas, Silvina de Valais, Patricia Vickers-Rich and Thomas Rich formally described and named the species in 2005.

Is the model scientifically accurate?

The proportions follow published figures, but the skin color and texture are an artistic reconstruction - fossils preserve bone, not soft tissue or color. This model is not a fossil-accurate skeleton. The length, weight, and age figures shown are real published values, with ranges cited because sources disagree and the animal is known from partial remains.

Do I need to install anything to view it?

No. The model renders in your browser with WebGL - no app, no account, and nothing about your visit is sent to a server. The 3D engine loads once and is then cached.
