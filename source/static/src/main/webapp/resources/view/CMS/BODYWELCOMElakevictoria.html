Lake Victoria 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-29

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Lake Victoria in cross-section: a vast, gently sloping saucer of a basin where the floor barely drops - about 84 m at its deepest point, against a mean depth of about 40 m across the whole 68,800 sq km surface. That flatness is the story: unlike a rift-valley lake with a deep narrow trench, Victoria is wide and shallow from shore to shore.

The lake sits at about 1,133 m above sea level and is shared by Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda. Its only major outflow is the White Nile, which leaves the lake at Jinja, Uganda, near the site of the historic Ripon Falls (now the Nalubaale Dam) - the traditionally cited source of the Nile. Geologically the lake is young, roughly 400,000 years old, and it has dried up completely at least once in the past - around 17,300 years ago - before refilling to its present size. Its shallow, warm waters also support several hundred endemic cichlid fish species, among the richest concentrations of fish diversity anywhere in the world.

For a lake with the opposite profile - narrow, ancient, and extremely deep - open the Lake Baikal 3D Explorer, or compare a sea-scale basin in the Caspian Sea 3D Explorer.

The Lake Victoria basin - its saucer-flat floor, depth bands, and mean-depth reference plane spanning the Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda shoreline - is drawn right in your browser with WebGL, so orbiting, zooming, and toggling the East African lake model all stay on your own device. The three.js engine that renders the scene loads just once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached for instant reloads.

This is an educational approximation - the basin profile, shelf boundary, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey data. The Shoreline view is different: it streams real measured elevation (Mapzen/AWS Open Data terrain tiles, derived from NASA/USGS DEMs) and real satellite imagery (Copernicus Sentinel-2) of the northern shore and White Nile outlet near Jinja, so that part of the scene is genuine surveyed terrain, not a teaching approximation - Terrarium elevation data records the lake's surface, not its underwater depth, which is why the depth-band cross-section stays in the schematic basin view above.
