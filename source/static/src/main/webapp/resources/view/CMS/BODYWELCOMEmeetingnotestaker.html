Meeting Notes Taker

Last reviewed: 2026-08-29

Press record, hold your meeting, press stop: the recording is transcribed with a speech model that runs inside this browser tab, and the transcript is turned into draft minutes you can edit, copy or download.

Typical use: a standup, a client call or an interview you want written up without handing the audio to a note-taking service.

What the minutes contain

The notes are assembled from the transcript in six sections. Summary picks the sentences that carry the most repeated content words, in the order they were said. Decisions collects lines with decision language ("we agreed", "approved", "we are going with"). Action items collects lines with commitment language ("I will send", "let us schedule", "needs to") and, when the sentence names one, the deadline it mentions. Risks and blockers, open questions and figures mentioned follow the same idea for problems, questions and any money, percentage or count that came up. Every line keeps its timecode, so you can jump back to the moment in the recording and check it.

This is deliberately a text-analysis pass, not a model writing prose: it can only surface sentences that were actually spoken, so it cannot invent a decision nobody made. On a computer with WebGPU there is an optional button that asks a small language model, also running in this tab, to rewrite the same notes as prose - it appears next to the extracted version rather than replacing it, because a small model can still drift.

Three ways to get the audio in

Choose one at the top of the tool. Microphone only is the simplest and works in every browser that can record. Microphone plus the audio of a tab or window is the option for online calls: Chrome and Edge on a computer let you share a tab and tick "share tab audio", and the tool mixes that with your microphone so both sides of the conversation are transcribed. Upload a recording takes an audio or video file you already have - a Zoom recording, a voice memo, an MP3 - reads it in the tab and transcribes it the same way. Recording other people is your responsibility; ask for consent before you start.

What the first run costs

The speech model is OpenAI Whisper base, about 78 MB in its quantised ONNX form. The tool shows the real download percentage, then the browser keeps the files, so later meetings start with no wait and transcription still works offline. Speed depends on the machine: with WebGPU a recording transcribes several times faster than real time, while on the CPU it takes roughly as long as the meeting itself - a 30 minute call is a 30 minute wait, and the status line says which mode is in use so the number is never a surprise. Long meetings are processed in 30 second chunks, so memory stays flat rather than growing with the recording.

Where this sits next to the other tools

The speech to text tool is for live dictation: it types what you say into a box as you speak, using the recognition service built into your browser. This page is for the opposite shape of work - a whole meeting, captured first and processed afterwards, entirely on your device, ending in a document rather than a text box. If you need the reverse direction, text to speech reads written text aloud with neural voices.

Privacy, licences and credit

No audio, transcript or note is uploaded: the model files come down to your browser and the processing happens locally, which is also why the tool keeps working with the network disconnected. The speech model is Apache-2.0 licensed and served from our own asset server at a pinned version, checksummed when it is published. The idea comes from meetily, an MIT-licensed desktop meeting assistant; that project is a Rust and Tauri application with no browser build, so this page is an independent implementation of the same job for the browser rather than a port of its code.
