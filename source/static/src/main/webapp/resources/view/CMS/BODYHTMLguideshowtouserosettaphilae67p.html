How to Use the Rosetta + Philae 67P 3D Explorer - Step by Step

Last reviewed: 2026-08-11

Open the Rosetta + Philae 67P 3D Explorer, press Play or drag the mission-progress slider, then read the facts panel to see the real published date and figures at each mission milestone.


Press Play or drag the progress slider

Press #t3dRpPlayBtn to auto-advance Rosetta and Philae along the real 2004-2016 mission timeline, or drag #t3dRpProgress (0 to 100) to jump straight to any point. Dragging the slider pauses auto-play.


Read the milestone table

The facts panel lists all 7 real mission milestones - launch, orbit insertion, Philae's separation and first touchdown, its final rest site, perihelion, and mission end - with the currently active one highlighted. Scrub the slider and watch the highlighted row change.


Watch Philae separate, descend, and bounce

Between about 40% and 55% progress, the Philae lander visibly detaches from Rosetta, descends to the comet's small lobe, and bounces along an arc from the first touchdown site to its final resting site - the real story of the harpoons failing to fire on November 12, 2014.


Watch for the perihelion jets

Scrub to around 70-82% progress to reach the comet's closest approach to the Sun on August 13, 2015. Particle jets appear near the nucleus, and a note explains the real increase in outgassing activity at that point in the orbit.


Click the comet, Rosetta, or Philae

Click any of the three objects in the scene to refresh the facts panel with the current milestone without moving the slider.


Drag to orbit and use fullscreen

Drag to orbit the camera, scroll or pinch to zoom, and use the Fullscreen button to expand the whole wrapper for a clearer view of the two-lobed nucleus and the landing sites.
