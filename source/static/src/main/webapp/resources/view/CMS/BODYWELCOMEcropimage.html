This image cropper and rotator trims a JPG, PNG, or WebP to the exact frame you need: drag the crop box freehand, lock it to a preset such as 1:1 for a LinkedIn profile photo or a passport-style headshot, and straighten a tilted shot before you download. Every step happens in this browser tab.

  1. Upload a JPG, PNG, or WebP image.
  2. Drag the crop box to your target frame, or pick an aspect-ratio preset; rotate with the controls if needed.
  3. Download the cropped image.

Accepted formats and what comes back out

The crop output preserves the source format: JPG comes out as JPG, PNG as PNG (transparency intact), WebP as WebP - no re-encoding, no quality re-compression. One exception: the upload box also takes an .svg file, but the crop step draws the image onto a canvas before it can be trimmed, so an SVG comes out as a JPG, PNG, or WebP raster and never back out as an editable vector. Crop a logo or icon in a vector editor instead when it has to stay scalable. If the next step is a format switch, that conversion is a separate trip to image converter tools. Choosing between the two for the crop itself? Photographic crops save more bytes as JPG, while flat colour, sharp edges, or transparency keep their fidelity as PNG - JPG vs PNG for the web has the byte counts, and when WebP beats both.


Framing an exact pixel size

Five aspect-ratio presets (16:9, 4:3, 1:1, 2:3, Free) cover the common social and print sizes without manual pixel math. The Free preset pairs with four side inputs - X, Y, Width, and Height - that take the frame directly in pixels, so a 1920x1080 source can be reframed to an exact 1080x1080 square, or any other custom rectangle, without dragging the box by eye - the exact footprint a landing page or marketplace listing demands. The original image stays in the workspace, so a second crop at a different ratio takes one click instead of a re-upload. For the opposite case - keeping the full frame and scaling the entire image proportionally - the dedicated tool is resize image online free; this page handles the framing-and-cut decision only. Zoom, pan, and rotate controls

The zoom controls magnify the working canvas so the crop frame can land on a single eye, a label, or a corner. Switch the drag-mode toggle from Crop to Move to pan the magnified region, then flip back to Crop to draw the frame. A live preview pane shows the result at the natural output size while the frame is being dragged - no need to commit and re-crop. For straightening, the Rotate Left and Rotate Right buttons turn the image in 45-degree steps from the toolbar, and the rotation handle takes a free angle instead; either way the crop frame stays anchored to the rotated content, so the final cut tracks the new orientation.


Every crop stays in your browser

Every crop runs inside the browser via HTML canvas - the picture is never uploaded to a server, never persists past the page reload, and closing the tab drops the working copy. Useful when the source contains a face, a personal document, or other sensitive content. No account to create, no watermark, and no daily cap.

Crop a JFIF image, not just JPG or PNG

If your picture arrived as a .jfif file - the extension Windows and some email clients attach to ordinary JPEG photos - there is no need to convert it first. The cropper accepts .jfif alongside .jpg, .jpeg, .png, and .webp, and opens it straight in the workspace.
