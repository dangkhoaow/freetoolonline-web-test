Frequently Asked Questions

How long is a galactic year?

Roughly 225-250 million years, the time the Sun takes for one full orbit of the Milky Way's center. This scene uses 230 million years, the figure most commonly cited.

How many times has the Sun orbited the galaxy?

About 20 times. The Solar System formed roughly 4.6 billion years ago, and dividing that by a 230-million-year galactic year gives about 20 completed laps.

How far is the Sun from the center of the Milky Way?

About 26,000-28,000 light-years, roughly 8 kiloparsecs, orbiting at a speed commonly cited around 220-230 km/s (Gaia-era measurements run slightly higher, about 234-240 km/s).

Does the Sun really bob up and down through the galaxy?

Published studies agree the Sun oscillates vertically through the galactic disk plane, but they disagree on the exact cycle length - estimates range from about 30 to 74 million years depending on the study. This page discloses that range rather than picking one number as settled fact.

Is this different from the Milky Way Map explorer?

Yes. The Milky Way Map 3D Explorer shows the galaxy's overall structure. This page is specifically about the Sun's own motion - its orbit path, speed, and vertical bobbing - as a passenger inside that structure.

Does this page simulate real Milky Way gravity?

No. It is an educational approximation using published distance, speed, and period figures. The spiral-arm disk is a teaching shape, not a gravitational simulation, and on-screen sizes are compressed and exaggerated for visibility.
