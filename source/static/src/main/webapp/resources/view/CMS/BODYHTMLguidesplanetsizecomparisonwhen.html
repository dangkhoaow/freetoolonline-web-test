Planet Size Comparison 3D Explorer - When to Use It, Step by Step, and How It Compares

Last reviewed: 2026-08-11
freetoolonline.com editorial team

The Planet Size Comparison 3D Explorer draws the Sun and all eight planets as spheres sized from published NASA mean radii, from the Sun at 696,340 km down to Mercury at 2,440 km. It runs in this browser with no install and no account: drag to orbit the lineup, scroll or pinch to zoom, and click a world button to highlight one and read its radius.


How to compare two planets step by step

Three actions cover a full comparison in the Planet Size Comparison 3D Explorer. First, wait for the status line under the canvas to read that you can drag to orbit and scroll to zoom; the facts panel opens with Earth already highlighted and a table of all nine radii. Second, click Jupiter to highlight its sphere at 69,911 km, then Earth at 6,371 km, and watch the panel heading switch to the world you picked while the camera nudges closer to it. Third, press Pause spin to freeze the lineup for a screenshot, or Play spin to rotate it slowly. Fullscreen keeps the world buttons and the panel in view while you orbit and zoom.

Each sphere radius stays proportional to the number in that row, so the size jump from Earth to Jupiter is the real ratio rather than a drawing. The scene uses Jupiter as its reference scale, which keeps the inner planets large enough to see beside the Sun.


A five-minute classroom demo

A five-minute demo is the session the Planet Size Comparison 3D Explorer fits best: open the page, highlight Jupiter at 69,911 km, then Earth at 6,371 km, and let the class watch the sphere size jump. Pause spin gives a still frame to talk over, and the panel table answers the follow-up question about the Sun at 696,340 km against Mercury at 2,440 km without leaving the scene. Nothing is installed and no sign-in interrupts the lesson, so a shared classroom machine works the same as a laptop.

Sessions it does not fit

Some sessions need a different scene. The Planet Size Comparison 3D Explorer does not show orbital motion, does not place the worlds at real distances from the Sun, and does not compare mass, so heliocentric distance work and mass questions belong elsewhere. For orbiting planets with compressed sphere sizes, use the Solar System 3D Explorer. For Sun, Earth and Moon phase geometry, use the Moon Phases 3D Explorer. The scene also needs WebGL support in the browser to render at all.


Frequently asked questions

Do I need to install anything to use the 3D explorer?

No. Everything renders in your browser on your own device. There is no install and no account, and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

How do I compare two planets side by side?

Click any world button to highlight it and read its NASA mean radius in the facts panel, then drag to orbit and scroll or pinch to zoom until both worlds sit in view at the same time.

Are the gaps between the spheres real distances?

No. Only the radii are to scale. The spacing between worlds is chosen so the whole lineup stays readable on one screen, so treat the scene as a size comparison rather than a map of the Solar System.
