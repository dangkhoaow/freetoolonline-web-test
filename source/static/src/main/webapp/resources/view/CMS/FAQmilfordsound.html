Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Milford Sound so deep?

Glaciers moved more slowly and powerfully than rivers, eroding deeper into the bedrock. The fjord's depth of about 291 meters reflects the immense erosive force that carved it over thousands of years.

Why are there so many waterfalls?

Milford Sound receives about 200 rainy days per year - among the wettest places on Earth. Steep cliffs and high rainfall combine to create waterfalls that flow year-round, especially dramatic after heavy storms.

What is Mitre Peak?

Mitre Peak is the iconic 1,695-meter mountain rising near Milford Sound's entrance. Its sharp, pointed shape (like a bishop's mitre) makes it one of New Zealand's most recognizable landmarks.

How is a fjord different from a regular valley?

Fjords are valleys carved by glaciers and then flooded by the sea. They have steep, nearly vertical walls and are much deeper than river valleys. River valleys form V-shaped canyons; glacier valleys form U-shaped fjords.

Can you visit Milford Sound?

Yes. Cruise boats depart from Milford Sound's settlement daily. The main attractions are scenic cruises on the fjord, hiking trails along the shore, and rainforest walks. It's one of New Zealand's most visited natural sites.
