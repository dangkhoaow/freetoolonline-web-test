Crop a Video in Your Browser - Free Crop Box, Square, 16:9 and 9:16

Last reviewed: 2026-10-15
freetoolonline.com editorial team

To crop a video, open it in Video Cropper, pick a shape or drag the crop box over the part you want, then press "Crop video". Every frame of the whole video is cut to that one rectangle, and you download a single MP4 or WebM file, with the sound kept unless you remove it.


The crop box and its shapes

The picture outside the crop box is dimmed, so you see what stays. Drag inside the box to move it, or drag one of its four corners or four edges to resize it; a rule-of-thirds grid appears while you drag. "Free" lets the box take any shape. With a fixed shape, a corner keeps the shape from the opposite corner and an edge keeps it around the box's centre line. Choosing a shape fits the largest box of that shape around the current centre. The box cannot leave the picture, and arrow keys move it once it has focus (Shift moves it ten times as far). For exact framing, type "Width", "Height", "Left" and "Top" in pixels of the picture as shown.

"Original" keeps the picture's own shape, so you can zoom into part of the frame without changing its proportions. The crop is one fixed rectangle for the whole video - it does not follow a moving subject - so move the slider under the picture to check a few moments before you crop.

Rotate, flip and fix a sideways phone video

"Rotate left" and "Rotate right" turn the picture 90 degrees, and the crop box turns with it, so the same part of the scene stays selected; a fixed shape turns too, so 16:9 becomes 9:16. "Flip" mirrors the picture left to right, which helps with front-camera recordings. "Reset" clears the crop, the rotation and the flip. A phone video that plays sideways in other apps usually only needs one "Rotate" and no crop at all.


Format, size and sound

The line above "Crop video" names the result before you start, for example "Output: MP4 (H.264 + AAC), 1080x1080, sound copied unchanged". "Format" picks the file type: "Auto (best for this browser)" makes MP4 (H.264) when the browser can encode it, otherwise WebM, and you can instead choose "MP4 (H.264)", "WebM (VP9)" or "WebM (AV1)". If the browser cannot encode the chosen format at that size, the same line says so before you press anything.

"Size" leaves the crop at its own resolution ("Same as the crop") or shrinks it so its short side is at most 1080, 720 or 480 pixels - it never enlarges a small crop. "Sound" keeps or removes the audio. Kept sound is copied unchanged when the file type can carry it - AAC or MP3 into MP4, Opus or Vorbis into WebM - and is otherwise re-encoded to AAC or Opus.


How it works in your browser

Nothing is uploaded: the browser reads the video from your device, decodes and crops each frame, and builds the new file in the tab's memory. Most files go through the browser's own video encoder (WebCodecs). A file the browser cannot decode itself - HEVC in Firefox, for example - goes through compatibility mode instead, a WebAssembly copy of FFmpeg that downloads once (about 31 MB), runs more slowly and always makes MP4 (H.264) of at most 1080p. When the browser cannot play a file at all, the preview shows still frames that follow the slider, and cropping works the same way.

Crop, trim, merge or edit

Video Cropper changes the frame of one video; it does not shorten it or join files. The video merger trims, rotates and crops each clip when you join two or more, the video trimmer keeps a single time range, the video compressor makes a file smaller, and the multi-track video editor adds text, captions and effects.
