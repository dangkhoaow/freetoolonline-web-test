Cloudy Aquarium Water: Photo Comparison vs Test Kit vs AI Apps

Last reviewed: 2026-09-05

Cloudy water has several very different causes, and each checking method answers a different question. Here is where Aquarium Water Checker fits next to a liquid test kit and the mobile AI aquarium apps.


Photo comparison - the trend question

Comparing two photos of the same tank answers "is it getting better or worse, and what does the cloudiness look like?" A smooth, even milkiness with little grain reads as a bacterial bloom; sharp high-frequency speckle reads as suspended dust or silt; a green shift points to algae; a warm transparent amber usually means tannins. The checker names the pattern, scores clarity 0-100 for each photo, and runs entirely in your browser with no account and no upload - which none of the app-store options offer as a direct two-photo comparison.


Liquid test kit - the chemistry question

Ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, pH and hardness are invisible. Perfectly clear water can be toxic and a dramatic-looking bloom can be harmless, so the kit is the ground truth whenever fish behave abnormally. Use the photo check between tests, not instead of them.


Mobile AI aquarium apps - the identification question

Phone apps that scan a photo focus on species identification, disease guesses and care logs, usually behind a subscription and a cloud upload. This checker keeps a deliberately narrower promise: measured clarity deltas between two states of the same tank, on your device. Its optional on-device AI pass can also suggest which fish it sees, but the suggestion only prefills a form you confirm.


Quick chooser

Water looks different than yesterday: photo comparison. Fish acting wrong: test kit, immediately. Want a species name or disease guess: a dedicated app or a fishkeeping community. Want a weekly clarity log for free without new hardware: photo comparison again.
