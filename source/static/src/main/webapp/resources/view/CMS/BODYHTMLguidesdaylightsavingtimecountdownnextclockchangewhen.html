When Do the Clocks Change? Using a Daylight Saving Countdown

Last reviewed: 2026-08-24

The fastest way to answer "when does the time change?" is the Daylight Saving Time Countdown: it auto-detects your timezone and immediately states the exact local date and minute of the next clock change, whether clocks go forward or back, and how long remains - no date entry, no lookup table.


When it fits

Use it when you need the precise moment for one place - your own zone or any IANA timezone you type in. It is the right tool before scheduling calls across regions around a change weekend: the built-in table compares the next change in New York, Los Angeles, London, Berlin, Sydney and Auckland side by side, and the northern and southern hemispheres change in opposite directions weeks apart.


Forward or back - what it means

The result spells out the direction and the cost: clocks going forward means you lose that hour of clock time (a 09:00 meeting arrives an hour sooner of real time); clocks going back means you gain it. The UTC offset before and after is shown, which is what actually matters when coordinating with a zone that does not change - Vietnam, India, Japan and most of Asia observe no daylight saving, and the page says so explicitly with their year-round offset.


What to expect

Everything runs locally in the browser from its bundled timezone database - no upload and no account. The countdown ticks every second while the tab is open; it does not notify in the background. And because it reads the rules browsers ship with, a freshly announced law change can lag until browsers update their database.
