K2 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-28

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows K2 as a single, near-symmetric steep pyramid rather than a broad ridged massif. The summit sits at 8,611 m in the Karakoram range, on the border between China and Pakistan, rising directly from the flat ice apron of the Godwin-Austen and Baltoro Glaciers. K2 is Earth's second-highest peak after Mount Everest, but its abrupt rise of about 3,500 m above its own glacier base in a short horizontal distance makes it steeper and more technically demanding to climb than the taller Everest - part of why it earned the nickname the "Savage Mountain".

The Abruzzi Spur is the ridge on K2's southeast side that carried the first successful summit team in 1954, and it remains the most-climbed route today. Toggle the route marker to trace it up the mountain, with a small camp marker partway up. Near the top, a pale overhanging shape marks the Bottleneck: a narrow couloir at about 8,200 m that sits directly beneath a huge hanging ice serac. Toggle the serac overhang to see why this single section is considered one of the most dangerous stretches on any of the world's 8,000 m peaks - it was the site of the 2008 K2 disaster, in which 11 climbers died in one incident.

  • Toggle the Abruzzi Spur route marker to trace the first-ascent line up the southeast side
  • Toggle the Bottleneck serac overhang to see the hanging ice shelf near the summit

For a different single free-standing peak, compare the four-ridged glacial horn at the Matterhorn 3D Explorer, or the taller but broader three-summit massif at the Mount Everest 3D Explorer.

K2's pyramid is drawn on your own device by the vendored three.js engine, which downloads once (about 0.7 MB) and stays cached, so returning to orbit the peak again loads instantly. Your route-marker and serac-overhang choices are saved between visits, so the explorer reopens the way you last left it.

This is an educational approximation - the pyramid shape, route line, and serac position are schematic teaching cues, not survey terrain.
