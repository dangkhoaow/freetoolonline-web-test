How to Check a US Product Recall - Step by Step

Last reviewed: 2026-09-13

Open the product recall lookup, type a product type or brand, press Search recalls, and read the matching US CPSC recall cards - hazard, remedy, models, and where it was sold, newest first. No account, nothing installed. This page walks through the four steps and what each result field means.


Step 1 - open the tool

Go to the product recall lookup. Before you type anything, the page already lists the recalls announced in the last 60 days, newest first, so you can see the shape of a real result card.


Step 2 - type a product, brand, or manufacturer

Short, generic keywords work best: "stroller", "space heater", "crib". The search checks recall titles first and automatically retries by manufacturer name when the title search finds nothing, so a firm name works too. The optional hazard box narrows results to a hazard keyword like "choking" or "fire" - useful for questions like "recalled heaters with a fire hazard".


Step 3 - read the recall cards

Each card shows the official recall title (linked to the cpsc.gov notice), the announcement date and recall number, the manufacturer and country of manufacture, remedy badges - refund, repair, or replace - the affected product models and unit counts, the hazard, reported incidents, what to do, and where the product was sold. Long result sets stay readable: the page shows the newest 25 with a count line naming how many matched in total.


Step 4 - act on a match

If a product you own matches, stop using it immediately - especially anything for babies or children - then open the official notice from the card's title link and compare the model number and date codes. The remedy (refund, repair, or replacement) is provided free of charge by the firm; the notice has the contact details. A no-match result is not a safety certificate - it only means no CPSC record matched that query, so try a broader keyword or check cpsc.gov/Recalls for very recent announcements.

Your keywords go only to the official US-government CPSC API at saferproducts.gov, never to this site's servers. For the moments this check pays off, see the real use cases; to pick the right source for a breaking recall, see the comparison guide.
