Mammoth Cave in Kentucky, USA is the longest known cave system on Earth - more than 400 miles (about 650 km) of surveyed limestone passages stacked in at least 5 levels, with mineral formations such as stalactites and stalagmites decorating its chambers and the Echo River flowing through the lower levels. This scene has two views: the karst plateau surface above the cave, built from real measured elevation and satellite imagery, and the cave interior below it, a schematic teaching model (the passages themselves are not laser-scanned here). Use the View button to switch between them, and the level colors / Echo River highlight buttons to tell the stacked interior passage levels apart.

About this scene

Published figures: Mammoth Cave National Park, Kentucky, USA; more than 400 miles (about 650 km) of surveyed passages - the longest known cave system on Earth; at least 5 mapped levels linked by vertical shafts; a sandstone caprock over the limestone has kept passages open for millions of years; the Echo River and Roaring River run through the lower levels; US National Park since 1941, UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1981, International Biosphere Reserve since 1990; home to more than 130 species, including the endangered Kentucky cave shrimp.
