Triton Cantaloupe Terrain 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-19

Triton is the largest moon of Neptune - about 2,700 kilometers across in NASA summaries, with Voyager 2 refining the diameter near 2,706 kilometers. It is the only large Solar System moon on a retrograde orbit. Voyager 2 flew past on 25 August 1989 and returned the first close views of a landscape nicknamed cantaloupe terrain.

That terrain fills much of Triton's western hemisphere: closely spaced, nearly circular dimples (cavi) about 30 to 40 kilometers across, with crosscutting ridges. It is not known elsewhere. Leading ideas include diapirism - buoyant icy material rising through denser layers - or stresses from global cooling after a major thermal pulse. This explorer shows a schematic dimple grid and ridge X without picking a single origin story, and it does not model Triton's nitrogen geysers.

  • Smooth Triton sphere with schematic cantaloupe cavi
  • Toggleable dimple grid plus a simple sulci ridge X
  • Neptune in the background for orbital context (not to scale)

Anyone comparing outer-planet moons gets a concrete sense of why Voyager called this landscape unique.
