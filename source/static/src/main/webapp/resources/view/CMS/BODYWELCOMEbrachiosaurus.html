Brachiosaurus lived in the Late Jurassic, about 154 to 153 million years ago, in what is now Colorado and the wider Morrison Formation of western North America. The Natural History Museum cites a length of about 22 m and a mass of about 46.9 tonnes for the type specimen; a wider cross-source range runs about 18-22 m long and about 28-47 tonnes, so figures vary by specimen and study. Unlike most sauropods, its front legs were longer than its hind legs, giving it a sloped, giraffe-like posture and letting it browse leaves up to about 9 m above the ground.

The first Brachiosaurus remains were dug up near Fruita, Colorado in 1900, and the paleontologist Elmer S. Riggs named the genus in 1903. No complete skeleton has ever been found, so most full-body reconstructions - including many museum mounts - borrow proportions from the closely related African sauropod Giraffatitan, which was once classified as a species of Brachiosaurus before being recognized as a separate genus.
