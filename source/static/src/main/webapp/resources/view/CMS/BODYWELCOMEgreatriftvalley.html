This browser explorer shows the Great Rift Valley in cross-section: a flat trench floor between two parallel escarpment walls, dotted with volcanic peaks and rift lakes along its length. Toggle the escarpment shading to see the fault walls stand out, or toggle the lake and volcano markers to spot the named features.

The Great Rift Valley is a system of tectonic trenches running about 6,000 km from Lebanon and the Jordan Rift south through East Africa to Mozambique, formed as the African and Somali plates pull apart. In East Africa the valley is typically 30-100 km wide, with escarpment walls rising roughly 600-2,000 m above the floor. It is dotted with volcanoes - including Mount Kilimanjaro and Mount Kenya, East Africa's two tallest peaks - and holds a chain of rift lakes such as Lake Malawi, Lake Tanganyika, and Lake Turkana. It is sometimes called the "Cradle of Humankind" because major hominid fossil sites, including Olduvai Gorge, lie within it.

The whole Great Rift Valley scene - the flat trench floor, the two escarpment walls, and the volcanic peaks and rift lakes scattered along it - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to see how the valley sits between its two fault walls, and zoom in to compare the floor against the surrounding plateau. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the valley segment, escarpment profile, and peak/lake positions are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
