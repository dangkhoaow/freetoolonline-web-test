Niagara Falls is really two principal falls sharing one crestline: the curved Horseshoe Falls on the Canadian side and the straight American Falls on the US side, split by Goat Island. About 2,832 cubic meters of water pour over the crestline every second - and about 90% of it goes over Horseshoe Falls. Toggle the flow-split colors to see that split, and the whirlpool marker to find the sharp bend downstream where the river doubles back on itself.

About this scene

Published figures: combined average flow about 2,832 cubic meters per second (about 168,000 cubic meters per minute); Horseshoe Falls carries about 90% of that flow, American Falls about 10%; Horseshoe Falls height about 57 m (188 ft), crestline about 670 m (2,200 ft); American Falls height about 21-34 m (70-110 ft, varies with the talus rock pile at its base), width about 320 m (1,060 ft); Niagara Gorge runs about 11 km (7 mi) downstream to the Niagara Whirlpool, a sharp bend in the river about 4.5 km (2.8 mi) below the falls.
