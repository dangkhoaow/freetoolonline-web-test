Frequently Asked Questions

How deep is Lake Baikal?

Baikal reaches about 1,642 m at its deepest point in the central basin - the deepest lake on Earth, more than twice as deep as the next-deepest lake. Average depth across the whole lake is about 744 m.

Why does Lake Baikal have three basins?

The lake sits in an active continental rift valley that is still slowly widening, and separate down-faulted sections formed a shallower north basin next to deeper central and south basins along the same rift.

Is Lake Baikal really the oldest lake in the world?

Geologists estimate Baikal is 25 to 30 million years old, based on its rift-formation history - most lakes are geologically young by comparison, often existing for only thousands of years.

Does Lake Baikal freeze in winter?

Yes - it typically freezes over from January into May or June most years, and is well known for exceptionally clear, transparent ice several tens of centimetres thick in places.

How much of the world's fresh water is in Lake Baikal?

Baikal holds about 23,615 cu km of water, which is roughly 23% of the world's unfrozen fresh surface water - more than all five of North America's Great Lakes combined.
