Frequently Asked Questions

How big is Vatnajokull?

About 7,700 sq km, roughly 8% of Iceland's land area - the largest ice cap in Europe by volume, with ice up to about 950 m thick in places.

Are there really volcanoes under the ice?

Yes. Grimsvotn, Iceland's most frequently erupting volcano, and Bardarbunga, which fed the 2014-2015 Holuhraun fissure eruption, both sit hidden beneath hundreds of metres of ice.

What is the Jokulsarlon lagoon?

A glacial lagoon at the ice cap's southern edge that has grown since the 1930s-40s as the Breidamerkurjokull outlet glacier retreated. Calved icebergs drift in the lagoon before washing out toward the nearby Diamond Beach.

How many outlet glaciers does Vatnajokull have?

About 30 named outlet glaciers flow outward from the main ice dome down toward the surrounding lowlands.

Is Vatnajokull protected?

Yes. Vatnajokull National Park has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2019.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the ice-dome relief, subglacial terrain, and lagoon geometry are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
