Frequently Asked Questions

When should I plan to climb Mount Fuji?

The official climbing season runs from early July through early September. July and August see the most climbers and most stable weather, though it is also the most crowded period. September offers fewer visitors but more unpredictable weather. Outside this window, the mountain is covered in deep snow and climbing is extremely dangerous.

How long does it take to climb to the summit?

Most climbers spend 2-3 days on the mountain, with one or two nights in mountain huts. The ascent typically takes 5-8 hours depending on the route chosen (Yoshida shortest, Gotemba longest). Descent is faster, usually 2-3 hours. Acclimatization to the high altitude (thin air above 2,000 m) is essential.

Is it safe for inexperienced hikers to climb Mount Fuji?

Yes, with proper preparation. Mount Fuji is considered an achievable goal for people of moderate fitness. However, altitude sickness affects many climbers. Recommendations: arrive at least one day early for acclimatization, climb slowly, stay hydrated, and use the mountain huts. Medical support is available at the huts. Avoid climbing alone and always inform others of your plans.

Why is Mount Fuji considered sacred in Japanese culture?

Mount Fuji is venerated in both Shintoism and Buddhism. It has inspired centuries of art, poetry, and pilgrimage. The mountain features prominently in Japan's national identity and was designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013. Climbers often refer to a "spiritual" dimension to the climb, reflecting centuries of cultural reverence for this iconic peak.

How often does Mount Fuji erupt?

Mount Fuji has erupted approximately 16 times in recorded history. The most recent eruption was the Hoei eruption of 1707-1708, which lasted about three weeks. Since then, the mountain has been quiet for over 300 years. While volcanic activity cannot be completely ruled out, current monitoring shows no signs of imminent eruption. Scientists consider the probability of an eruption in the immediate future to be low.
