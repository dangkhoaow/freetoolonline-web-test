Frequently Asked Questions

How tall is Mount Fuji compared to other mountains?

At 3,776.24 meters, Mount Fuji ranks as Japan's highest peak, but it is much lower than other major volcanoes and mountains worldwide. Mount Everest stands at 8,848.86 meters, while Kilimanjaro reaches 5,895 meters. However, Mount Fuji's isolated cone shape and prominence make it visually striking despite its lower absolute elevation.

Can you climb Mount Fuji year-round?

The official climbing season runs from July through September, when mountain huts are staffed and weather conditions are most favorable. Winter climbing is technically possible but extremely dangerous due to severe weather, heavy snow, and avalanche risk. Most climbers follow the designated season.

Why does Mount Fuji have a perfect cone shape?

Mount Fuji's symmetrical shape results from its eruption history. The consistent composition and behavior of its lava flows, combined with the angle of repose of volcanic material, naturally formed the steep, regular cone profile. This is characteristic of stratovolcanoes composed of alternating layers of lava, ash, and pumice.

When did Mount Fuji last erupt?

Mount Fuji's last eruption occurred in 1707 - 1708, lasting about 49 days. Since then, it has been dormant but remains classified as an active volcano. Historically, it erupted roughly once every 100 - 150 years, with the previous major eruption in 1511.

What makes Mount Fuji culturally important?

Mount Fuji is central to Japanese spiritual and artistic traditions. In Shinto, it is considered a sacred mountain. It has inspired countless artworks, most famously in Hokusai's "Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji." The mountain symbolizes Japan's natural beauty and cultural identity both domestically and internationally.
