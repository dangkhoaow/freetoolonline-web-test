Frequently Asked Questions

Is Mount Etna the tallest volcano in Europe?

Yes. At about 3,357 m, Mount Etna is the tallest active volcano on the European continent. Its height shifts slightly after major eruptions build up or collapse parts of the summit craters, so published figures vary by a few tens of meters from year to year.

Why does Etna have four summit craters?

Etna's summit area holds four named active vents - Voragine, Bocca Nuova, Northeast Crater, and Southeast Crater - that have formed and merged over the volcano's long eruptive history. Unlike a single-vent volcano such as Vesuvius, Etna's broad, gently sloped profile has room for multiple craters to develop side by side near the top.

How often does Mount Etna erupt?

Mount Etna has been continuously active for at least 2,700 years, making it one of the most frequently erupting volcanoes in the world. Its eruptions range from small ash and gas emissions to lava fountains and flows that occasionally threaten nearby towns on its lower flanks.

Is Mount Etna a UNESCO World Heritage Site?

Yes. UNESCO added Mount Etna to its World Heritage list in 2013, citing its exceptional record of continuous volcanic activity and its value for the ongoing scientific study of volcanic processes.

How close is Mount Etna to Catania?

Mount Etna sits about 29 km north-northwest of Catania, a major Sicilian city. Its broad base and lava-flow fans stretch across the surrounding countryside, and historic flows have occasionally reached close to towns at the mountain's lower slopes.
