How to Check a US Food Recall - Step by Step

Last reviewed: 2026-08-23

The food recall lookup searches the official US FDA enforcement database in three steps: open the page, type a product or brand, and read the report cards. No account is needed, and the newest recalls are already on screen when the page loads.


Step 1 - open the lookup

Go to the food recall lookup. The page is useful the moment it loads: the 8 newest US food recall enforcement reports render automatically, newest first, so you can see what a report card looks like before typing anything. The classification table below the results explains what Class I, Class II, and Class III mean.


Step 2 - search a product or brand

Type a keyword such as blueberry or a company name into the box and press Search recalls or hit Enter. The search checks both the product description and the recalling firm name; a multi-word entry is matched as an exact phrase. The optional state dropdown narrows results to firms based in one US state - leave it on Any state unless you are researching a specific company. Up to 25 matching reports return, newest first.


Step 3 - read the report cards right

Each card shows the exact product description, the recalling firm with its city and state, the classification badge, the current status, the reason for the recall, the affected lot codes, the initiation date, and where the product was distributed. Class I matches - FDA's most serious category, meaning a reasonable probability of serious health consequences or death - are flagged with a red banner. Compare the lot codes printed on your package against the lot codes line: a recall usually covers specific lots, not every package.


Step 4 - when nothing matches

A no-match result states plainly that no enforcement report matches your query and links to fda.gov. That is not always all-clear: enforcement reports lag public announcements by days to weeks, USDA FSIS meat, poultry, and egg-product recalls live in a separate system at fsis.usda.gov, and only US-market recalls are covered. For a recall you just saw in the news, check fda.gov/safety/recalls directly.
