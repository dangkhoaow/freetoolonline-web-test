Apollo Free-Return Trajectory 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-21

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows how an Apollo-style free-return path uses the Moon's gravity to loop a spacecraft home without a lunar-orbit insertion burn - and how a hybrid path, like Apollo 13 after MCC-2, can miss Earth's atmosphere corridor unless another burn restores free-return.

Apollo 8, 10, and 11 flew free-return translunar trajectories so that, if the service propulsion system failed before lunar orbit, lunar gravity alone would still return the crew toward Earth. Later landing missions switched to hybrid trajectories that reached sites farther from the lunar equator, but those hybrids needed a burn to get home if the landing was aborted.

After the oxygen-tank explosion on Apollo 13, the spacecraft was on a hybrid path with an Earth perigee near 2,500 nmi - too high for atmosphere capture. The crew used the lunar module's descent engine for a 34.23-second burn at GET 61:29:43 to restore a free-return geometry, then flew past the Moon and home.

Depth: why the paths are drawn, not solved

When you drag the phase slider from 0 to 99 percent, the craft marker follows a fixed parametric curve - freePos(t) for the free-return path and hybridPos(t) for the hybrid miss - rather than a computed orbit, because this explorer plots pre-drawn teaching paths instead of integrating gravity or modeling engine thrust. That means the shape you scrub through is chosen to read clearly at a glance, and the Earth and Moon sizes plus the distance between them are deliberately compressed, so nothing on screen is at true astronomical scale. The real numbers live in the facts panel and the figures table instead: a restored free-return pericynthion near 250 km, a furthest-from-Earth mark of 400,171 km, and the 34.23-second descent-engine burn at ground elapsed time 61:29:43 that pulled Apollo 13 off its roughly 2,500-nautical-mile hybrid perigee. Switching the Free-return / Hybrid toggle does not recompute a trajectory; it swaps which stored path the marker rides and shows or hides the hybrid line, so the two geometries stay directly comparable side by side. Treat the page as an educational approximation - a labeled diagram you can rotate and step through - and read the published values from the table when you need the actual mission figures.

Depth: the approximation disclosure, saved state, and the two pericynthion figures

The word simulation is kept off this page on purpose, because a genuine free-return solve is a patched-conic or n-body calculation, whereas what you rotate here is a plotted teaching arc chosen for readability rather than solved from gravity. That honesty shows up in the figures table, which keeps the original mission-design pericynthion near 110 km lunar altitude separate from Apollo 13's restored value near 250 km - two distinct geometries that a single averaged number would quietly blur. Your last mode and phase are written to the browser storage key ftol-game-apollo-free-return-trajectory, so reopening the page returns you to where you stopped whenever the browser allows local storage. A fullscreen control expands the #t3dWrapper view for classroom projection, and drag, scroll, or pinch reframes the camera without altering any published figure. Where the companion Hohmann Transfer explorer walks delta-v budgets and transfer ellipses, this page stays on lunar-flyby return geometry alone, so the two answer different questions instead of restating the same one.
