Cron Expression Parser When To Use

Last reviewed: 2026-08-04
FreetoolOnline editorial team

Cron Expression Parser is worth opening when you need a quick, one-off run without installing anything. This page covers when that fits and when it does not.


When it fits

It fits when you want to confirm what a cron schedule actually means, or preview the next few run times, without installing a scheduler-syntax checker or spinning up a server.


What to expect

Expect a plain-English schedule description plus the next 5 run times, computed instantly in your browser. Nothing is uploaded or sent to a backend.

ExpressionMeaningRuns
0 9 * * 1-509:00 every weekday5 per week
*/15 * * * *Every 15 minutes, all day96 per day
0 0 1 * *Midnight on the 1st of the month12 per year

Frequently asked questions

What does */15 * * * * mean?

It runs every 15 minutes, all day, every day - 96 times in 24 hours. The parser's plain-English description confirms this without you doing the math.

Can I preview when a schedule will actually run?

Yes - paste the expression and the next 5 run times appear instantly, computed in your browser with nothing sent to a backend.
