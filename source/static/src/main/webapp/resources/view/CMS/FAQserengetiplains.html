Frequently Asked Questions

How big is the Serengeti?

Serengeti National Park covers about 14,750 sq km in northern Tanzania. The wider Serengeti ecosystem it belongs to spans roughly 30,000 sq km, extending into Kenya's Maasai Mara.

What is the Great Migration?

An annual movement of about 1.5 million wildebeest, along with hundreds of thousands of zebra and gazelle, following a large roughly circular route across the Serengeti-Mara ecosystem as they track seasonal rains and fresh grass.

What are the granite kopjes?

Ancient Precambrian rock outcrops that rise abruptly from the flat plains, among the oldest exposed rock in the region. They give rare shade and lookout points on the otherwise open grassland.

Why does the grass change color?

The Serengeti has a dry season and a wet season. Grass turns golden-brown in the dry season and green again once the rains return, which is what drives the wildebeest to keep moving in search of fresh grazing.

Is the Serengeti protected?

Yes. Serengeti National Park was established in 1951, and the wider area has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site (natural) since 1981.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the plain, kopjes, grass-color seasonality, and migration trail are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
