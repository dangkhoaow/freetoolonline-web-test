Against the x 2,000 shortcut

Doubling the hourly rate and adding three zeros assumes a 2,000-hour year - 40 hours for 50 weeks. At 52 paid weeks the true multiplier is 2,080: 20 per hour is 41,600 a year, not 40,000. The shortcut is fine for an order of magnitude; the calculator gives the exact figure plus the weekly, biweekly and monthly lines the shortcut cannot.


Against a spreadsheet

A spreadsheet reaches the same arithmetic once the formulas are written, and it wins when you want a saved record or to model raises across years. For a one-off conversion the tool is faster and cannot slip a cell reference - and the reverse direction is one select away instead of a second formula.


Against payroll and take-home calculators

Take-home tools estimate taxes and deductions, which is exactly why they need your location and filing details before they answer. This page deliberately stops at gross pay: three inputs, nothing to configure, nothing stored. The tradeoff in one line - an exact gross conversion in seconds, and no net-pay claim at all.
