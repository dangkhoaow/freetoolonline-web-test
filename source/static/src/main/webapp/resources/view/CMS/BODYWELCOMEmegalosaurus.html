Megalosaurus lived in the Middle Jurassic, about 168 to 166 million years ago (Bathonian), in what is now England. Modern estimates put a typical adult at about 6 to 9 m long and about 0.7 to 1.1 tonnes, depending on which bones and scaling methods are used. William Buckland named Megalosaurus in 1824 - decades before Richard Owen coined the word "Dinosauria" in 1842 - making it the first dinosaur to receive a scientific name that still stands.

Compared with later Jurassic giants such as Allosaurus, Megalosaurus is a Middle Jurassic megalosaurid: robust, bipedal, and still incompletely known from fragmentary remains. The panel cites ranges rather than a single invented midpoint when published reconstructions disagree.
