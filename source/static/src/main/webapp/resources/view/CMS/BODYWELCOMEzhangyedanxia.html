Zhangye Danxia 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-28

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Zhangye Danxia as a set of rounded hills near the Qilian Mountains in Gansu, China, crossed by tilted bands of red, orange, yellow and green rock. Toggle golden-hour lighting to see the colors deepen the way they do near sunrise and sunset, and the marker showing the elevated wooden boardwalks built to protect the fragile rock surface.

Zhangye Danxia is a Danxia ("rosy cloud") landform - red sandstone and siltstone layers deposited over roughly 24 million years, then tectonically uplifted and eroded into rounded ridges and canyons. The colors come from different mineral content and oxidation states in the layers: iron oxide produces red and orange bands, while other minerals produce yellow, green, and grey-purple bands. Because the stripes follow the original tilted sedimentary layers rather than the modern hillside outline, they cut across the rounded terrain at an angle instead of wrapping around it. The geopark covers roughly 300 sq km across two main scenic areas, the narrower Binggou Danxia and the rounded Colorful Hills (Qicai Danxia), at elevations of roughly 2,000 to 3,800 m, and forms part of the "China Danxia" UNESCO World Heritage Site, a joint listing of six Danxia landform areas inscribed in 2010.

The whole scene - the rounded hills, the tilted color stripes, the golden-hour lighting toggle, and the boardwalk markers - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to trace how the stripes cut across the hill shapes at an angle, and toggle golden-hour light to see the colors deepen. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the hill shapes, stripe placement, lighting toggle, and boardwalk markers are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
