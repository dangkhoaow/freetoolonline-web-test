Frequently Asked Questions

Where are the Galapagos Islands?

They sit in the Pacific Ocean about 1,000 km (620 mi) west of mainland Ecuador, straddling the equator - the archipelago belongs to Ecuador.

Why is Isabela Island shaped like a seahorse?

Isabela is really six shield volcanoes - Wolf, Ecuador, Darwin, Alcedo, Sierra Negra, and Cerro Azul - joined into one elongated landmass by lava fields, which gives the island its distinctive curved outline.

Does the equator really cross the islands?

Yes. The archipelago straddles the equator, and Volcan Ecuador on northern Isabela is named for sitting almost exactly on that line.

How many islands are in the Galapagos?

Commonly cited figures list 13 major islands, 6 smaller islands, and more than 100 islets and rocks, spread across a marine reserve of about 133,000 sq km.

What is the highest point in the Galapagos?

Volcan Wolf on Isabela, at about 1,707 m (5,600 ft), is the archipelago's highest point.

Why are the Galapagos Islands famous for evolution?

Charles Darwin visited in 1835 aboard HMS Beagle. The islands' isolated wildlife, including several distinct giant tortoise populations found on different islands, later informed his observations in On the Origin of Species (1859).

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - island positions, sizes, and shapes are procedural teaching cues, not survey geography.
