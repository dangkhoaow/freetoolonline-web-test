Frequently Asked Questions

Who discovered Waitomo Caves?

Local Maori chief Tane Tinorau and English surveyor Fred Mace explored it by raft in 1887, following the underground Waitomo Stream.

What makes the glowworms glow?

A chemical reaction in the tail of the Arachnocampa luminosa larva, a glowworm species found only in New Zealand, produces a soft blue-green light that lures flying insects into sticky silk threads.

When did Waitomo Caves open to visitors?

1889, two years after its 1887 exploration. Tane Tinorau's descendants still co-manage tours of the Glowworm Grotto today.

What rock is the cave carved from?

Limestone laid down under an ancient sea roughly 30 million years ago, later uplifted and eroded by the Waitomo Stream that still flows through it.

Where is Waitomo Caves?

Near Otorohanga, in the Waikato region of New Zealand's North Island.

How do visitors see the glowworms?

By gliding through the dark Glowworm Grotto in a small boat along the Waitomo Stream, looking up at the glowing ceiling.

Is this a geological simulation?

No. It is an educational explorer using published discovery and geology figures - the passage shape, glowworm density, and lighting are procedural teaching cues.
