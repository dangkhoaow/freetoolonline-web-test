Frequently Asked Questions

How big was Brachiosaurus?

The Natural History Museum cites a length of about 22 m and a mass of about 46.9 tonnes for the type specimen. Other sources give a range of about 18-22 m long and about 28-47 tonnes, since a complete skeleton has never been found. Turn on the human figure in the viewer to see the scale against a 1.8 m person.

Why does Brachiosaurus stand at an angle?

Its front legs were longer than its hind legs, which is unusual among sauropods and tilts the body upward from hips to shoulders. Combined with a long neck, this let it browse leaves up to about 9 m above the ground, higher than most other Late Jurassic plant-eaters could reach.

When and where did Brachiosaurus live?

In the Late Jurassic, about 154 to 153 million years ago, in the Morrison Formation of what is now Colorado and nearby states. The first remains were found near Fruita, Colorado in 1900, and Elmer S. Riggs named the genus in 1903.

Is the model scientifically accurate?

The proportions follow published figures, but no complete Brachiosaurus skeleton has ever been found, so this model - like most reconstructions of the species - follows the general body plan supported by the fossil material and the closely related Giraffatitan. Skin color and soft-tissue outline are an artistic reconstruction; the length, weight, and age figures shown are real published values, with ranges cited where sources disagree.

Do I need to install anything to view it?

No. The model renders in your browser with WebGL - no app, no account, and nothing about your visit is sent to a server. The 3D engine loads once and is then cached. When available, a free-licensed glTF model may swap in after first paint.
