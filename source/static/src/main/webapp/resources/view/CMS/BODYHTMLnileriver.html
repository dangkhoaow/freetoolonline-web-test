The Nile is the world's longest river at approximately 6,650 kilometers, flowing north from the East African highlands (Lake Victoria) through Sudan and Egypt to the Mediterranean Sea. The river passes through 11 countries and drains a basin of about 2.8 million square kilometers.

For millennia, the annual flood cycle - with summer inundation deposits (June to September) - shaped Egyptian agriculture and civilization. The Aswan High Dam, completed in 1970, created Lake Nasser and transformed the seasonal cycle into a year-round controlled water supply.
