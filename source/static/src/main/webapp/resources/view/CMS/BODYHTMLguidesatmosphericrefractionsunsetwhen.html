When to Use the Atmospheric Refraction Sunset 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-14
freetoolonline.com editorial team

Open the Atmospheric Refraction Sunset 3D Explorer when you want a hands-on answer to why the setting Sun looks flattened, sits higher than its true position, and can briefly flash green.


Sessions this explorer fits

Students and teachers introducing atmospheric refraction, readers curious why "sunset" is defined the way it is, anyone wanting to understand the physical cause of a green flash before trying to spot one, and readers who want to see the Sun's true and apparent positions separate all get a direct answer here.


When Twilight Types is the better page instead

If the real question is about the wider civil, nautical, and astronomical twilight sky-brightness bands that fade across the much larger 0-18 degree range below the horizon, this explorer is the wrong stop. The Twilight Types 3D Explorer renders that global sky darkness after sunset, whereas this page isolates the sub-degree optical bending of the Sun's own disk right at the horizon. Once you have landed on the right page, work through each control at your own pace with the step-by-step guide.
