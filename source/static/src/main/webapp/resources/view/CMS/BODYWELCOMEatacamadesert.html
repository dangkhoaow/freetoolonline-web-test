Atacama Desert 3D Explorer

The Atacama Desert is the driest place on Earth outside the polar regions, with some areas receiving zero measurable rainfall for years. Perched at about 2,000 - 3,000 meters elevation in the Central Andes, the Atacama spans roughly 140,000 square kilometers across northern Chile, Peru, Bolivia, and Argentina. Its hyperarid climate preserves extreme clarity - the region hosts some of the world's most advanced astronomical observatories precisely because the dry, cloudless skies offer unparalleled visibility.

The landscape is dominated by salt flats (Salar de Atacama being the core feature at about 3,000 sq km), rocky plateaus, sparse dune fields, lava flows from ancient volcanic activity, and a chain of volcanic peaks. The Central Volcanic Zone nearby contains over 200 volcanoes, 27 of which are still active. Two iconic peaks visible from the tourist town of San Pedro de Atacama are Licancabur (about 5,960 m) and Juriques (about 6,178 m), both active volcanoes that frame the desert landscape.
