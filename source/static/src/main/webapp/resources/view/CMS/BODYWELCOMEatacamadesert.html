The Atacama Desert is Earth's driest place by precipitation. Spanning over 1 million square kilometers across northern Chile, Peru, Bolivia, and Argentina, it receives less than 1 millimeter of rain per year - some coastal sections record virtually zero rainfall in recorded history. The white salt flats (Salar de Atacama covers about 3,000 square kilometers) contrast dramatically with rust-red sand dunes, creating a surreal, almost extraterrestrial landscape.
