The Atacama Desert at a glance

The Atacama Desert is Earth's driest desert, spanning approximately 600 kilometers north to south and 160 kilometers east to west across northern Chile, southern Peru, and southwestern Bolivia. At elevations of 2,000 to 3,000 meters on average, the Atacama experiences extreme aridity - some regions have never recorded a single drop of rain in recorded history.

Terrain and landscape

The terrain features rust-red dunes, stark salt flats, and vast expanses of barren rock. A dry river valley carved through the landscape, while sparse vegetation clings to life in this hyper-arid environment. The extreme temperature fluctuations between day and night create a stark, otherworldly appearance unique among Earth's deserts.
