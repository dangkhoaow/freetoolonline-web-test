Frequently Asked Questions

Which recalls does this tool cover?

US consumer products in the Consumer Product Safety Commission recall database at saferproducts.gov - toys, nursery gear, appliances, furniture, electronics, sports equipment, and similar goods sold in the United States. Vehicles are NHTSA territory and food and medicines are FDA systems - this site has dedicated vehicle, food, and drug recall lookups for those.

What should I type to find a product?

Start with the product type - "stroller", "space heater", "crib" - or the brand printed on the product. The search checks recall titles first and automatically retries by manufacturer name when the title search returns nothing, so a firm name works too. Shorter, more generic keywords match more reports than full product names.

What do the refund, repair, and replace badges mean?

They are the remedy options the firm offers in the official notice. Refund means your money back, and the notice says whether you must return, destroy, or photograph the product first. Repair means a fix - a repair kit, a replacement part, or free service. Replace means the firm swaps the recalled product for a new or equivalent unit. Whichever applies, the remedy is provided free of charge.

A product I own is recalled - what do I do?

Stop using it immediately, especially anything for babies or children. Open the official notice (each result title links to the cpsc.gov page), compare the model number and date codes against your product, and follow the remedy instructions - the contact details and whether to return, fix, or dispose of the product are all in the notice.

Why does a recall from today's news not show up?

New recalls can take hours to days to appear in the database this page queries. If you just saw a recall announced, check cpsc.gov/Recalls for the notice itself; this tool is best for checking products you own and confirming details - models, hazard, remedy - once the record is published.

Does the hazard box change what matches?

Yes - it narrows results to recalls whose hazard field matches your keyword, so "choking" finds choking hazards and "fire" finds fire and burn hazards. Leave it empty for normal searches; combine it with a product keyword to answer questions like "recalled heaters with a fire hazard".

My search found nothing - does that mean my product is safe?

No. It only means no CPSC recall record matched that query. Try a shorter keyword, the brand instead of the product type, or the manufacturer name - and check cpsc.gov/Recalls directly for very recent announcements. If your product hurt someone or seems hazardous, you can report it at SaferProducts.gov, where reports help trigger future recalls.

Is my search private?

Your keywords go directly from your browser to the official US-government CPSC API at saferproducts.gov - never to this site's servers - and this page does not store them. The CPSC API is keyless and free; no account is needed on either side.

Does this cover recalls outside the United States?

No. The CPSC database covers consumer products sold in the US market. Other countries publish their own recall feeds (for example recalls-rappels.canada.ca in Canada or productsafety.gov.au in Australia) - this page does not query them.
