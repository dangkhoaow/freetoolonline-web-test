Where the Twilight Types Explorer wins

The Twilight Types Explorer wins when the question is how dark the whole sky gets, and for how long, once the Sun is several degrees below the horizon - including why some latitudes get white nights in summer.


Where the Atmospheric Refraction Sunset Explorer wins

The Atmospheric Refraction Sunset Explorer wins when the question is why the Sun's own disk looks flattened, sits higher than its true position, and can briefly flash green right at the horizon crossing - a sub-degree optical story the Twilight Types Explorer does not cover.


A reasonable rule

As a reasonable rule, use the Atmospheric Refraction Sunset Explorer first to see what happens to the Sun's disk itself in the last few minutes before it sets, then use the Twilight Types Explorer to see how the whole sky dims afterward.
