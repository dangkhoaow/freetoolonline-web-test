This browser explorer shows Lake Superior in cross-section: the largest freshwater lake on Earth by surface area, an elongated basin with a deep east-central trough, Isle Royale rising above the surface near the northwest shore, and a marker at the storm-famous Edmund Fitzgerald wreck site near Whitefish Bay.

Lake Superior is the northernmost and largest of the five Great Lakes of North America, bordered by the United States (Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan) and Canada (Ontario). Its basin holds about 12,100 cubic km of water - roughly 10% of the world's surface fresh water - and the lake's long retention time of about 191 years means water entering today stays for generations before it leaves. Cold, deep water and sudden storms have claimed more than 350 known shipwrecks, most famously the SS Edmund Fitzgerald, which sank on November 10, 1975 near Whitefish Bay.
