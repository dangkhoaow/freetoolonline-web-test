Frequently Asked Questions

What does this page show?

The next daylight saving clock change for your auto-detected timezone: the exact local date and minute, whether clocks go forward or back, the size of the shift, and a live countdown to that moment. You can search any other timezone to check it instead.

How does it know when my clocks change?

It reads your browser's built-in timezone database and scans the next 400 days for the first moment your zone's UTC offset shifts, then refines that to the minute. Nothing is uploaded and no server is involved. Because it relies on the database your browser ships with, a very recent law change may not appear until browsers update.

My country does not use daylight saving - what will I see?

A clear "no clock changes in the next 13 months" answer with your zone's year-round UTC offset. Vietnam, India, Japan and most of Asia fall in this group.

Is this a countdown timer I can set myself?

No. The target is always the next clock change derived from the timezone database - there is no date entry and no start or pause control. For a user-set countdown, use the countdown timer or event time countdown instead.

Can I check the next clock change for a city that is not in the comparison table?

Yes. Type any place or region name into the timezone field and pick a match from the list - the summary, the live countdown and the "no DST" state all update for that zone immediately.

When do the clocks go back in the US and Canada in 2026?

Most of the US and Canada set clocks back one hour at 2:00 a.m. local time on Sunday, November 1, 2026, so 2:00 a.m. becomes 1:00 a.m. and that hour happens twice - this "fall back" is the one that gains you an hour rather than losing one. Search your own timezone above to see the exact local moment and the live countdown to it.

Is daylight saving time being abolished before the 2026 change?

Not yet. A bill to end the twice-yearly clock change, H.R. 139, passed the US House on July 14, 2026, but has not passed the Senate, so the November 1 change above is still the current law. This page reads your browser's timezone database, not legislation directly, so if a change is ever signed into law the countdown will only reflect it once your browser ships an updated database - checking back after a browser update is the reliable way to see a newly-enacted change.
