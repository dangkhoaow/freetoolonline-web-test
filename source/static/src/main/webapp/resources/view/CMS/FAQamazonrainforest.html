Frequently Asked Questions

How big is the Amazon Rainforest?

The rainforest's core spreads across about 5.5 million sq km and nine countries, with roughly 60% of it inside Brazil.

How tall is the Amazon's canopy?

The main canopy typically stands about 30-45 m tall. Scattered emergent trees break through the canopy to about 55-60 m, and the tallest Amazon tree measured to date reached about 88 m in 2019.

How long is the Amazon River?

About 6,400 km, with an average discharge of about 209,000 cubic meters per second - roughly a fifth of all river water reaching the world's oceans.

How many species live in the Amazon?

The basin is estimated to hold about one in ten of the world's known species, though exact counts vary by taxonomic group and survey method.

What are emergent trees?

Trees whose crowns rise above the main forest canopy, catching more direct sunlight - they are shown here as small markers over the taller canopy bumps.

Is this a survey map?

No. It is an educational explorer using published rainforest and river figures - the canopy bands, emergent-tree markers, and river channel are schematic teaching cues, not a survey map.
