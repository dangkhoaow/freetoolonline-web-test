Where the Expanding Universe Explorer wins

The Expanding Universe Explorer wins when the question is the mechanism itself - how recession speed scales with distance, and what the Hubble tension between early- and late-universe H0 measurements means.


Where the Horizon Explorer wins

The Horizon Explorer wins when the question is the resulting geometry - why the observable universe's real edge is 46.5 billion light-years away rather than the 13.8 billion light-years its age alone would suggest, and how that compares on one log scale to the Milky Way, Local Group, and Hubble radius.


A reasonable rule

As a reasonable rule, use the Expanding Universe Explorer first to see why space stretches and recession speed grows with distance, then use the Horizon Explorer to see how that stretching adds up over 13.8 billion years into a 46.5 billion light-year horizon.
