How to Fill In the Time Card Calculator - Step by Step

Last reviewed: 2026-10-02

The time card calculator adds up a week of work hours from clock-in, clock-out and break minutes, live as you type. These are the exact inputs, what each accepts, and how the rate and overtime fields extend the result.


Step 1 - open the tool

Open the calculator and a full result is already on screen: the week loads pre-filled with Monday to Friday 9:00-17:00 and a 30-minute break, so the weekly total - 37:30 on the clock, 37.50 in decimal hours - and the reference table are visible before you type anything.


Step 2 - enter each day's clock times

Overwrite any row with your own clock-in and clock-out times. Every change recalculates immediately - there is no submit button - and each day's hours appear in the row twice, as hours and minutes (7:30) and as the decimal figure payroll systems expect (7.50). A day missing either time shows a dash and simply contributes nothing to the total.


Step 3 - subtract the breaks

Type each day's unpaid break in minutes (0 to 720). The break is subtracted from that day's span before the total, so a 9:00-17:00 day with a 30-minute break counts 7:30, not 8:00.


Step 4 - night shifts that end after midnight

When a clock-out time is at or before the clock-in time, the calculator rolls the shift past midnight instead of producing a negative day: 22:00 to 06:00 counts as 8 hours before the break. No extra setting is needed.


Step 5 - add a rate or an overtime threshold if you want them

An hourly rate above 0 adds a gross-pay line for the week - gross only, before any taxes or deductions. The weekly overtime threshold is preset to 40 and is your own number to change: when the week exceeds it, the result splits into regular and over-threshold hours. The calculator applies your threshold; it does not look up any country's or state's rule.


Step 6 - read the reference table

Below the result, a table lists the standard minute conversions - 15 minutes is 0.25, 30 is 0.50, 45 is 0.75 - so a part-hour entry on a paper time sheet never needs guessing. Nothing you type is stored or sent anywhere, so copy the totals into your own records before closing the tab.
