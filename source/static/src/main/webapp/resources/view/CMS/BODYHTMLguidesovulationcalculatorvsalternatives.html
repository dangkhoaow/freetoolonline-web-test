Against period-tracking apps

An app that logs every cycle can average your real cycle lengths and adapt its predictions; this page deliberately keeps nothing, so each visit starts fresh from the two numbers you type. That is the privacy tradeoff in one line: no history to refine the estimate, and no fertility data stored anywhere either.


Against basal-temperature and hormone tracking

Charting basal body temperature or using hormone test strips detects ovulation from your body's signals rather than assuming it from calendar math - the usual recommendation when cycles are irregular. This calculator does not measure anything; it is the quick first estimate, not the measurement.


Against a paper calendar

The math is the same calendar method - last period start plus cycle length minus the luteal phase - but the tool does it live for three cycles at once and cannot slip a day the way a hand count can. Paper wins only when you want a physical record.
