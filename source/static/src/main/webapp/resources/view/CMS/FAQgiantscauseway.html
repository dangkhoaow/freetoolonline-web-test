Frequently Asked Questions

How many columns does the Giant's Causeway have?

About 40,000 interlocking basalt columns, mostly hexagonal (six-sided) with some four to eight-sided, forming a stepping-stone pavement out from the cliffs.

How did the Giant's Causeway form?

About 50-60 million years ago a volcanic eruption sent lava flowing across the area. As the thick lava sheet cooled and contracted, it cracked into regular columns - the same process that forms mud-crack patterns at a much larger, slower scale.

Is the Finn McCool legend true?

No - it is a well-known Irish legend, not a scientific explanation. The story says the giant Finn McCool built a causeway across to Scotland; geologically, the same lava flow does reach Fingal's Cave on the Scottish island of Staffa, which likely inspired the myth.

Where is the Giant's Causeway?

On the north Atlantic coast of County Antrim, Northern Ireland.

Is the Giant's Causeway covered by tides?

Yes - it sits on the intertidal coast, so the lower and outer columns are exposed at low tide and partly covered at high tide, the way the tide toggle in this explorer demonstrates.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the column field, tide level, and height bands are schematic teaching cues, not survey data, and the scene renders a representative dense field of columns rather than the literal 40,000.
