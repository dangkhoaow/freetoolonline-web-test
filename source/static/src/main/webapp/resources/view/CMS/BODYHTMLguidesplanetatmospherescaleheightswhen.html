When to Use the Planet Atmosphere Scale Heights 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-02

Open the Planet Atmosphere Scale Heights 3D Explorer when you want to see, side by side, how fast five real worlds' atmospheres thin out with altitude, and why one slider moves five very different curves.


Sessions this explorer fits

Students checking why Jupiter and Titan keep more of their pressure at a given altitude than Earth despite being colder, teachers showing one physics relationship apply to five different worlds with a single slider, and curious readers who want one side-by-side comparison instead of five separate fact sheets all get a direct answer here.


Sessions this explorer does not fit

Sessions this page does not fit are studying Earth's own named atmosphere layers and the space-boundary conventions, which is on the Karman Line & Atmosphere Layers 3D Explorer; detecting a molecule inside a distant exoplanet's atmosphere, which is on the Exoplanet Atmosphere Spectroscopy 3D Explorer; or studying Jupiter's own cloud bands and the Great Red Spot, which is on the Gas Giant Atmosphere 3D Explorer.
