Apollo Landing Sites on the Moon 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-07 | Authored by the FreeToolOnline team

Between 1969 and 1972, six Apollo missions put twelve astronauts on the Moon's surface at six different sites. This explorer plots every site at its real coordinates on a 3D Moon - click a marker or pick a mission from the dropdown to see the landing date, crew, and location for that flight.

Key Features

  • Six real landing sites: Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, and 17, each plotted at its published selenographic coordinates.
  • Click-to-explore markers: click any yellow pin on the 3D Moon, or use the dropdown, to load that mission's facts.
  • Real published figures: coordinates, landing dates, and crew names sourced from NASA LRO-based lunar site tables and NASA mission history.
  • Educational approximation: the Moon is a smooth procedural sphere, not a rendered terrain map, and marker size is enlarged for visibility - it is not a physical simulation.
  • No accounts needed: runs fully in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

Why Six Different Sites

NASA spread the six crewed landings across different types of lunar terrain to broaden the geologic sampling and to test the Lunar Module and later the Lunar Roving Vehicle in varied settings. Apollo 11 landed on a flat mare (lava plain) for safety on the first attempt; Apollo 15 through 17 targeted highland and mountain-edge sites for more scientifically interesting rock samples once the landing technique was proven.

Privacy + How It Runs

The Apollo Landing Sites 3D Explorer runs entirely in your browser using WebGL and the vendored three.js engine. No data is sent to any server. The Moon and its markers are generated in code (procedural geometry), and all calculations happen on your device. The facts panel displays real coordinates, dates, and crew names from NASA's published mission records.

The Space 3D collection also includes Apollo Free-Return Trajectory 3D (the Apollo 13 emergency flight path, a distinct reader task from this page's six landing locations) and Moon Libration 3D (how the Moon's slight wobble reveals a bit more than half its surface over time).
