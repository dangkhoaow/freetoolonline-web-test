Against pen, paper and spreadsheets

The math is the same - distance over MPG, or distance times L/100km over 100 - but the calculator does the unit conversions and the per-person split without a formula, and it recalculates on every keystroke, which makes trying scenarios cheap. A spreadsheet still wins when you want to keep a history of many trips in one file.


Against the in-car trip computer

The car measures what actually happened - real consumption, real cost after the fact. This tool estimates before you leave, which is what a budget needs. Use the car's measured L/100km or MPG from past trips as the efficiency input here and the two agree closely.


Against fuel-price apps

Price apps know today's cheapest pump near you - this tool deliberately does not fetch prices, so nothing about your location or route leaves the page. The practical combination: look up the price once, then bring the number here for the trip math, the round-trip toggle and the split.
