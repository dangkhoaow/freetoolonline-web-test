Frequently Asked Questions

What percentage of the Sahara is actually sand dunes?

Sand dunes, called ergs in Arabic, cover approximately 25-30% of the Sahara. The rest consists of rocky plateaus, gravel plains, and other terrain types. Despite its reputation as an endless sea of sand, the Sahara's actual dune coverage is smaller than many people expect.

How tall can Sahara sand dunes get?

Most Saharan sand dunes range from 100 to 200 meters in height. The tallest recorded dunes reach approximately 600 meters. The height of dunes depends on wind patterns, sand supply, and the surrounding terrain.

How hot does the Sahara get?

Daytime temperatures in the Sahara commonly reach 40-50°C during summer months. However, the Sahara experiences extreme diurnal temperature variation - nights can drop to near freezing, especially in winter. This thermal swing is one of the harshest environmental stresses.

How many countries border or contain the Sahara?

The Sahara spans 11 countries: Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Egypt, Sudan, Mauritania, Mali, Niger, Chad, and Western Sahara. The desert extends from the Atlantic Ocean in the west to the Red Sea in the east.

What was the historical importance of the Sahara?

The Sahara has been a crucial crossroads in human history. Trans-Saharan caravan routes connected sub-Saharan Africa with the Mediterranean for centuries, trading in salt, gold, ivory, and other goods. Oases along these routes became vital trading posts and cultural centers.
