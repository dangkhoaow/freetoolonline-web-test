Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Loch Ness so straight and narrow?

It fills part of the Great Glen Fault, a geological fault line that runs diagonally across the Scottish Highlands. The loch is one flooded segment of that fault, which is why it is long, narrow, and unusually straight compared to a winding fjord or a round volcanic caldera.

How deep is Loch Ness?

The maximum recorded depth is about 230 m (755 ft), with an average depth of about 132 m (433 ft) - deep enough that the loch alone holds more fresh water than all the lakes of England and Wales combined.

Why is the water so dark?

Peat washed in from the surrounding hills stains the water heavily, limiting visibility to just a few meters even though the loch itself is very deep.

What is the Great Glen Fault?

A major geological fault line that cuts diagonally across the Scottish Highlands. Loch Ness, along with several other lochs in the same line, fills a low-lying section of that fault.

Where is Loch Ness?

In the Scottish Highlands, near the city of Inverness, Scotland.

Is this a scientific terrain model?
