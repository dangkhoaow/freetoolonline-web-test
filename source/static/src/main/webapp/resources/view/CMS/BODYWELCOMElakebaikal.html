Lake Baikal 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Lake Baikal as a narrow rift valley cut deep into southern Siberia, flanked by steep mountains on both sides. The lake is split into three connected basins along its length - toggle the depth bands to see the shallower north basin next to the far deeper central and south basins that make Baikal the deepest lake on Earth.

Baikal reaches about 1,642 m at its deepest point in the central basin, more than twice the depth of any other lake. It stretches about 636 km end to end but is narrow, averaging around 48 km wide, and holds roughly 23,615 cu km of water - close to a quarter of all the unfrozen fresh surface water on the planet. Formed by an active continental rift that is still widening, geologists estimate the lake is 25 to 30 million years old, making it the oldest lake on Earth as well as the deepest.

  • Toggle the depth bands to compare the shallower north basin with the deeper central and south basins
  • Toggle the winter ice cover to see the lake as it freezes over most years from January into spring
  • Read depth, length, volume, and age figures in the panel
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload
