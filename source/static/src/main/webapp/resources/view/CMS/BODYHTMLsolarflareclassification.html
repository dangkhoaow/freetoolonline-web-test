About this scene

The facts panel fills when the scene loads. Drag the class slider to see the real flux and R-scale figures update.

Whenever a bright kink flares up on the Sun's disk, NOAA's GOES satellites are already measuring how bright it is in soft X-rays and sorting it into a letter class. Slide through A, B, C, M, and X here to see why an M5 triggers a NOAA alert while an X20 blacks out high-frequency radio across the whole sunlit side of Earth.

Drag to orbit and scroll or pinch to zoom. For the plasma cloud a strong flare can throw outward, see the Coronal Mass Ejection 3D Explorer; for the sunspot count this activity rides on top of, see the Sunspot Activity 3D Explorer.
