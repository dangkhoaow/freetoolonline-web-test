Caribbean Sea 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-28

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Caribbean Sea as a broad, semi-enclosed basin rather than a flat bowl. The Greater Antilles (Cuba, Hispaniola, Jamaica, Puerto Rico) frame the north edge, and the Lesser Antilles curve down the east side, together ringing the basin the way they do on a real map. Toggle the Cayman Trough highlight to see the one narrow notch that plunges far deeper than the rest of the floor, or switch on the Antilles island arc to see the island framing more clearly against the basin.

The sea covers roughly 2,754,000 sq km with an average depth of about 2,200 m, but the Cayman Trough - between the Cayman Islands and Jamaica - cuts down to about 7,686 m, the deepest point anywhere in the Caribbean Sea. More than 700 islands, islets, reefs, and cays sit within or around the basin, and the region is home to the Belize Barrier Reef, part of the Mesoamerican Reef and the second-largest barrier reef system in the world at about 300 km long. The sea also sits squarely in the Atlantic hurricane belt, with its warm surface waters feeding storms roughly June through November.

For another shallow, reef-dense basin, open the Great Barrier Reef 3D Explorer, or compare a marine basin with its own deep-versus-shallow contrast in the Black Sea 3D Explorer.

The Caribbean basin, the Greater and Lesser Antilles arc, and the deep Cayman Trough notch all render on your device with WebGL - the 3D engine downloads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached, so re-opening the explorer or orbiting the basin again is instant and nothing you view is uploaded.

This is an educational approximation - the basin profile, island-arc footprint, and vertical scale (including the trough) are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
