Aquarium Water Checker - When To Use It

Last reviewed: 2026-10-05

Aquarium Water Checker is worth opening whenever the real question is a trend: is the water clearer or cloudier than it was? It compares two photos of the same tank on your device, scores each one 0 to 100, and says which way things moved - with no upload and nothing installed.


When it fits

Typical moments: the tank turned milky after a new setup or a gravel disturbance and you want to know if the bloom is fading; you dosed bacteria or added filter floss yesterday and want proof it helped; you changed water and the cloudiness looks worse instead of better; or you simply want a weekly clarity log without buying anything. Because eyes adapt to slow change, the measured delta often contradicts your impression - that is exactly the case the tool exists for.


When a short video fits better

When the worry is the fish rather than the water - they crowd just under the surface, rest on the substrate and barely move, or dash around in sudden bursts - a 15 to 30 second clip says more than two photos. The same page reads the clip on your device and reports how active the fish were and how their movement split between the top, middle and bottom of the frame, so you can film again after a water change or extra aeration and see whether the pattern shifted. It shows the behavior, not its cause, so test the water first when fish look distressed.


When it does not fit

No photo can measure ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, pH or temperature. If fish are gasping at the surface, resting on the bottom with clamped fins, or you suspect a spike, reach for a liquid test kit first - the photo check is a trend instrument between tests, not a replacement for them. It also does not diagnose fish diseases.


What to expect

A swipe-to-compare slider over your two photos, a named cloudiness pattern (milky bloom, suspended dust, green algae direction, or amber tannin tint), and a short prioritized action list such as a feeding pause or finer filter floss. When your photos carry a capture time, the report also says how many hours or days apart the two states are.
