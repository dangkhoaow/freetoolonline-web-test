The Tibetan Plateau, known as the "Roof of the World," is Earth's highest and largest plateau, spanning approximately 2.5 million square kilometers across Central Asia. This 3D explorer displays the plateau's characteristic terrain: vast rolling uplands, deep river valleys, and peaks exceeding 7,000 meters. Use the toggles to view elevation banding and named summit markers.

Published Figures

Area About 2.5 million sq km
Average Elevation Approximately 3,600 - 4,500 meters (12,000 - 15,000 feet)
Highest Peak Mount Everest, approximately 8,849 meters (29,032 feet) - shared with Nepal
Countries Primarily China (Tibet Autonomous Region and surrounding provinces)
Major Rivers Yangtze, Yellow River (Huang He), Brahmaputra, Mekong, Salween originate here
Population Approximately 10 million people, including Tibetan and other ethnic groups
Age of Plateau Began uplift approximately 50 - 55 million years ago due to tectonic collision

Note: This 3D visualization is an educational approximation. The terrain-relief standard shows the plateau's general character and relative elevations. Peak markers represent notable summits. Heights are not to literal scale due to rendering constraints.
