Frequently Asked Questions

How big was Austroraptor?

An estimated 5-6 m long and 300-520 kg depending on the study - one of the largest raptors ever found, rivaled in size only by Utahraptor and Achillobator. Turn on the human figure in the viewer to see it against a 1.8 m person.

Why were Austroraptor's arms so short?

Its upper arm bone (humerus) measured only about 46 percent the length of its thigh bone (femur) - unusually short for a dromaeosaurid. Only a few other raptors, such as Tianyuraptor and Zhenyuanlong, share similarly reduced forelimbs; the reason is still debated among researchers.

Did Austroraptor really eat fish?

Its teeth suggest so. Unlike most raptors' blade-like serrated teeth, Austroraptor had small, conical, unserrated teeth - a pattern seen in fish-eating dinosaurs such as spinosaurids. No stomach contents have been found, so this diet is inferred from tooth shape, not directly observed.

Is the 3D model scientifically accurate?

The proportions follow the fossil skeleton, but the skin color and texture are an artistic reconstruction, and the pose shown is a resting, crouched stance rather than an active stride. Fossils preserve bone, not soft tissue or color. The length, weight, and age figures shown are real published values.

When and where did Austroraptor live?

In the Late Cretaceous, roughly 70 to 66 million years ago, in what is now Patagonia, Argentina. The holotype fossil was found in December 2002 at Bajo de Santa Rosa in the Allen Formation and was named in 2008.
