Angel Falls 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Angel Falls as the world's tallest waterfall: the Churun River plunges off the sheer sandstone edge of Auyan-tepui, a flat-topped table mountain in Canaima National Park, Bolivar State, Venezuela, dropping about 979 m to the jungle valley floor below.

The fall is not one continuous sheet of water all the way down. Its longest single, uninterrupted free-fall segment is about 807 m - roughly 15 times the height of Niagara Falls' Horseshoe Falls - before the water reaches a series of lower cascades that carry it the rest of the way to the base. Because of that extreme height and the wind at altitude, much of the water breaks apart into fine mist before it ever reaches the plunge pool; during the dry season, roughly December to March, the flow can drop so much that the falls are reduced to little more than a haze of spray. The name honors US aviator Jimmie Angel, who flew over the falls in 1933 and helped bring them to wider attention; the local Pemon name, Kerepakupai Vena, means "waterfall of the deepest place."
