Mariana Trench 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Mariana Trench in cross-section: a narrow V-shaped slot cut into the Pacific seafloor down to about 10,935 m at Challenger Deep - the deepest point on Earth. Color bands mark ocean depth zones from sunlight near the surface to hadal at the trench floor, and a translucent plane marks sea level so the true scale of the depression is visible at a glance.

The trench sits in the western Pacific east of the Mariana Islands, formed where the Pacific plate subducts beneath the smaller Mariana plate. Depth-survey figures vary by roughly 100 m between sonar campaigns, so encyclopedic sources round Challenger Deep to about 10,935 m. Pressure at the bottom reaches about 1,086 bar - roughly 1,000 times sea-level atmospheric pressure - and the water stays near-freezing, about 1-4 C, year round.

  • Toggle the sea-level plane to compare the trench floor with 0 m elevation
  • Toggle the Everest comparison to see the mountain's height planted at the trench floor - its peak still sits under about 2 km of water
  • Read depth, pressure, and temperature figures in the panel
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload
