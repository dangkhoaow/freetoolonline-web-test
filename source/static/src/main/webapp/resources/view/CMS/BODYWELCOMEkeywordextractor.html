Keyword Extractor - Pull Key Phrases from Text in Your Browser

Last reviewed: 2026-09-03

Paste any text - an article, a report, meeting notes, a product description - and this tool ranks its key phrases on your device. The instant mode counts how often each phrase appears and needs no download at all. The optional AI mode downloads a small language model (about 24 MB, one time, cached by your browser) and ranks phrases by how close each one sits to the meaning of the whole text. In both modes your text never leaves the browser.

The instant frequency mode is enough when you want a quick list of the terms a document leans on - tagging a note, labelling a support ticket, or skimming what a long page is about. The AI mode helps when frequency misleads: a phrase that appears once can still carry the core idea, and the on-device model surfaces it by meaning rather than by repetition. Each result shows its relevance score so you can judge the cutoff yourself.

You control the phrase length (single words up to three-word phrases) and how many results to show. The copy button puts the list on your clipboard, one phrase per line, ready to paste into a spreadsheet, a tag field, or an outline.

One honest boundary: relevance here means relevance to the text you pasted. The tool has no web data, so it cannot tell you search volume, ranking difficulty, or what other people type into search engines. It reads best in English; other languages work but with lower quality, and very short snippets (under about 30 words) give the model too little context to rank well.

What decides which phrases make the list?

Before ranking anything, Keyword Extractor builds a working list of candidate phrases straight from what you pasted: every one, two, and three-word run in your text, up to 400 candidates in the order they first appear. A phrase is dropped from that list if it starts or ends on a filler word like "the" or "and", or if any of its words is a single letter, so odd fragments never reach the ranking step. In the instant mode, a two or three-word phrase gets a small scoring boost over a single word with the same repeat count, so a real phrase like "quarterly report" can beat a bare, frequently repeated word like "report" on its own. In AI mode, the on-device model does something the instant mode cannot: it spreads its top picks across different ideas instead of returning several phrases that all mean roughly the same thing, so a 10-phrase result tends to cover more of what the text is actually about rather than ten near-duplicates of one point.
