When to Use the Kreutz Sungrazing Comets 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-14
freetoolonline.com editorial team

Open the Kreutz Sungrazing Comets 3D Explorer when you want a hands-on answer to why some comets pass almost at the Sun's surface and mostly do not come back, and to see three real historical comets that did.


When the Kreutz sungrazing explorer answers your question

Students and teachers introducing extreme sungrazing comets, readers curious why a comet's perihelion distance decides whether it survives, anyone wanting to see the real Great Comet of 1843, Great Comet of 1882, or Ikeya-Seki (1965) sweep through perihelion, and readers who want the "why don't these comets just come back every time" question answered directly all get a direct answer here.


When to reach for a different comet explorer instead of the Kreutz family view

Skip this Kreutz sungrazing explorer if your question is about a normal periodic comet's moderate-eccentricity loop or the ion and dust tails that stretch out far from the Sun; the Comet Orbit 3D Explorer renders a Halley-type orbit and the Comet Tail Types 3D Explorer covers generic tail physics, neither of which models the sub-solar-radius perihelion passes that define the Kreutz family and drive most of its members to fragment or evaporate near the Sun rather than survive one more pass. It also does not compute an individual comet's next return date or predict which fresh Kreutz fragment SOHO will catch next - use it to understand why the tight perihelion geometry decides survival, then walk through the controls in the step-by-step guide.
