Lake Titicaca 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Lake Titicaca in cross-section: two connected basins at very different depths, sitting at an elevation - about 3,812 m above sea level - higher than any other large lake on Earth. Lago Grande, the larger northwest basin, plunges to about 284 m, while Lago Pequeno (also called Winaymarka), the smaller southeast basin, rarely passes about 40 m. The two are joined only by the narrow Strait of Tiquina.

The lake straddles the border of Peru and Bolivia, high in the Andes, and covers a total surface area of about 8,372 sq km. Geologically it is one of the oldest lakes on Earth, roughly 3 million years old. Its waters support the Uros people, who have lived for generations on floating islands woven from totora reeds near Puno, Peru - marked in this scene by the small cluster of discs near the west shore. On the Bolivian side, Isla del Sol sits within Lago Grande and is held sacred in Inca mythology as the mythical birthplace of the sun.

  • Toggle the two-named-basin colors to compare Lago Grande against the much shallower Lago Pequeno
  • Highlight the narrow Strait of Tiquina that connects the two basins
  • Read elevation, depth, and area figures in the panel
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
