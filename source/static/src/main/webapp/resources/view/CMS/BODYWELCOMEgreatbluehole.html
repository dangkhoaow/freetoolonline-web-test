Great Blue Hole 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-01

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Great Blue Hole as a cross-section: shallow turquoise reef flat surrounding a near-vertical shaft that drops into deep navy water, at Lighthouse Reef atoll off the coast of Belize in the Caribbean Sea.

The hole formed as a limestone cave during past ice ages, when sea level was much lower and the area was dry land. As the cave's ceiling eroded and collapsed and the sea rose after the ice age, it flooded into the near-perfectly circular, steep-walled sinkhole seen today. Divers have documented limestone stalactites hanging from overhangs around 40-50 m depth - formations that only form in air, confirming the hole was once an open, dry cave before the sea reclaimed it. The site was popularized worldwide after Jacques Cousteau surveyed it in 1971 and named it one of the top diving sites on Earth.

Everything renders on your device with WebGL. The 3D engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is cached.

Two toggles carry most of the teaching value here. The depth-band coloring maps the color-coded drop from the turquoise reef flat to the deep navy floor, so the roughly 124 m plunge reads at a glance instead of as a flat number, and the stalactite-ledge marker pinpoints where divers document limestone overhangs around 40-50 m down - the tell that this sinkhole was a dry, air-filled cave before the sea flooded it. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, and your toggle choices are saved in browser storage so the scene reopens the way you last left it.

This is an educational approximation - the reef texture, wall profile, and stalactite placement are schematic teaching cues, not a survey-grade bathymetric model.
