Frequently Asked Questions

What does this vehicle recall lookup do?

It checks whether a vehicle has open US safety recalls - by 17-character VIN or by year, make, and model. Each campaign card shows the campaign number, report date, affected component, the problem, the safety risk, and the fix, newest first.

Where does the recall data come from?

From the official US-government NHTSA APIs. The VIN mode decodes the year, make, and model with the NHTSA vPIC service first, then pulls the matching recall campaigns. Results are model-level campaigns - whether a specific car already had its repair completed is confirmed by a dealer or the nhtsa.gov VIN search. Your VIN or vehicle query is sent only to NHTSA - never to this site's servers - and this page does not store it.

Is the recall repair free?

Yes - recall repairs are performed free of charge by the manufacturer's authorized dealers. After a match, the what-to-do list on this page explains the steps: contact an authorized dealer, mention the campaign number, and book the repair.

What do the do-not-drive and park-outside badges mean?

They are high-severity flags rendered straight from the NHTSA data. Do-not-drive (park it) means the vehicle is unsafe to drive until repaired. Park-outside means the defect can cause a fire even while parked, so keep the vehicle away from buildings until it is fixed. Over-the-air update means the fix arrives as a software update, with no dealer visit needed for that campaign.

Does it cover vehicles outside the United States?

No. It covers US-market vehicles only - NHTSA campaigns cover vehicles sold in the United States, and other countries' recall systems are not queried. Manufacturer service bulletins (TSBs) are also not included. A no-recalls result here is not a substitute for official manufacturer letters - confirm anything important at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
