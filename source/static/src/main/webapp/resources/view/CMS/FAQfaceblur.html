Frequently Asked Questions

Is my photo uploaded to a server?

No. The photo is opened, analyzed, and blurred entirely in your browser - it never leaves your device. The only thing that ever downloads is the face detector itself, about 1.3 MB, one time, and only after you click the AI button.

What happens if the tool misses a face?

Small, turned-away, or partly covered faces can slip under the detector's 70 percent confidence bar. Paint over the missed face with the brush and press the no-download blur button - the painted area is blurred instantly, with no network use at all.

Can it blur licence plates or tattoos automatically?

No - the on-device model detects faces only. Plates, tattoos, name tags, and house numbers are exactly what the brush is for: paint the area by hand and blur it with the no-download button.

Should I pick pixelate or smooth blur?

Pixelate gives the classic mosaic-block look and, at higher strength, is the harder of the two to reverse-read. Smooth blur looks softer and less conspicuous in a photo you still want to look natural. The strength slider scales either one, so preview both on your picture before downloading.

Does the downloaded picture keep the original photo's hidden metadata?

No. The result is a PNG re-encoded from the canvas, so the original file's embedded metadata is not carried over - the download contains the pixels you see and nothing more.

Is a blurred face guaranteed to be anonymous?

Blurring hides the face; it does not certify anonymity. Clothing, location, and the text you post alongside a photo can still identify someone, so review the whole picture before sharing. To remove a person from the scene entirely instead of hiding the face, use the object remover tool on this site.
