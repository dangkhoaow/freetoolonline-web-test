Giant's Causeway 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Giant's Causeway as a dense field of hexagonal basalt column tops - a promontory of interlocking stone "steps" pushing out from the County Antrim cliffs into the North Atlantic. Toggle the tide level to see the outer, lower columns covered and exposed the way the real intertidal causeway is, and toggle the height bands to see how columns of different heights, not a smooth surface, create the stepping-stone look.

The columns formed about 50-60 million years ago when a lava flow cooled and contracted, cracking into mostly six-sided (some four to eight-sided) pillars - the same volcanic process that formed the columns at Fingal's Cave on the Scottish island of Staffa, part of the same ancient lava flow. Local legend credits the giant Finn McCool with building the causeway across to Scotland; that is a well-known myth, not the geological explanation.

  • Toggle the tide level to compare low tide (fully exposed) with high tide (outer columns partly submerged)
  • Toggle the height bands to see the color-coded column heights that form the "steps"
  • Read column count, size, age, and heritage figures in the panel
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

The whole causeway scene - the hexagonal column field, the tide-level water plane, and the color-banded height toggle - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to see the promontory step from the cliff backdrop into the sea, and zoom in to compare the column tops. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the column field, tide level, and height bands are schematic teaching cues, not survey data. The scene renders a representative dense field of columns rather than the literal 40,000, sized to read clearly at the intended camera distance.
