Frequently Asked Questions

How tall is Angel Falls?

About 979 m (3,212 ft) in total, making it the world's tallest waterfall. Its longest single, uninterrupted free-fall drop is about 807 m (2,648 ft).

Why isn't the whole 979 m one continuous drop?

The water free-falls for about 807 m off the tepui's edge, then reaches lower cascades that carry it the rest of the way down to the valley floor, making up the remaining height.

Where is Angel Falls?

On the edge of Auyan-tepui, a table mountain in Canaima National Park, Bolivar State, Venezuela, fed by the Churun River.

Why does the water turn to mist?

The fall is so high, and the wind at altitude strong enough, that much of the water breaks apart into fine spray before it reaches the base pool - especially during the dry season, roughly December to March, when the flow drops sharply.

Where does the name "Angel Falls" come from?

It honors US aviator Jimmie Angel, who flew over the falls in 1933 and helped bring them to wider attention. The local Pemon name is Kerepakupai Vena, meaning "waterfall of the deepest place."

Is this a scientific hydrological model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the tepui shape, wall profile, and water column are schematic teaching cues, not a survey-grade model.
