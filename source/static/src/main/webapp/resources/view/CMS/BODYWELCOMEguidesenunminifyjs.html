What survives the round trip, and the two things unminify can never bring back

The Unminify button restores whitespace, line breaks, and indentation deterministically, so a bundle that arrived as one 40,000-character line reopens as legible nested blocks in the right pane, and the whole pass runs in the tab without the source ever leaving your browser. What it cannot restore is anything the minifier discarded at build time: the variables named a, b, and t stay a, b, and t, because their original names were thrown away and only a source map (the .map file shipped beside the bundle) points back to them. Comments are gone for the same reason, stripped before the file ever reached you, and constant folding is not reversed either, so a literal like 18000000 stays a bare number rather than turning back into 5 * 60 * 60 * 1000. Module structure survives as it was bundled, which is why a double-loaded polyfill shows up as two duplicate IIFE blocks you can spot at a glance during build-output verification. Most bundles of a few megabytes reformat instantly, though files past roughly 10 MB can make the editor stutter for a moment before the indented output appears. Read that way, unminify answers what the code does while a source map answers what the symbols were called - two different jobs for two different files.
