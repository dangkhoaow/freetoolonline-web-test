Last reviewed: 2026-08-04

Pick any of the eight planets and see a half-sphere cutaway of its crust, mantle, and core - click a layer to read what it is made of and whether the boundary was directly measured or modeled.

Drag to orbit the cutaway, scroll or pinch to zoom, and click any layer to open its composition note in the facts panel. Switching planets rebuilds the cutaway on the same shared size scale, so a rocky world's small iron core and a gas giant's huge, fuzzy core are directly comparable.

  • All eight planets, each with its own layer stack (2-3 layers depending on how well its interior is known)
  • Click-to-inspect layers with a composition note and a MEASURED / MODELED ESTIMATE evidence badge
  • Mars and Earth layers trace to real seismometer data - InSight (2021) for Mars, decades of seismographs for Earth
  • Jupiter and Saturn's fuzzy cores trace to gravity-field science from the Juno and Cassini Grand Finale missions
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

Mercury's core reaches about 83 percent of its total radius - the largest core-to-planet ratio in the solar system - while Earth's solid inner core, radius about 1,220 km, stays solid at temperatures that would melt iron anywhere else because of the crushing pressure. Mars's InSight mission measured a liquid core radius of about 1,830 km in 2021, bigger and lighter than earlier models predicted. Jupiter and Saturn do not have a sharp rocky ball at the center at all - Juno and Cassini gravity data both point to a large, diffuse "fuzzy" core where heavy elements mix gradually into the surrounding metallic hydrogen instead of settling into a hard boundary.

Everything renders on your device with WebGL. The 3D engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is cached; no scene data is sent to a server.

The scene is an educational approximation, not a physical simulation - layer boundaries are drawn from published missions and interior models, but Venus, Uranus, and Neptune have never had a seismometer or a close gravity-science orbiter, so their layer boundaries are the least certain of the eight and are labeled MODELED ESTIMATE throughout.
