Frequently Asked Questions

Is Mount Vesuvius still an active volcano?

Yes. Mount Vesuvius is active and last erupted in March 1944. It is the only volcano on the European mainland to have erupted within the last hundred years, and it is closely monitored for signs of renewed activity.

What is Monte Somma?

Monte Somma is the horseshoe-shaped ridge north of the modern Vesuvius cone. It is the remnant rim of an older, larger volcano whose summit collapsed long ago, leaving an open arc toward the south. The modern cone later grew inside that ancient caldera, so today the two features sit side by side.

What happened at Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD?

In 79 AD, Mount Vesuvius erupted violently, burying the nearby Roman towns of Pompeii and Herculaneum under ash and pyroclastic material. The eruption killed thousands of people but also preserved the towns in remarkable detail, giving archaeologists a rare window into daily Roman life. The event was documented by the writer Pliny the Younger.

How many people live near Mount Vesuvius today?

About 600,000 people live in the modern "Red Zone" - the area around the volcano's base identified for mandatory evacuation in the event of a major eruption. The nearby city of Naples, with a much larger metropolitan population, sits within view of the volcano about 9 km away.

How tall is Mount Vesuvius?

The modern Vesuvius cone stands about 1,281 m tall. The neighboring Monte Somma ridge, the remnant of an older volcano, reaches about 1,132 m at its highest point, Punta Nasone. Both figures can change slightly after major eruptions reshape the summit.
