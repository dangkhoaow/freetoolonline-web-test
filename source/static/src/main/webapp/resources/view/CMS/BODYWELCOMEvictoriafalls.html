Victoria Falls 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-27

This browser explorer shows Victoria Falls in cross-section: the Zambezi River crossing a wide basalt plateau, the roughly 1,708 m curtain plunging about 108 m over the lip, and the narrow, zigzagging Batoka Gorge that carries the river away downstream.

Victoria Falls sits on the Zambezi River, on the border of Zambia and Zimbabwe. Its local name, Mosi-oa-Tunya, means "The Smoke That Thunders" - the mist plume rising off the falls can be seen up to about 30 km away at peak flood, usually around April. The falls formed as the river cut backward through a series of basalt cracks, leaving the sharp zigzag bends of the Batoka Gorge below.

The whole scene - the basalt plateau, the wide Zambezi channel, the falls curtain sections, the mist plume, and the zigzag Batoka Gorge downstream - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to see the plateau split open along the falls line, and zoom in to compare the flood-season and dry-season curtain width. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the plateau relief, gorge zigzag, and curtain sections are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
