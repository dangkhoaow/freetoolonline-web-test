Abelisaurus 3D Viewer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-20

This page renders Abelisaurus as a 3D model you can spin in the browser - drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, toggle a 1.8 m person beside it for scale, and click the skull, a short forelimb, or a hind leg to read a fact about that part. The buttons below the scene let you go fullscreen for a closer look, stop the auto-rotation when you want to study one angle, or add a gentle idle motion so the reconstruction reads as a living animal rather than a static prop. When the scene finishes loading, the panel beside the model fills in on its own with the published figures, and clicking an empty patch of the canvas clears any part note and brings the full fact sheet back, so you can move between the whole animal and a single bone without losing your place. When a free-licensed detailed model is published for this species, it swaps in quietly a moment after the first frame so the shape sharpens without a page reload, and the built-in procedural model stays in place whenever no such file is available, which means the scene always shows something to explore rather than stalling on a missing download.

When you switch on the scale figure, the person is not dropped in at a fixed size - it is scaled from the same length the panel reports, so a roughly 1.8 m human stands against the body at an honest ratio rather than an exaggerated one. That turns a printed figure like 7.4 m into an immediate sense of bulk, and because the pair share the scene you can spin them together and judge the animal's reach and standing height from any angle instead of trusting a single side-on picture.

Abelisaurus is known from a partial skull from the Anacleto Formation of Argentina. Named by Bonaparte and Novas in 1985, it was the first abelisaurid to receive a formal name. Published length estimates span about 7.2 to 11 m and mass about 1.4 to 3 t; this viewer uses about 7.4 m and about 2 t as mid-range values within those ranges. The body you see is inferred from close abelisaurid relatives - not from a complete Abelisaurus skeleton.

Everything runs on your device with WebGL, so the model works without an account, without any install, and without sending anything to a server. Because the whole scene renders locally, the controls stay responsive: you can drop into fullscreen for a closer look while the buttons remain in reach, stop the auto-rotation to hold one angle steady, or switch on a gentle idle motion so the reconstruction reads as a living animal instead of a frozen prop. The skin tone and pattern are an artistic reconstruction, because fossils do not preserve color or soft tissue; this model is not a fossil-accurate skeleton.
