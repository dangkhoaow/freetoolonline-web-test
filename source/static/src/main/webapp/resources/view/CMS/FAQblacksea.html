Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the Black Sea called anoxic?

Below a thin oxygen-rich surface layer, only about 150-200 m thick, dissolved oxygen runs out almost completely - about 90 percent of the sea's total volume is anoxic (oxygen-free) down to the 2,212 m floor.

How deep is the Black Sea?

The maximum depth is about 2,212 m and the mean depth is about 1,253 m, though the northwest shelf near Ukraine and Crimea stays much shallower.

Why doesn't the deep water mix with the surface?

The Black Sea is strongly stratified: dense salty water flows in from the Mediterranean along the bottom while fresher river water sits on top, so the layers almost never mix - which is also why the deep anoxic zone stays oxygen-free.

Which countries border the Black Sea?

Turkey and Bulgaria and Romania to the south and west, Ukraine and Russia to the north and east, and Georgia to the east.

Is the Black Sea salty?

Yes, but only about half as salty as the open ocean - around 18 parts per thousand versus roughly 35 for world ocean average - because of heavy freshwater inflow from rivers like the Danube.

Is this a scientific terrain model?
