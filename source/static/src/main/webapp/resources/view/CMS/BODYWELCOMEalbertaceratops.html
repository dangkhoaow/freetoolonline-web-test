Albertaceratops 3D Viewer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-18

Rotate this Albertaceratops model with a drag, zoom in with scroll or pinch, and size it against a 1.8 m person standing next to it. Three clickable spots - the brow horns, the frill, and a shoulder - each surface a short fact.

A row of buttons under the scene controls the view. The model turns slowly on its own, and Stop rotation freezes it so you can hold one angle; Idle motion switches on a subtle animation so the pose is not perfectly still; Fullscreen opens the viewer edge to edge for a closer look at the frill and horns. The same controls are available on phone and desktop.

Albertaceratops lived in the Late Cretaceous, about 80 to 75 million years ago (often cited near 77.5 Mya), in Alberta. Length estimates run about 5.8 to 7 m; mass about 2 to 3.5 tonnes. It is a basal centrosaurine with long brow horns unusual for that group - different from Triceratops (later chasmosaurine) and from Pachyrhinosaurus (nasal boss, short brow horns).
