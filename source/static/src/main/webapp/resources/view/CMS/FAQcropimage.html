What does the crop tool do?

It trims an image to a specific aspect ratio and rotates it in 45-degree steps or at a free angle. The crop runs in your browser; the source format (JPG, PNG, WebP) is preserved when you download the result.

Which aspect ratios does the tool support?

The presets cover the common social sizes - 1:1, 16:9, 4:3, and 2:3 - so you avoid manual pixel math. Pick Free for a custom rectangle when the picture needs an off-preset shape.

Can I crop to an exact pixel size?

Yes. Beside the image the X, Y, Width, and Height inputs accept direct pixel values, so you can reframe a 1920x1080 source to a 1080x1080 square - or any custom rectangle - by typing the numbers instead of eyeballing the drag handles. The live preview updates at the natural output size as you type, so you confirm the exact dimensions before you download.

Does cropping change the image file format or quality?

The tool keeps the format you started with: a JPG stays a JPG, a PNG stays a PNG, and a WebP stays a WebP when you download the cropped result. Because the trim and rotate run on a canvas inside your browser, the pixels outside the crop box are simply discarded and the kept region is re-encoded in the same format, so there is no round-trip through a different file type. For lossless formats such as PNG the retained area stays pixel-exact; for JPG the kept region is re-saved at the tool's standard quality, which is why a heavily cropped JPG can look marginally softer than the original crop region.

Can I straighten a tilted photo before I crop it?

Yes. Use rotate-left or rotate-right to turn the working image in 45-degree steps, or drag the free-angle rotation handle for a continuous tilt correction - handy for a sideways phone photo or a horizon that is a few degrees off level. The crop box stays anchored to the rotated content, so you line up the frame once and the straighten and the trim land together in a single download. The whole adjustment happens on the canvas in your browser, with no server upload.

Will the cropped file still open in the app I am sending it to?

Yes, because the download keeps the exact format you started with: a JPG comes back as a JPG, a PNG as a PNG, and a WebP as a WebP, with no silent swap to a different file type. That matters when the place you upload to is picky - a print shop that only accepts JPG, a web form that rejects WebP, or a logo slot that needs the transparency a PNG carries. You decide the format when you pick the source file; the crop only trims the pixels outside the frame and re-saves the kept region in that same format, so the file you download drops straight into the destination without a separate convert step.

How do I crop precisely when the detail is small or the image is large?

Zoom in on the working canvas first, then switch the drag-mode toggle from Crop to Move so a drag pans the magnified region instead of drawing a box - that lets you reach a small logo or a face in the corner of a big photo. Flip the toggle back to Crop and draw the frame over the part you enlarged. The preview pane updates at the natural output size as the box moves, so you settle the "is this the right ratio?" question before the Download click rather than after.
