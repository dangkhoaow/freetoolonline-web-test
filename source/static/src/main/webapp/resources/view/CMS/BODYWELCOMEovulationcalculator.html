Ovulation Calculator

Last reviewed: 2026-09-30

The ovulation calculator estimates three dates from two numbers you already know - the first day of your last period and your average cycle length: the estimated ovulation day, the 6-day fertile window around it, and when your next period is expected. Everything runs in your browser; nothing you enter is stored or sent anywhere.

It uses the calendar method: ovulation is assumed to fall a luteal-phase length before the next period starts. The luteal field defaults to 14 days and accepts 10 to 16 if you know yours; cycle length accepts 21 to 45 days. The fertile window shown is the 5 days before estimated ovulation through ovulation day itself.

A look-ahead table lists the fertile window, estimated ovulation and next-period date for the current and next two cycles, so a date a few weeks out is one glance away rather than a hand count on a calendar. Every field recalculates the result as you type, and clearing the date shows a prompt instead of broken output.

The result is a planning estimate, not a diagnosis: the calendar method assumes your cycles run at a consistent length, and actual ovulation varies from cycle to cycle - the page says so right in the result. It is not a contraception method. And it answers the before question only: for a due date after a positive test, use the pregnancy due date calculator instead.
