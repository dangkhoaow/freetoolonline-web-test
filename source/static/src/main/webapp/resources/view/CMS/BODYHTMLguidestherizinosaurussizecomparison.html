What a person would see beside a Therizinosaurus

What a person would see is a bipedal, long-necked herbivorous theropod with dramatically enlarged forelimbs and giant hand claws - turn on the person in the viewer and orbit beside a hind leg or a giant claw to feel how the body sits relative to the scale figure. Skin color is an artistic reconstruction; overall body shape is inferred from more complete relatives (Beipiaosaurus, Nothronychus), and feathering is inferred from Beipiaosaurus, not directly observed on Therizinosaurus.

The Therizinosaurus facts panel and the giant-claw click

Beyond the published-figures table above, the Therizinosaurus facts panel also carries the naming history: Evgeny Maleev first described the fossil in 1954, from a holotype (PIN 551-483) discovered in 1948 in the Nemegt Formation, Gobi Desert, Mongolia. A short click, not a drag, on a giant hand claw swaps in a dedicated giant-claw fact instead of the generic arm note - the one click target unique to this species' facts panel; clicking the head, body, neck, tail, or a leg surfaces its own short note, and clicking empty space in the viewer returns to the full fact sheet.


A herbivore with the most fearsome-looking claws in the collection

Despite carrying the longest claws of any known land animal, Therizinosaurus was a herbivore (high-browser), not a predator - a counterintuitive pairing the facts panel discloses directly rather than letting the claws imply a hunting role; researchers propose the claws served foraging, defense, or display, not predation. The overall body shape shown is inferred from more complete relatives, Beipiaosaurus and Nothronychus, because Therizinosaurus itself is known almost entirely from its arms and claws - a fossil-incompleteness disclosure stronger than any other species shipped in this collection so far, including Carnotaurus, whose main body was known and only the tail and legs were inferred.

Frequently asked questions

Who named Therizinosaurus, and when?

Evgeny Maleev in 1954, from a holotype (PIN 551-483) discovered in 1948 in the Nemegt Formation, Gobi Desert, Mongolia.

Why does the facts panel call Therizinosaurus a herbivore if it has huge claws?

The giant claws are disclosed as most likely used for foraging vegetation, defense, or display, not hunting - Therizinosaurus was a high-browsing herbivore despite having the most fearsome-looking claws in the collection.

Which body part gives Therizinosaurus its own dedicated fact in the viewer?

A giant hand claw. Clicking it swaps in a dedicated giant-claw fact instead of the generic arm note that other species' forelimbs use.

How complete is the Therizinosaurus fossil record?

Very incomplete for this genus alone - Therizinosaurus is known almost entirely from its arms and claws, so the overall body shape shown is inferred from more complete relatives, Beipiaosaurus and Nothronychus, not from Therizinosaurus's own fossils.

Did Therizinosaurus have feathers?

No feather fossils are known from Therizinosaurus itself; feathering is inferred from quill-like structures on the smaller, more completely known relative Beipiaosaurus.

What do the Stop rotation and Idle motion controls do?

Stop rotation freezes the auto-spin and flips its label to Auto-rotate; Idle motion adds a subtle breathing and leg-sway loop and flips its label to Stand still when tapped again. Both sit under the canvas alongside Fullscreen and Size vs human.
