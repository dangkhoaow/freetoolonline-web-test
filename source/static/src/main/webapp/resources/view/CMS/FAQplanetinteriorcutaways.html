Frequently Asked Questions

What does Planet Interior Cutaways 3D Explorer show?

A half-sphere cutaway of any of the eight planets, layer by layer - crust, mantle, and core. Pick a planet button, then click a layer in the 3D view (or read the table) to see its composition, depth, and how confidently the boundary is known.

How is this different from Planet Size Comparison 3D Explorer?

Planet Size Comparison lines up whole spheres by outer radius. This explorer cuts each planet open to show what is INSIDE - the crust, mantle, and core layers and how deep each one starts.

What does MEASURED vs MODELED ESTIMATE mean?

MEASURED means an instrument directly probed the interior - InSight's seismometer on Mars (2021) or decades of seismographs on Earth. MODELED ESTIMATE means the boundary comes from gravity-field data (Juno at Jupiter, Cassini's Grand Finale at Saturn) or mass and moment-of-inertia calculations, not a direct probe.

Why don't Jupiter and Saturn have a sharp rocky core?

Juno's and Cassini's gravity-science passes both found that heavy elements are diffused into the surrounding metallic hydrogen over a large region instead of settling into a hard boundary - scientists call it a "dilute" or "fuzzy" core.

Which planet has the largest core relative to its size?

Mercury. Its iron-rich core reaches about 83 percent of the planet's total radius, the largest core-to-planet-radius ratio of any planet in the solar system.

Is this a physical simulation?

No. It is an educational approximation built from published mission data and interior models, not a solver. Venus, Uranus, and Neptune have never had a seismometer or close gravity-science orbiter, so their layer boundaries are the least certain of the eight.
