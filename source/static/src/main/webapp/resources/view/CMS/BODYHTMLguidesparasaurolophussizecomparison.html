Frequently asked questions

Are all Parasaurolophus crests the same shape?

No - three species are recognized, and the crest shape differs between them. P. tubicen carries a long, straight crest; P. cyrtocristatus has a shorter, more rounded one; P. walkeri (the type species) sits between the two. The Parasaurolophus 3D Viewer models a representative long form rather than picking one species to the exclusion of the others.

Can you rotate and zoom the model yourself, or does it just auto-play?

Drag to orbit and use the scroll wheel or a pinch gesture to zoom in the Parasaurolophus 3D Viewer; a separate toggle turns automatic rotation on or off, another toggles idle breathing and leg-sway motion, and a Fullscreen button expands the canvas - all independent of the Size vs human control described above.

What happens if you click directly on the head crest?

The head crest is its own clickable part, separate from the rest of the head. Clicking it surfaces a dedicated fact about the crest itself - the nasal-passage structure and the debated resonance, recognition, and heat-regulation theories - rather than the generic head fact that clicking elsewhere on the skull shows.

Does viewing the Parasaurolophus model send anything to a server?

No - the viewer renders entirely in the browser using WebGL. Rotating, zooming, toggling Size vs human, and clicking parts all happen client-side; nothing about the visit is sent to a server to generate the model or the facts panel.

Will a more detailed 3D model replace the current one?

The viewer supports an optional upgrade: when a free-licensed, higher-fidelity model becomes available for a species, it is cached in the browser and checked against a stored hash before it swaps in over the procedural shape. No such model is published for Parasaurolophus yet, so the viewer stays procedural with no loss of function in the meantime.

Where do these Parasaurolophus size figures come from?

The length, standing-height, and weight ranges are drawn from Wikipedia's synthesis of the published paleontology literature and cross-checked against Dimensions.com's standing-height and low-end weight estimates, rather than a single primary source - which is also why the figures are shown as ranges instead of one invented precise number.
