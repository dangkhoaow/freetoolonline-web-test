Mount St. Helens 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-28

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Mount St. Helens in cross-section: the horseshoe-shaped crater blasted open toward the north on May 18, 1980, and the lava dome that has been rebuilding inside it ever since. A ghost outline marks where the original, taller summit used to stand before the eruption tore it away.

Mount St. Helens is an active stratovolcano in the Cascade Range of Washington State, part of the Pacific Ring of Fire. The 1980 eruption is one of the most studied volcanic events in US history: a massive debris avalanche, commonly cited as the largest landslide ever recorded, stripped the north flank away in seconds, and the resulting lateral blast flattened forest for kilometers around. Inside the horseshoe crater left behind, a lava dome has grown episodically ever since, adding fresh rock with each new eruptive phase.

The whole Mount St. Helens scene - the horseshoe crater, the ghost outline of the lost summit, and the rebuilding lava dome - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to look into the breached crater from different angles, and toggle the pre-1980 outline to see how much of the mountain the eruption removed. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the crater shape, dome, and pre-1980 outline are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
