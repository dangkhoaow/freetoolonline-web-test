Last reviewed: 2026-09-15

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

Mosasaurus 3D Viewer pairs published marine-reptile figures with a 1.8 m person on screen - length about 11-17 m (catalogue ~17 m) and weight up to about 10 tonnes - so the scale gap stays readable at a glance.

How the Mosasaurus size-vs-human toggle stays honest

The Mosasaurus model is drawn to a fixed on-screen length so it fits the canvas; the Size vs human control then places a 1.8 m person at the true length ratio to the catalogue ~17 m figure (within the published 11-17 m range). Tap Size vs human under the canvas to show or hide the scale figure - the comparison is proportional, not a decorative sticker.


What a person would see beside a Mosasaurus

What a person would see is a scale gap of nearly nine body lengths: from snout to finned tip a large Mosasaurus can span roughly 17 m in the catalogue model, about nine body lengths of a 1.8 m person laid end to end. Turn on Size vs human and orbit beside a flipper to feel how far the tail extends past the scale figure while the animal floats on the water plane.


The four Mosasaurus controls under the canvas

Open the Mosasaurus 3D Viewer and wait for the facts panel to fill, then drag to rotate and scroll or pinch to zoom. Four controls sit under the canvas: Fullscreen expands the wrapper so the buttons stay visible beside the scene; Stop rotation freezes the auto-spin (the label flips to Auto-rotate while spinning is off); Idle motion adds a subtle flipper sway (tap again for Stand still); and Size vs human shows the 1.8 m scale figure described above (the label flips to Hide human while it is on).


The Mosasaurus facts panel and part clicks

Beyond the measures in the table above, the Mosasaurus facts panel carries the diet (carnivore) and a discovery note: the first fossils were found near Maastricht in the Netherlands in 1764, giving the animal its old nickname "Meuse river lizard." A short click - not a drag - on the head, a flipper, or the tail swaps in a note for that part; click empty space to return to the full fact sheet. The conical teeth were suited to gripping fish, ammonites, and other marine prey rather than tearing large land animals.


What the Mosasaurus size numbers are not

What these size numbers are not is a land-dinosaur estimate: the Mosasaurus size comparison uses published bone-based estimates for a swimming mosasaur - not a land dinosaur - and skin color in the viewer is still an artistic reconstruction. Frequently asked questions

Does the model show Mosasaurus's flippers and tail fin?

Yes - the model carries paddle-shaped flippers and a finned, propelling tail built for swimming, distinct from a land dinosaur's walking legs.

Which body parts can I click for facts?

Head, a flipper, or the tail - each swaps in a short note tied to that fossil evidence class; click empty space in the viewer to return to the full fact sheet.

Can I download the Mosasaurus 3D model or its mesh?

No - the viewer is view-only in the browser. There is no downloadable mesh export and no server-side rendering; everything runs client-side once the model has loaded.

Are the part-click notes a full scientific reference?

No - they are short interpretive notes keyed to fossil evidence classes, not a full osteology atlas. For the published measures themselves, use the figures table above.

Does loading the 3D engine slow down the rest of the page?

No - the three.js engine is site-vendored (not pulled from a third-party CDN) and lazy-loads after the page's initial paint, about 0.7 MB the first time, then stays cached in the browser for repeat visits.
