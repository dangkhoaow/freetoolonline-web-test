Frequently Asked Questions

What does Live AQI Checker by City do?

Check the current air quality (US AQI) for any city, right in the browser.

When should I reach for live aqi checker by city?

A 3-day peak-AQI forecast table below the result answers "will it improve tomorrow" for wildfire-smoke or pollution spikes.

Does this replace an indoor air-quality sensor or a health advisory?

No. It reports outdoor ambient air quality only - not an indoor air-quality sensor reading and not a substitute for local health advisories during a smoke event.

Is this an official EPA or AirNow reading?

No. The AQI, PM2.5, PM10, and ozone numbers are modeled by Open-Meteo's forecast system, not measured by a government ground monitoring station, so a reading here can differ from an official AirNow or EPA report for the same city.

Does Live AQI Checker by City keep a history of past AQI readings?

No. The tool shows only the current reading and the upcoming 3-day forecast window - it does not store or display any past day's AQI.

Why does my result show a different number than an app reporting the EU AQI scale?

Live AQI Checker by City requests the US AQI parameter specifically, not the EU AQI parameter the same underlying API can also return. The US and EU scales use different math, so the same pollution level can produce two different numbers depending on which scale an app asks for.

What do PM2.5, PM10, and ozone mean next to the AQI number?

Live AQI Checker by City always shows three separate pollutant readings next to the single AQI number: PM2.5 and PM10 (particulate matter, measured in micrograms per cubic meter) and ozone. PM2.5 covers the smallest, lung-penetrating particles - typically from wildfire smoke, vehicle exhaust, or industrial sources - while PM10 covers slightly larger particles such as dust, pollen, and mold spores. Ozone is a gas that forms when sunlight reacts with other pollutants, so it tends to build on hot, sunny afternoons rather than overnight. All three numbers come from the same Open-Meteo forecast as the AQI figure itself, so they update together every time you run a new lookup.
