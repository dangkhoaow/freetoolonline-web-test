Frequently Asked Questions

Where is White Sands?

In the Tularosa Basin of southern New Mexico, USA, protected as White Sands National Park.

Is White Sands really the largest gypsum dune field?

Yes - at about 275 sq mi (712 sq km), it is widely described as the largest gypsum dune field on Earth. Roughly 40% of it lies inside the national park.

Why is the sand white instead of tan or red?

It is made of gypsum (calcium sulfate) crystals rather than the quartz found in most desert sand, so it stays pale instead of picking up an iron-oxide tint.

How did the gypsum dunes form?

Gypsum washed down from the surrounding Sacramento and San Andres Mountains into ancient Lake Otero, which later dried up. Wind then ground the leftover gypsum crystals into fine white sand.

How tall are the dunes and how fast do they move?

Dunes reach up to about 60 ft (18 m) tall and migrate up to about 30 ft (9 m) per year, driven by prevailing southwest winds.

Does the white sand get as hot as other deserts?

The pale gypsum reflects sunlight rather than absorbing it, so the surface stays notably cooler underfoot than a typical hot desert dune, even under strong sun.

What is the soaptree yucca?

A dune-climbing plant that stretches its stem several meters upward to keep pace with the sand as a dune migrates over it, so it always stays above the surface.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the dune shapes, color gradient, moonlight toggle, and yucca markers are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
