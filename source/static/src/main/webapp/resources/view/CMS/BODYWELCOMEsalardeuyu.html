Explore the world's largest salt flat. Nestled at 3,655 meters in the Andes between Bolivia, Chile, and Argentina, Salar de Uyuni spreads across 10,582 square kilometers - larger than the entire country of Cyprus. The salt layer reaches depths of 100 meters in places, sitting above layers of brine and a mineral-rich lake. The white salt tiles are formed by crystallization and thermal contraction, creating natural hexagonal polygons visible from the ground. During the rainy season (December to February), standing water up to 5 centimeters deep transforms the flat into the world's largest mirror, reflecting the sky so perfectly that photographers cannot distinguish ground from clouds.
