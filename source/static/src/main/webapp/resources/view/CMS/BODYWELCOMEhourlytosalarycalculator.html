Hourly to Salary Calculator

Last reviewed: 2026-10-01

The hourly to salary calculator converts an hourly wage into weekly, biweekly, monthly and annual gross pay, or turns an annual salary back into its equivalent hourly rate.

Pick a direction, enter the amount, then set your hours per week and your paid weeks per year - the gross-pay breakdown updates on screen as you type.

How each gross-pay figure is calculated

The calculator shows the hourly, weekly, biweekly, monthly and annual figures together in one breakdown. Each line is plain gross-pay arithmetic, so you can check any amount by hand:

Pay periodHow the calculator works it out
Hourlythe rate you enter
Weeklyhourly rate times hours per week
Biweeklytwo weekly paychecks
Monthlyannual pay divided by 12
Annualhourly rate times hours per week times paid weeks per year

Inputs, ranges and switching direction

  • Direction: hourly rate to annual salary, or annual salary to hourly rate.
  • Amount: the hourly rate or salary you are converting.
  • Hours per week: half-hour steps from 1 to 100, default 40.
  • Paid weeks per year: 1 to 53, default 52, so part-time schedules and unpaid-break years are one edit away.

Switching the direction converts the amount you already entered in place, so the result stays equivalent instead of resetting.

The common-rates reference table

Below the result, a reference table lists common hourly rates from 15 to 50 with their weekly and annual gross pay at a standard 40-hour, 52-week schedule - a quick anchor when you compare a job offer against the number you already have in mind.

Gross pay only

Every figure is gross pay before taxes and deductions - the result says so itself, and the page makes no take-home or tax claims. It gives a general estimate, not tax or financial advice.

Your salary figures stay in your browser

The wage and salary numbers you enter never leave this page. Every calculation runs locally in your browser, with no upload and no account, and nothing you type is stored or sent anywhere - so a rate you are weighing against a job offer stays private to you.
