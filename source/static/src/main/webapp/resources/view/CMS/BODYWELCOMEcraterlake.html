Crater Lake 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Crater Lake as a cross-section of a collapsed volcano: a nearly circular caldera rim, a steep inner wall plunging almost straight into the water, a small cinder-cone island breaking the surface, and two toggleable markers that compare how far you can actually SEE into the lake against how far the floor really lies.

Crater Lake formed about 7,700 years ago when the stratovolcano Mount Mazama erupted violently and its emptied summit collapsed into a caldera. Rain and melting snow are the only water source - no river flows in and none flows out - so almost no sediment ever reaches the lake. That is why Crater Lake is regularly measured among the clearest lake water on Earth, with visibility readings around 44 m (143 ft), even though the true floor lies about 594 m (1,949 ft) below the surface - the deepest of any lake in the USA.

  • Toggle the max-depth marker to see the 594 m drop to the caldera floor
  • Toggle the water-clarity marker to compare it against the roughly 44 m you can actually see through
  • Read elevation, depth, clarity, and island figures in the panel
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

The whole Crater Lake caldera - the rim, the steep inner wall, Wizard Island, and the two depth markers - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to look down into the caldera, and toggle the markers to compare the shallow depth you can see through against the true depth of the basin. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the caldera profile, island shape, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
