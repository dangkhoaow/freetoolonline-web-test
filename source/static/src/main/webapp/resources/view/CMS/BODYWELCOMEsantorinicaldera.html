Santorini Caldera 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-28

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Santorini caldera as a sea-flooded volcanic ring: a near-complete rim of cliffs, tallest on the northwest side where the clifftop towns sit, broken by an open channel on the southwest where the sea flows in, with two dark lava-dome islands rising from the lagoon near the center. Two toggleable markers compare how far the caldera floor lies below the surface against how high the clifftop rim rises above it.

The caldera formed during the Minoan eruption, about 3,600 years ago (around 1600 BCE), one of the largest volcanic eruptions in recorded history. The eruption emptied the volcano's magma chamber and its summit collapsed, letting the sea flood in through the southwest breach to form today's lagoon. Nea Kameni and Palea Kameni, the dark islands near the lagoon's center, are younger lava domes built up by eruptions long after the original collapse - Nea Kameni last erupted in 1950 and its slopes still vent volcanic gas.

  • Toggle the caldera-depth marker to see the roughly 400 m drop to the lagoon floor
  • Toggle the clifftop-height marker to compare it against the roughly 300 m the rim rises above the sea
  • Read caldera size, depth, clifftop height, and Kameni figures in the panel
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

The whole Santorini caldera - the breached rim, the clifftop cliffs, the Kameni islands, and the two depth markers - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to look down into the flooded ring, and toggle the markers to compare the sea-floor depth against the clifftop height. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the caldera profile, island shapes, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
