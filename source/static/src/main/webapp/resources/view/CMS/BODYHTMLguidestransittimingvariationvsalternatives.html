Where the Exoplanet Transit Explorer wins

The Exoplanet Transit Explorer wins when the question is what a transit itself actually looks like and why its depth follows (Rplanet/Rstar) squared - it is the direct, single-planet signal every transit-timing analysis starts from.


Where the TTV Explorer wins

The TTV Explorer wins when the question is how astronomers find planets that transits alone cannot reveal - it is the only page on the site visualizing the timing-wobble detective work behind a planet like Kepler-19c that has never once been seen crossing its own star.


A reasonable rule

As a reasonable rule, use the Exoplanet Transit Explorer first to see what a single transit's brightness dip looks like, then use the TTV Explorer to see how a second planet's gravity leaves its own, indirect signature in that transit's timing.
