Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Amazon the longest river on Earth?

The Nile is generally cited as the longest at about 6,650 km. The Amazon is about 6,400 km, making it the second-longest. However, the Amazon discharges far more water - about one-fifth of all river discharge on Earth.

Why is the discharge plume visible from space?

The Amazon's freshwater plume extends 100+ km into the Atlantic because its enormous discharge (209,000 m3/s) carries tons of silt and sediment. Satellite sensors see the plume as a lighter water color caused by the suspended sediment.

What is the várzea and how high does it flood?

Várzea is the flooded forest along the Amazon and its tributaries. During wet season (May-June), the river rises significantly and the várzea inundates by up to about 40 m above the dry-season baseline - a dramatic seasonal cycle that defines the floodplain ecosystem.

How many species live in the Amazon basin?

The tropical basin is estimated to support about 1 in 10 of all known species on Earth - roughly 10% of global biodiversity - across its 7,050,000 km2 area.
