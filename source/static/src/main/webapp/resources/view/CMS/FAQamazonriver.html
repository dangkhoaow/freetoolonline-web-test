Frequently Asked Questions

How long is the Amazon River?

The Amazon River is approximately 6,400 kilometers (about 4,000 miles) in length, making it one of the longest rivers in the world. Its exact length varies slightly depending on where the source is defined in the Andes Mountains.

What is the Amazon River's discharge volume?

The Amazon River discharges approximately 209,000 cubic meters of water per second into the Atlantic Ocean, accounting for about 20% of all river water flowing into the world's oceans. This enormous discharge is one-fifth of the world's total river discharge.

How many tributaries does the Amazon have?

The Amazon River has over 1,100 tributaries of significant size, with more than 17 major tributaries that contribute substantially to the main channel. The largest tributaries include the Rio Negro (the second-largest river by volume), Rio Madeira, Rio Xingu, and Solimões.

What is the sediment plume visible from space?

The Amazon's sediment plume is the visible brownish coloration where the river's fresh water and sediment meet the Atlantic Ocean. It can extend hundreds of kilometers into the ocean and is visible from satellites due to the massive amount of sediment (clay, silt, and organic matter) the river carries from its drainage basin.

How much of Earth's species live in the Amazon?

An estimated 10% of all known species on Earth live in or around the Amazon River basin. This includes an incredible diversity of fish species (over 1,100 recorded), amphibians, reptiles, mammals, birds, and insects that depend on the river's freshwater ecosystem.
