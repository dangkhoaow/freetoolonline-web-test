Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Crater Lake so deep?

It fills the caldera left when Mount Mazama's summit collapsed after a violent eruption about 7,700 years ago. The floor lies about 594 m (1,949 ft) below the surface - the deepest of any lake in the USA.

Why is the water so clear?

No river flows in or out, so almost no sediment ever reaches the lake - it fills only from rain and snowmelt. Visibility readings around 44 m (143 ft) put it among the clearest lake water on Earth.

What is Wizard Island?

A cinder cone that rises about 230 m (760 ft) above the lake surface near the west side of the caldera - a small volcano that grew inside the larger collapsed one.

How was Crater Lake formed?

The stratovolcano Mount Mazama erupted catastrophically about 7,700 years ago, emptying its magma chamber; the unsupported summit then collapsed inward, and the resulting caldera filled with rain and snowmelt over the following centuries.

Where is Crater Lake?

Inside Crater Lake National Park in southern Oregon, USA.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the caldera profile, island shape, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
