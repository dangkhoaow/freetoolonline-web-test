How to Use the Hubble Tuning Fork 3D Explorer - Step by Step

Last reviewed: 2026-08-13

Open the Hubble Tuning Fork 3D Explorer, press an E0-E7 button or an a/b/c button to morph the specimen, toggle Normal vs Barred, and try the real-galaxy challenge to test what you have learned.


Press the E0-E7 buttons

Press E0 through E7 to watch the specimen squash from a nearly round point cloud to the most flattened shape astronomers actually observe - the facts panel shows the exact ellipticity formula behind each rung.


Press S0, then a, b, or c

Press S0 to see the lenticular transition class - a bulge and a disk with no spiral arms at all - then press a, b, or c to grow spiral arms, watching the central bulge shrink and the arms loosen from tightly-wound to loosely-wound.


Toggle Normal spiral vs Barred spiral

Press "Barred spiral (SB)" to add the central bar and watch the spiral arms spring from the bar's ends instead of the bulge; press "Normal spiral (S)" to remove it. The toggle only changes anything once you are on S0 or a/b/c.


Press "Walk the fork"

Press "Walk the fork" to auto-step through all twelve rungs back and forth hands-free, or press it again to pause and take control with the buttons.


Show irregulars, beyond the fork

Press "Show irregulars (beyond the fork)" to reveal a lumpy shape off to the side - the class Hubble deliberately left out of his diagram entirely.


Try the real-galaxy challenge

Press "Real-galaxy challenge" to get a clue about a real, named galaxy. Set your own guess with the E0-E7/S0/a-b-c buttons and the Normal/Barred toggle, then press "Reveal" to see its real classification and the sourced fact behind it.


Drag to orbit and use fullscreen

Drag to orbit the camera, scroll or pinch to zoom, and use the Fullscreen button to expand the whole wrapper for a clearer view.
