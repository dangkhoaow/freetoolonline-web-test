Carlsbad Caverns 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-28

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Carlsbad Caverns' Big Room as a domed underground chamber: an elliptical limestone void with a rock wall rising to its rim and a curved ceiling closing overhead, forested across the floor with stalagmites, stalactites, and columns built up over hundreds of thousands of years.

The Big Room sits in the Guadalupe Mountains near Carlsbad, New Mexico, and its floor covers about 8.2 acres (roughly 33,000 sq m) - one of the largest known cave chambers in North America - about 230 m (roughly 750 ft) below the surface at its deepest point. Unlike most limestone caves, which are carved by mildly acidic groundwater (carbonic acid), Carlsbad Caverns formed through sulfuric-acid dissolution: hydrogen sulfide rising from the Permian Basin's oil and gas deposits mixed with groundwater and oxidized into sulfuric acid, dissolving the surrounding reef limestone on a much larger scale than typical karst caves. The cave and its park were declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995.

For a cave carved by a river through a chain of wide halls instead of one domed chamber, open the Postojna Cave 3D Explorer, or for the longest known cave system on Earth - a stacked multi-level maze rather than a single Big Room - walk the Mammoth Cave 3D Explorer.

The whole Big Room scene - the domed chamber shape, the dripstone forest, and the Giant Dome marker ring - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to look around the chamber, and toggle the lighting to compare the warm show-cave amber against a cooler simulated headlamp view. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the chamber shape, dripstone placement, and Giant Dome marker are procedural teaching cues, not survey cave geology.
