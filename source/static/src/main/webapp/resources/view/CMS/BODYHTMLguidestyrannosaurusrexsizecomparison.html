Tyrannosaurus rex Size Comparison

Last reviewed: 2026-09-05

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

Tyrannosaurus rex 3D Viewer puts published fossil figures beside a 1.8 m person on screen - length about 12.3 m, hip height about 3.7 m, weight about 8.9 tonnes. This page covers those numbers, the four controls that produce the comparison, and when a phone AR app or a museum serves you better.

How the Tyrannosaurus rex size-vs-human toggle stays honest

The Tyrannosaurus rex model is drawn to a fixed on-screen length so it fits the canvas; the Size vs human control then places a 1.8 m person at the true length ratio to that real ~12.3 m figure. Tap Size vs human under the canvas to show or hide the scale figure - the comparison is proportional, not a decorative sticker.


What a person would see beside a Tyrannosaurus rex

What a person would see is a hip joint towering overhead: at about 3.7 m at the hips, a Tyrannosaurus rex hip joint sits well above a 1.8 m person's head. From snout to tail the animal spans roughly 12.3 m - turn on the person in the viewer and stand the camera beside a hind leg to feel how far the back is above eye level.


The four Tyrannosaurus rex controls under the canvas

Open the Tyrannosaurus rex 3D Viewer and wait for the facts panel to fill. Once the status line offers drag to rotate and scroll or pinch to zoom, orbit the model with the pointer and zoom with the wheel or a two-finger pinch. Four controls sit under the canvas:

  • Fullscreen - expands the wrapper, so the buttons stay visible beside the scene
  • Stop rotation - freezes the auto-spin; the label flips to Auto-rotate while spinning is off
  • Idle motion - adds a subtle hind-leg sway; tap again for Stand still
  • Size vs human - shows the 1.8 m scale figure described above; the label flips to Hide human while it is on

The Tyrannosaurus rex facts panel and part clicks

Beyond the measures in the table above, the Tyrannosaurus rex facts panel carries the diet (carnivore) and the naming history: the first described skeleton came from a 1902 Montana find by Barnum Brown. A short click - not a drag - on the head, tail, or a leg swaps in a note for that part; click empty space to return to the fact sheet.

What these Tyrannosaurus rex numbers are not

The figures are published bone-based estimates, and the skin color in the viewer is an artistic reconstruction - fossils preserve no color or soft tissue. The controls above cover the in-browser viewer only: it never places the model in your room, and it claims no soft-tissue accuracy. The comparison table is a trade-off sheet for the shipped viewer, not a ranking of commercial AR brands.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Tyrannosaurus rex model show its arms and tail?

Yes - the model carries the classic short two-fingered arms and a heavy tail balanced for the animal's stride, alongside the head and legs already covered by the part-click facts.

Which body parts can I click for facts?

Head, body, neck, tail, or a leg - each swaps in a short note tied to that fossil evidence class; click empty space in the viewer to return to the full fact sheet.

Can I download the Tyrannosaurus rex 3D model or its mesh?

No - the viewer is view-only in the browser. There is no downloadable mesh export and no server-side rendering; everything runs client-side once the model has loaded.

Are the part-click notes a full scientific reference?

No - they are short interpretive notes keyed to fossil evidence classes, not a full osteology atlas. For the published measures themselves, use the figures table above.

Does loading the 3D engine slow down the rest of the page?

No - the three.js engine is site-vendored (not pulled from a third-party CDN) and lazy-loads after the page's initial paint, about 0.7 MB the first time, then stays cached in the browser for repeat visits.
