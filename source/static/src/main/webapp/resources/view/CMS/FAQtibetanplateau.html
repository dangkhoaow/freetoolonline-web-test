Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Tibetan Plateau?

The Tibetan Plateau is the world's largest and highest plateau, with an average elevation of approximately 4,500 meters. Known as the "Roof of the World," it spans about 2.5 million square kilometers across Tibet, China, and surrounding regions. The plateau is characterized by high-altitude grasslands, interior basins, and mountains including Mount Everest on its southern edge.

Why is it called the Roof of the World?

The Tibetan Plateau earned this nickname because of its extreme elevation. At an average height of 4,500 meters, it is the highest inhabited plateau on Earth. The only higher feature is the summit of Mount Everest (8,848.86 m), which rises on the plateau's southern margin. This vast high-altitude region dominates the landscape of Central Asia.

What rivers originate on the Tibetan Plateau?

The Tibetan Plateau is the source region for several major Asian rivers. The Yangtze, Yellow River (Huang He), Mekong, Brahmaputra, and Indus Rivers all originate in various parts of the plateau. These rivers are vital water sources for billions of people across Asia, making the plateau a critical freshwater resource despite its semi-arid and arid climate.

How many people live on the Tibetan Plateau?

Approximately 15 million people live on the Tibetan Plateau, making it one of the most sparsely populated large regions in the world with an average density of about 6 people per square kilometer. Most communities are nomadic pastoralists or farmers who have adapted to the harsh high-altitude environment over thousands of years. Major population centers include Lhasa and other cities along the plateau's lower-elevation zones.

What is Mount Kailas and why is it sacred?

Mount Kailas, at approximately 6,638 meters, is one of the world's most sacred mountains in Buddhism, Hinduism, Taoism, and Bon traditions. Located in western Tibet, it remains unclimbed out of respect for its spiritual significance. The mountain is considered the axis of the world in these religious traditions. Many pilgrims circumambulate the mountain as an act of devotion, walking the approximately 53-kilometer route that surrounds it.
