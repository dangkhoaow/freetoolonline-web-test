Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the Tibetan Plateau called the Roof of the World?

It is the world's highest and largest plateau at about 2.5 million sq km with an average elevation of 4,000+ m - nearly the entire area sits above the tree line, higher than the summits of most mountains elsewhere on Earth.

Which rivers originate on the Tibetan Plateau?

Four of Asia's greatest rivers begin here: the Yangtze (Asia's longest river), the Yellow River (China's second-longest), the Mekong, and the Brahmaputra (Yarlung Tsangpo). Together they drain to seven countries and support over a billion people.

Is Mount Everest on the Tibetan Plateau?

Mount Everest is on the southern border of the plateau, where it meets the Himalayan range. The plateau extends far inland to the north, with average elevation still around 4,000 m.

Why is it so cold and dry on the Tibetan Plateau?

The high elevation means thin atmosphere and intense solar radiation but rapid heat loss. The plateau is surrounded by mountain ranges (Himalayas, Kunlun, Hengduan) that block moisture-bearing winds, creating a rain shadow - most areas get less than 500 mm of rain per year, making it a semi-desert.

Do people live on the Tibetan Plateau?

Yes - about 3 million people, mostly Tibetan, Qiang, and Mongolian peoples, live on the plateau despite the harsh climate and thin atmosphere. They have adapted to the environment over thousands of years with livestock herding, barley cultivation, and Buddhist monasteries.
