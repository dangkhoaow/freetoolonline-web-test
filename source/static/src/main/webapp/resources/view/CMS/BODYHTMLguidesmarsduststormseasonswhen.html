When to Use Mars Dust Storm Seasons 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-11

Open the Mars Dust Storm Seasons 3D Explorer when you want to see WHEN and WHY Mars gets global dust storms - the real Ls 180-340 season window, the 2018 storm that ended NASA's Opportunity rover, and how the eccentric orbit drives the season - not just what Mars's surface or thin atmosphere looks like on its own.


Sessions this explorer fits

Students asking why a single dust storm could end a working Mars rover, teachers walking through NASA/JPL's 2018 mission updates, and readers who want to connect an eccentric orbit to a real seasonal weather event by scrubbing through a Mars year instead of reading a mission report first all get a direct answer here.


Sessions this explorer does not fit

Skip this dust-storm season view if what you actually need is Mars's fixed surface relief - the canyons, craters, and Olympus Mons profile that do not change with the season - because that terrain lives on the Mars Terrain 3D Explorer. If your question is instead why Mars has such a thin atmosphere to begin with, over billions of years, that slow escape story is on the Atmosphere Escape: Mars 3D Explorer, not here. And if you only want to compare orbital ellipses - Earth's near-circular path has no comparable dust-storm season - start with the Earth Perihelion and Aphelion 3D Explorer. For a control-by-control walkthrough of scrubbing the Ls season window on this page, follow the step-by-step guide.
