Frequently Asked Questions

Are flags at half-mast today?

On load this page compares today's date against the five recurring US federal half-staff observances and states whether today is one of them.

Which dates does this cover, and when is the next one?

Peace Officers Memorial Day, Memorial Day (half-staff until noon local time, then full staff), Patriot Day, National POW/MIA Recognition Day and Pearl Harbor Remembrance Day. The table on the page lists this year and next year with exact dates, and names the next one and how many days away it is.

Does this track flag orders issued after a death or tragedy?

No. This does NOT track proclamation-driven half-staff orders following a death or tragedy - those are announced case-by-case and cannot be predicted from any fixed rule; the page links the official GSA half-staff notice page for that.

Why are flags at half-mast today?

If today matches one of the five dates this half-mast checker tracks, it is because that date is a recurring US federal observance that returns to half-staff status every year on the same rule, not because of a single news event. Check the disclaimer above for the one case this checker does not cover.

What happens when one of these five dates falls on a weekend?

Pearl Harbor Remembrance Day, December 7, is a concrete case of the weekend rule already noted above: in a year where December 7 lands on a Saturday, this half-mast checker's table shows it observed on Friday, December 6 instead of the calendar date.

Does this account for my time zone?

Yes. The status is computed from your device's local calendar day, midnight to midnight in whatever time zone your device is set to, not a fixed reference time zone. If your device's clock or time zone is set incorrectly, the status and the days-away countdown could be off by a day right around midnight.
