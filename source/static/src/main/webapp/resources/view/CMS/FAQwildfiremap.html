Frequently Asked Questions

Where does the fire data come from?

The page loads the current incident list from NIFC WFIGS - the National Interagency Fire Center's open reporting system that US federal and state fire agencies file into. The feed is public and keyless, and it loads directly in your browser with no account or app install.

How current is the map?

Incidents appear on the agencies' reporting cadence, so a brand-new fire can take hours to show up and acreage figures update as crews re-survey. Hit Refresh to pull the latest list. This page is for situational awareness - it is not an emergency-alert or evacuation source; always follow local officials.

Does it show fires outside the US?

No. WFIGS is the US interagency system, so only US incidents appear - including Alaska and Hawaii. Fires in other countries are not in this feed.

What does "percent contained" mean?

It measures how much of the fire's edge has a completed control line around it. 100 percent contained means fully surrounded - not extinguished; hot spots can keep burning inside the line for weeks. The page's built-in explainer also decodes the NWCG size classes A through G.

What are the grey dots (prescribed burns)?

Prescribed burns are planned, controlled fires that land managers set on purpose to reduce fuel. They are hidden by default so the map shows wildfires only; one checkbox adds them to the same view, drawn in grey so they never look like emergencies.

Does the page track my location?

Only if you press the distance button, and only then does the browser ask for permission. Your location is used once, in this tab, to sort the list nearest-first with a distance column in km and miles. It is never stored and never sent anywhere - and the map works fully without it.

Why is there no street or satellite background?

The map is a coordinate plot on a longitude and latitude grid, on purpose: no basemap tiles means nothing external loads, the page stays fast, and hovering a dot still gives you the fire's name, county, size, and containment. For "how far from me", use the distance sort instead of eyeballing roads.
