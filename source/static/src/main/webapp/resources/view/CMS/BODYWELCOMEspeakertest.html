Speaker & Audio Channel Test

Last reviewed: 2026-09-01

Test left and right speaker channels with a short in-browser tone. Click Play Left, Play Right, or Play Both; adjust the frequency, then Stop. Everything runs in the browser - no microphone, no upload, and no account.


How Speaker & Audio Channel Test compares to the other device-test tools

Speaker & Audio Channel Test is one of several device checks on this site that run with nothing installed, but only two of them ask for a browser permission first - the two that read a live stream FROM your device rather than only playing or displaying something TO you. Microphone Test and Camera Test fall into the first group; Speaker & Audio Channel Test and LCD (Screen) Test fall into the second, so neither ever shows a permission prompt.

If sound does not play when you expect it to, the fix depends on which category the tool is in: a no-permission tool like this one only needs audio playback allowed at the tab or system level, while a permission tool needs camera or microphone access re-granted in your browser's site settings.


Why the tone fades out on its own, and what happens if you switch channels

Every tone Speaker & Audio Channel Test plays stops on its own after about 2.5 seconds, even if you never click Stop. The volume does not hold steady and then cut off - it ramps down smoothly from the moment you click Play Left, Play Right, or Play Both, reaching silence right around the 2.5-second mark, so there is no click or pop at the end. That auto-stop exists so a tone can never get stuck playing if you switch tabs or forget to click Stop.

You also do not have to click Stop before testing a different channel. Clicking Play Right while Play Left is still sounding stops the left tone first, then starts the right one - the two channels never overlap or play together by accident. Click Stop only when you want silence immediately instead of waiting out the fade.
