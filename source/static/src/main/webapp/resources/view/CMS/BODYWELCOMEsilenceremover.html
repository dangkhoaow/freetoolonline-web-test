Remove Silence from Audio - Auto Dead-Air Cutter, On-Device

Last reviewed: 2026-08-24

Cut the silent gaps - long pauses, dead air, gaps between takes - out of a voice recording automatically, without the audio ever leaving your browser. A small on-device voice detector listens through the file, keeps every spoken part, and removes the gaps between them. There is no upload, no account, and no server: the recording stays on your device from the moment you pick it to the moment you download the result.

Where this saves real time. A raw podcast take where you paused to check notes; a lecture or meeting recording with long stretches of nobody talking; a voice memo you started and then left running. Cutting that dead air by hand in a waveform editor means finding every gap yourself. Here you pick the file, choose how long a pause has to be before it counts as dead air - 0.3, 0.5, or 1 second - and press one button.

How the cut is decided, honestly. The detector is a small neural voice-activity model (about 0.1 MB - the size is shown before the one-time download, and it is cached for your next visit). It scores every 10 ms of audio for the probability that someone is speaking; stretches below the line that run longer than your chosen minimum are removed. Speech is always kept with 0.15 s of breathing room on each side, and pauses shorter than your minimum are left exactly where they were, so the result still sounds like a person talking rather than a chopped edit. The kept audio itself is your original recording, untouched: the detector only chooses where to cut - it never changes the sound in between.

Check before you commit. The result appears as two players side by side - the original and the trimmed version - with a plain summary of how many seconds of dead air were removed across how many gaps. Listen first, then download. If the cut feels too tight, pick a longer minimum pause and run it again; each pass starts from your original file.

No download at all? The Basic cut button does the same job by loudness alone: frames quieter than the recording's own noise floor are treated as silence. It is instant and needs zero network, but it can clip breathy or very quiet speech that the voice detector would keep - the button labels say which is which, so you can choose.

Know its limits. The detector is tuned for speech. On music it will treat quiet instrumental passages as silence and cut them - for music editing, use the manual Audio Trimmer, where you pick the cut points yourself. The output is always a mono 48 kHz WAV file, whatever the input format was. And if your recording also has constant hiss or hum behind the voice, run it through the Audio Denoiser - the same on-device engine family, pointed at noise instead of pauses - before or after cutting.
