Against mental math

Multiplying a cost by 1.40 for a 40% markup is manageable; converting that same deal into a margin percentage in your head is not, because the base changes from cost to price. Here both percentages compute together, and the explainer line states outright that a 100% markup is a 50% margin - the exact confusion mental math tends to produce.


Against a phone calculator app

A plain calculator app gets you the selling price in one multiplication, but getting the margin back out means a second calculation - profit divided by price, not by cost - that most people skip or get backwards. This tool runs both at once and labels which is which.


Against a spreadsheet

A spreadsheet reaches the same arithmetic once the markup and margin formulas are written, and it wins when you are pricing a whole catalog of items at once and want the rows saved. For a single price-check tonight, the calculator is faster, needs nothing installed, and cannot slip a cell reference - at the cost that nothing here is saved, so copy the number out before closing the tab.


Against point-of-sale pricing software

Retail and inventory systems often have a markup or margin field built into the item record, and that wins once a business is already running on one - no second tool needed for prices already in the system. This calculator fits the gap before that: a quick quote, a one-off cost from a new supplier, or checking a price idea before it goes anywhere near the inventory system, with no account or setup.
