Frequently Asked Questions

What does Holiday Countdown & Date Lookup do?

Look up the next public holiday for your country and see a live countdown to it.

When should I reach for holiday countdown & date lookup?

Holiday dates are computed fresh for any year from durable calendar rules (fixed dates, nth-weekday-of-month, and the Easter algorithm) - never a hard-coded list that goes stale.

What complementary tools work well alongside holiday countdown & date lookup?

Share a specific holiday's countdown via the Copy Link button, which encodes the country and holiday in the URL.

Does the tool detect my country automatically?

No. Holiday Countdown & Date Lookup defaults to the United States calendar on first load. Pick a different country from the dropdown, or open a shared link that already has a country encoded in it, to see that country's holidays instead.

What happens when the live countdown reaches the holiday?

The countdown label switches to "It's today!" and stops updating once the target date arrives. To start a fresh countdown to a different holiday after that, reload the page or pick another entry from the holiday dropdown - the tool does not automatically advance to the next holiday on its own.

How many holidays does each country's calendar cover?

Holiday Countdown & Date Lookup covers four countries' national-level calendars: the United States (11 holidays), Canada (10), the United Kingdom (8), and Australia (7). Each calendar mixes fixed-date holidays with floating ones computed from a rule, and the mix differs by country.

United Kingdom83 (New Year's Day, Christmas Day, Boxing Day)5 (Good Friday, Easter Monday, Early May Bank Holiday, Spring Bank Holiday, Summer Bank Holiday)

The floating holidays use one of two rules: an nth-weekday-of-month rule (Memorial Day is the last Monday in May; Thanksgiving in the US is the fourth Thursday in November) or the Easter Sunday date, which the tool derives with the Meeus/Jones/Butcher algorithm and then offsets (Good Friday is two days before Easter Sunday; Easter Monday is one day after).

What happens when a fixed-date holiday falls on a weekend?

The full-year table marks a fixed-date holiday as observed on the nearest weekday when it lands on a weekend: a holiday that falls on Saturday is annotated "observed Fri" with the prior day's date, and one that falls on Sunday is annotated "observed Mon" with the following day's date. This note only applies to the fixed-date holidays in the table above (New Year's Day, Independence Day, Christmas Day, and the rest of that column); the nth-weekday and Easter-based holidays such as Labor Day, Thanksgiving, or Good Friday already fall on a weekday by definition and never carry the note.

For example, Independence Day (July 4) falls on a Sunday in 2027, so the United States table for that year annotates it as observed the following Monday (Jul 5, 2027) next to the true July 4 date. The annotation is informational only - the holiday date used for the countdown and the shareable link never changes, only the observed-day note shown next to it in the full-year table.
