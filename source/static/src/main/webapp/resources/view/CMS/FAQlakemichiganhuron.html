Frequently Asked Questions

Are Lake Michigan and Lake Huron really the same lake?

Hydrologically, yes. Both basins sit at the exact same water-surface elevation, about 176 m above sea level, and are connected through the Straits of Mackinac - the same water flows freely between them. They carry two separate names by long-standing convention, not because they are separate water bodies.

How wide and deep are the Straits of Mackinac?

The connecting strait is about 5.6 km wide at its narrowest point and only about 50 m deep - far shallower than either main basin, which is why it reads as a narrow doorway rather than a third deep basin in this explorer.

Which is bigger, Lake Michigan or Lake Huron?

By surface area, Lake Huron is slightly larger at about 59,590 sq km versus Michigan's about 57,750 sq km. By depth, Michigan is deeper, reaching about 281 m versus Huron's about 229 m.

Is Lake Michigan-Huron the largest lake in the world?

Combined, the two basins cover about 117,340 sq km, making them the largest connected fresh water body on Earth by surface area - larger than Lake Superior. Superior is still deeper and holds more total water volume, so it remains the largest Great Lake by volume.

What separates the two lakes on a map?

Michigan's Lower Peninsula sits between them as dry land for most of their length, narrowing to nothing at the northern tip where the Straits of Mackinac open the connection.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the basin profile, connecting strait, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
