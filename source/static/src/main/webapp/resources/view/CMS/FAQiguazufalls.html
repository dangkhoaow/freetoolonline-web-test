Frequently Asked Questions

How tall is Iguazu Falls?

The tallest single drop is about 82 m, at the Devil's Throat. Most of the roughly 275 cascades across the 2.7 km crescent are shorter than that.

What is the Devil's Throat?

The Devil's Throat (Garganta do Diablo) is a U-shaped chasm about 150 m wide and 700 m long near the center of the crescent, where water plunges from three sides into a single deep basin - the tallest and highest-volume part of the falls.

Is Iguazu Falls in Argentina or Brazil?

Both. The falls straddle the border, with most of the individual cascades on the Argentina side (Iguazu National Park) and a wide panoramic view from the Brazil side (Iguacu National Park).

Is Iguazu Falls bigger than Niagara Falls?

Iguazu is roughly twice as wide as Niagara Falls and its tallest drop is taller, though Niagara moves more water on average - the two are shaped very differently, a single broad drop at Niagara against a wide crescent of many separate cascades at Iguazu.

Does the flow rate change with the season?

Yes, substantially. Average flow is about 1,746 cubic m per second, but it can drop to a few hundred in a dry spell and exceed 12,000 during flood peaks, when many smaller cascades merge into one continuous wall of water.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the cliff profile, crescent shape, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
