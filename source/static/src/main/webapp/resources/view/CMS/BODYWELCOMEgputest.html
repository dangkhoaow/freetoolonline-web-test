See what your browser's graphics stack can do, then push it with an escalating benchmark - an interactive 3D scene you can orbit, zoom, and view fullscreen, with no account and no upload.

What the capability report shows

  • WebGL 1 and 2 availability, plus the renderer and vendor your browser reports - or a "masked by this browser for privacy" note when it hides them.
  • Maximum texture size, render-buffer size, and viewport dimensions, plus the MSAA sample count on WebGL 2.
  • WebGPU presence (feature-detect only), device pixel ratio, screen resolution, reported CPU threads, and device memory.

How the benchmark works

The score reflects sustained geometry throughput - tiers advance while the median holds 55 FPS, and frame rates above 60 count as 60 so high-refresh screens stay roughly comparable. The last 20 runs persist on your device, so you can compare a driver or browser change across sessions.

Inside the escalating benchmark - how the nine tiers scale in depth

The benchmark climbs through nine tiers, doubling the instance count at each step from 1,000 icosahedrons up to 256,000, all drawn from a single InstancedMesh so the number reflects raw geometry throughput rather than draw-call overhead. Every tier opens with a one-second warmup that is thrown away, then three seconds of measured frames whose median frame time sets that tier's FPS; the run advances to the next tier only while the median holds at 55 FPS or better and stops at the first tier that falls below. Frame rates above 60 are counted as 60 when scoring, and the score sums instances times that capped FPS across completed tiers and divides by 1,000, so a 120 Hz display cannot inflate the total past an ordinary 60 Hz screen. A per-tier results table records what each step reached, and a rating line names the highest tier your device held at 45 FPS or more. You can end a run early with the Cancel button, and the test also aborts itself if you switch to another tab or the browser loses its WebGL context, since throttled background frames would otherwise corrupt the median.

What the score does not measure

It is honest about limits - an indicative browser benchmark, not a lab-grade suite; device heat, battery mode, and other tabs shift the numbers, so compare scores only between runs on the same device and browser. It does not measure video memory, compute or ML throughput, thermals, or power draw.
