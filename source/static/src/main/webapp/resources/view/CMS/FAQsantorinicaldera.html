Frequently Asked Questions

How did the Santorini caldera form?

The Minoan eruption, about 3,600 years ago (c. 1600 BCE), emptied the volcano's magma chamber; the unsupported summit collapsed and the sea flooded in through a breach on the southwest, forming today's lagoon.

How deep is the caldera?

The floor of the flooded lagoon lies about 400 m (1,300 ft) below sea level at its deepest.

Why are the clifftop towns so high above the water?

The caldera rim on the northwest side, where towns like Fira and Imerovigli sit, rises about 300 m (984 ft) above the sea - the steep wall left by the collapse.

What are the Kameni islands?

Nea Kameni and Palea Kameni are younger lava-dome islands that grew inside the flooded caldera long after the original collapse. Nea Kameni last erupted in 1950 and still vents volcanic gas.

Can ships sail into the caldera?

Yes. The caldera rim is breached on the southwest side, leaving an open channel that lets ships sail directly into the lagoon.

Where is Santorini?

In the Aegean Sea, part of Greece's Cyclades island group.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the caldera profile, island shapes, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
