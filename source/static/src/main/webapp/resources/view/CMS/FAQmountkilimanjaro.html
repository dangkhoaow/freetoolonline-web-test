Frequently Asked Questions

Is Mount Kilimanjaro an active volcano?

Mount Kilimanjaro is a dormant volcano, not active. It last erupted about 360 years ago. The mountain is considered geologically young and could potentially erupt again in the future, but there is no current volcanic activity.

Why does Kilimanjaro have snow if it's on the equator?

Mount Kilimanjaro has permanent snow and ice above about 4,000 m because temperature decreases with altitude - roughly 6.5 degrees Celsius per 1,000 m. Even though the mountain sits near the equator, the summit at 5,895 m reaches elevations where temperatures stay below freezing year-round. However, climate change has reduced the ice coverage by about 85% since 1912.

How many people climb Mount Kilimanjaro each year?

About 50,000 people attempt to climb Mount Kilimanjaro annually. Of these, roughly 65-70% reach the summit. The most popular route is the Marangu Route (about 5-6 days), though several other routes exist. Most climbers are not mountaineers but instead tourists seeking a high-altitude experience.

What is the highest point on Mount Kilimanjaro?

The highest point on Mount Kilimanjaro is Uhuru Peak, at 5,895 metres. Uhuru means "freedom" in Swahili. It is the highest mountain in Africa and the highest free-standing mountain in the world - meaning it is not part of a mountain range but rises independently from the surrounding plateau.

How much of Kilimanjaro's ice has melted?

Mount Kilimanjaro's ice coverage has declined dramatically over the past century. In 1912, the ice cap covered about 11.7 square kilometres. Today, it covers only about 2 square kilometres - a loss of roughly 85%. Scientists attribute this retreat primarily to climate change and the warming of the East African region.
