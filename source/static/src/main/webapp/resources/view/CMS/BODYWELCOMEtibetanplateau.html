The Tibetan Plateau is the world's highest and largest plateau, known as the "Roof of the World." Averaging 4,500 meters in elevation, it spans approximately 2.5 million square kilometers across China, Tibet, and surrounding regions. This vast highland hosts the sources of major Asian rivers, including the Yangtze, Yellow River, and Mekong, while supporting about 15 million people in sparse nomadic and agricultural communities.

Explore the plateau's distinctive features: layered elevation zones that step down from interior highlands to surrounding basins, sacred peaks including Mount Kailas and Mount Everest rising on the southern edge, and the sparse grassland terrain that characterizes this high-altitude steppe.
