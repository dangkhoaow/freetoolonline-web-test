Tibetan Plateau 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-30

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Tibetan Plateau from above and in cross-section: the world's highest and largest plateau at an average elevation of 4,000 m, with sparse alpine vegetation and snow-capped peaks where four of Asia's greatest rivers have their sources - the Yangtze, Yellow, Mekong, and Brahmaputra.

The Tibetan Plateau is sometimes called the "Roof of the World" - at about 2.5 million square kilometres, it covers an area roughly the size of Western Europe. The plateau is very cold and dry, with average temperatures around -5 degrees Celsius and less than 500 mm of annual rainfall in most areas. The thin atmosphere, intense solar radiation, and permafrost create an extreme environment that supports alpine tundra vegetation and sparse grasslands. Despite the harsh climate, Tibetan peoples have inhabited the plateau for thousands of years, and it remains culturally and politically significant to the region.

  • Toggle the vegetation to switch between grassland and bare scree - showing the sparse cover of the high-altitude plateau
  • Toggle snow and glaciers to see the ice patches on the highest peaks
  • Look for river valleys radiating outward from the central highlands - these are the headwaters of Asia's greatest rivers
  • Switch the View button to real measured terrain for the Mount Kailash massif - the plateau's most globally recognized peak
  • Read elevation, area, and climate figures in the panel
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom

The Tibetan Plateau sits on top of the world - for the actual highest point, explore the Mount Everest 3D Explorer, or visit Africa's highest peak with the Mount Kilimanjaro 3D Explorer.

Because the 2.5-million-square-kilometre plateau itself cannot be reduced to a single window of real elevation data, this explorer's View button switches to the actual measured terrain of the Mount Kailash massif - a peak sacred to Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, and Bon practitioners that rises from the Barkha plain near the plateau's southwest edge. That view is built from real terrain elevation data (Mapzen/AWS Open Data Terrarium tiles, sourced from NASA/USGS DEMs) and Copernicus Sentinel-2 satellite imagery, at true 1:1 scale with no vertical exaggeration.

Everything renders on your device with WebGL. The 3D engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is cached.

This is an educational approximation - the plateau profile, elevation bands, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
