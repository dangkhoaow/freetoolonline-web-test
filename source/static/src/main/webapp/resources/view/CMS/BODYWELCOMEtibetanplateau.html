Tibetan Plateau 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-29 | By: Places-3D Discovery Loop

The Tibetan Plateau is the highest plateau in the world by average elevation, reaching about 4,000 meters above sea level across an area larger than Western Europe. Rising in a series of step-like ridges from the surrounding lowlands, the plateau is home to vast sparse grasslands, internal salt lakes, and river valleys that originate here before flowing across the continent. This 3D explorer displays the plateau's signature relief - sparse vegetation tufts scattered across a high-altitude tundra surface, with the five major Asian river systems beginning at the plateau's core and flowing outward toward the Indian Ocean and Pacific Ocean basins.

The scene shows the plateau's characteristic features: elevation color banding (from low brown to high white peaks), internal lakes represented as water surfaces, and river canyon systems cutting through the terrain. Toggle the elevation bands to see the continuous color gradient, and toggle the river headwaters to highlight the origins of the Yangtze, Yellow, Mekong, Salween, and Brahmaputra rivers. This is an educational relief approximation - the terrain profile, elevation bands, and river paths are schematic teaching visualizations, not survey data.

Areaabout 2.5 million km2 - the largest plateau in the world by area
Average elevationabout 4,000 m (range 2,000-6,000+ m) - the highest plateau on Earth
Geographic rangespans 11 countries across Central and South Asia - about 3,000 km east-west, 1,500 km north-south
Highest pointsMount Everest (8,849 m) at the southern edge; interior peaks: Tanggula Range (~5,000 m), Nyenchen Tanglha (~7,100 m)
Climatesemi-arid, cold - annual mean -5 to 0 degrees C in the interior; less than 500 mm rain per year
Populationabout 2-3 million people, mostly Tibetan - sparse settlements due to extreme altitude and climate
River systemsorigins of five major rivers: Yangtze (6,300 km total), Yellow (5,500 km), Mekong (4,909 km), Salween (3,200 km), Brahmaputra (3,000 km)
Major lakesQinghai Lake, Namtso, Yamdrok Tso, Pangong Tso - many lakes are internal basins with no outlet
Unique featuresUNESCO World Heritage recognition as part of the Xizang plateau system; traditional home of Tibetan Buddhist monasteries and nomadic herding communities

Educational approximation: This 3D visualization uses schematic terrain, height banding, and river paths as teaching cues. The displayed relief, lake positions, and elevation color bands are simplified models of the real terrain for educational clarity, not survey-grade geographic data.
