Frequently Asked Questions

Why is it called the Roof of the World?

The Tibetan Plateau averages over 5,000 meters in elevation, making it the highest major plateau on Earth. It is named the Roof of the World because it sits at the apex of the Asian continent and towers above surrounding regions.

What countries does the Tibetan Plateau span?

The plateau spans across multiple countries and regions, including China (particularly Tibet autonomous region and Qinghai province), Bhutan, Nepal, and India. It is a transnational geographic feature of immense scale.

How do people survive at 5,000+ meters altitude?

The atmosphere at 5,000 meters contains only about 40-50% of the oxygen available at sea level. Indigenous populations like Tibetans have adapted over many generations with genetic and physiological changes that help them thrive in thin air. Modern visitors experience altitude sickness initially and require time to acclimatize.

What rivers start on the Tibetan Plateau?

The plateau is the source region for major Asian rivers including the Yangtze River (China's longest), the Yellow River (a historically vital water source), the Brahmaputra (which flows through India), the Salween, and the Mekong River (which flows through Southeast Asia). These rivers collectively serve over a billion people.

Is the Tibetan Plateau growing or shrinking?

The plateau continues to rise due to ongoing geological uplift from tectonic plate collision between the Indian and Eurasian plates - a process that began about 50 million years ago and continues today at rates of about 5 mm per year in some areas.
