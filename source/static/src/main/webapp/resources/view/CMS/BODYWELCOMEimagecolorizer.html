Colorize Black and White Photos - AI, On-Device

Last reviewed: 2026-08-27

This page adds color to a black-and-white photo using an AI model that runs on your own device - the picture never leaves your browser. Choose a photo, click the colorize button, and in roughly 15 to 60 seconds you get a full-color version you can compare against the original and download as a PNG.

What actually happens to your photo

The model looks at the brightness detail of your photo and predicts a color layer for it. Your photo's original full-resolution sharpness is kept: only the colors are new, blended back into the picture at its native size. Nothing is uploaded at any point - the one network request this tool ever makes is downloading the colorization model itself, about 130 MB, once, after you click the button. Your browser caches the model, so the next photo and the next visit skip the download entirely.

What to expect from the colors

Predicted colors are a plausible guess, not a historical record. The model has seen enough photos to make skin tones, skies, grass, and wood look believable, but it cannot know that a jacket was green or a car was red - it picks the most likely option. The "Show original" toggle flips between the two versions so you can judge the result before saving. If a photo comes out muted, running it again will not change the answer; the model is deterministic for a given picture.

If you do not want a 130 MB download

The "Warm tint" button gives an instant result with zero download and zero network: a fixed sepia-style tone mapped over the photo. It is honestly a tint - every picture gets the same warm palette - while the AI button predicts colors specific to the content of your photo. The tint path is there so a quick job, a metered connection, or an older machine still gets something useful.

Old photo workflow: repair first, then colorize

Colorization works best on a clean scan. If your original is faded, low-resolution, or damaged, run it through the photo restoration tool first - it sharpens and upscales on-device the same private way - and then colorize the restored version here. Doing it in that order keeps the color prediction from amplifying scratches and grain.

Practical limits

This tool processes photos, not video. Very large photos are scaled to 4000 pixels on the long side before processing, and the color layer itself is predicted at one fixed working resolution - on very large images, fine color boundaries can bleed slightly at edges even though the detail stays sharp. Processing time depends on your device; a typical laptop lands in the 15 to 60 second range per photo.
