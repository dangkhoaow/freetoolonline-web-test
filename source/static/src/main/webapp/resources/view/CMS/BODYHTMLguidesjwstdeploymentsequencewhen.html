When to Use the JWST Deployment Sequence 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-12

Open the JWST Deployment Sequence 3D Explorer when you want a fast, real-dates answer to why the James Webb Space Telescope had to fold up for launch, and exactly how its tower, sunshield, and mirror unfolded once in space.


Sessions this explorer fits

Students and teachers explaining why a 6.5 m mirror and a 21x14 m sunshield could not fit inside a rocket fairing already assembled, readers who remember the January 2022 deployment news and want the real order of events, and anyone curious how a spacecraft can have 178 release mechanisms that all had to work all get a direct answer here.


Sessions this explorer does not fit

If your question is about what the James Webb Space Telescope does AFTER the roughly 30-day unfolding finished - cooling down, aligning its 18 mirror segments, and beginning science - this deployment explorer is the wrong stop. That later chapter lives on the JWST L2 Halo Orbit 3D Explorer, which picks up once JWST is fully deployed and settling into its orbit around the second Sun-Earth Lagrange point about 1.5 million km out. To learn the on-screen controls that step through each fold, tower extension, and sunshield tensioning stage, open the step-by-step guide.
