Scrub the slider to watch the Moon's orbit widen and Earth's day lengthen: laser ranging off mirrors the Apollo astronauts left on the Moon shows it receding about 3.8 cm per year (NASA/JPL), a slow drift with a measurable knock-on effect on how long a day lasts.

Time (billion years ago): Today

Drag the 3D view to rotate, scroll to zoom. Use the time slider to move forward or backward 1 billion years and see the Moon's orbit expand as it recedes from Earth.

Published figures: mean Earth-Moon distance today is 384,400 km; the day-length trend is about 1.7 milliseconds per century, an average drawn from paleontological growth-ring records. The slider projects those measured rates forward and backward across geologic time.
