When to Use the Geomagnetic Pole Wander 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-15

Open the Geomagnetic Pole Wander 3D Explorer when you want a hands-on answer to how far Earth's magnetic north pole has actually moved since 1831, and why news stories keep saying it is heading toward Siberia.


Sessions this explorer fits

Students and teachers introducing how the geomagnetic field changes over time, readers who saw a headline about the pole "moving toward Russia" and want the real recorded positions behind it, anyone curious how much faster the pole drifts today than it did a century ago, and readers who want the 1831 discovery story and the most recent NOAA figures side by side all get a direct answer here.


Sessions this explorer does not fit

Sessions this page does not fit are studying the magnetic field's present-day 3D shape, tilt, and strength, which is on Earth's Magnetosphere 3D Explorer, or looking up the storm-intensity scale the field's disturbances are measured on, which is on the Kp-Index Geomagnetic Storm Scale page - neither of those tracks the pole's own historical position.
