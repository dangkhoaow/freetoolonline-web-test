Frequently Asked Questions

How deep is the Caribbean Sea?

Average depth is about 2,200 m, but the Cayman Trough between the Cayman Islands and Jamaica plunges to about 7,686 m - the deepest point in the Caribbean Sea, much deeper than the surrounding basin floor.

What islands border the Caribbean Sea?

The Greater Antilles (Cuba, Hispaniola, Jamaica, Puerto Rico) frame the north edge, and the curving Lesser Antilles run down the east side, together with Central America to the west and South America to the south.

What is the Cayman Trough?

It is a narrow, unusually deep notch in the northwest Caribbean floor, reaching about 7,686 m - far deeper than the sea's average depth of about 2,200 m.

Does the Caribbean Sea have coral reefs?

Yes - it is home to the Belize Barrier Reef, part of the Mesoamerican Reef and the second-largest barrier reef system in the world at about 300 km long.

Why is the Caribbean known for hurricanes?

It sits in the Atlantic hurricane belt, and its warm surface waters help feed storms during the season, which runs roughly June through November.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the basin profile, island-arc footprint, and vertical scale (including the trough) are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
