Milford Sound - New Zealand's most famous fjord

Milford Sound is a drowned fjord on New Zealand's southwest coast, carved by glaciers during the last Ice Age. The valley was flooded by rising seas, creating one of the world's most dramatic coastal landscapes. Steep cliff walls rise 1,200+ meters directly from water so deep it rivals inland lakes.

The fjord's U-shaped profile is the classic signature of glacial carving. Unlike river valleys, which form narrow V-shaped canyons, glaciers move slowly and widely, sculpting broad, rounded valleys with nearly vertical walls. When such a valley floods, it becomes a fjord.

Milford Sound's high annual rainfall - about 200 days per year - keeps its cliff faces alive with waterfalls. After heavy rain, dozens cascade into the sea. The landscape is ever-changing with the weather: misty in rain, brilliant in rare sunshine, dramatic under racing clouds.

Length about 15 km
Width 1-2 km at the entrance, narrower inland
Depth about 291 meters - deep for any fjord
Mitre Peak 1,695 meters (5,560 feet) - the iconic landmark rising near the entrance
Annual rainfall about 200 rainy days - creating the famous waterfalls

The fjord sits within Fiordland National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage area. It is one of the most visited natural sites in New Zealand, drawing travelers to cruise its waters and hike its valley trails.
