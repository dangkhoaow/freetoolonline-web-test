Which compressor fits your file, and why zipping photos rarely helps

The quickest way to choose is to match your input to a row in the decision table rather than reaching for one universal shrink button. A folder of mixed files belongs in the ZIP tool, but loose JPG or PNG photos shrink far more when you run them through the image compressor instead of zipping them - a ZIP archive of already-compressed photos barely changes the total size, which is why the green answer panel steers photos away from the folder route. iPhone shots saved as HEIC are the odd case: convert them to JPG first through the HEIC converter, because that single re-encode usually does more for the size than any archive step. When you have several images or several PDFs headed to one recipient, the images-to-PDF and merge-PDF rows bundle them into one attachment instead of a pile of separate files. If a single file is simply too large for any of these to help, the last row of the table sends you to a transfer link through Google Drive, Dropbox, or WeTransfer rather than an email attachment.
