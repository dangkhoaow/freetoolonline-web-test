Frequently Asked Questions

How big is Grand Prismatic Spring?

About 110 m (370 ft) across, and about 50 m (160 ft) deep at its deepest point - the third-largest hot spring in the world.

What causes the rainbow colors?

Rings of heat-loving microbes (thermophiles), mostly pigmented cyanobacteria, that grow in different temperature zones around the pool. Each zone's dominant microbial community produces a different color band.

Why is the center blue instead of colored?

The center runs close to 70 C, too hot for most of the pigmented microbes that color the outer rings. What is left is just clear, deep water scattering light blue.

Do the colors really change with the season?

Yes. The National Park Service notes the mats run more orange in summer and greener in winter, as the balance of pigments in the microbial communities shifts.

Where is Grand Prismatic Spring?

At Midway Geyser Basin in Yellowstone National Park, Wyoming, USA. It was named and first documented by the 1871 Hayden Geological Survey.

Is this a scientific thermal model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the terrace slope, channel placement, and color-ring proportions are schematic teaching cues, not a survey-grade thermal map.
