Dead Sea 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Dead Sea basin in cross-section: the hypersaline lake about 430 m below sea level, the salt terraces on its shore, and a translucent plane marking sea level so the depth of the depression is visible at a glance.

The Dead Sea is a terminal lake between Jordan and Israel / the West Bank: the Jordan River flows in and nothing flows out, so evaporation concentrates the salt to about 34% - roughly nine to ten times typical ocean water. The brine is so dense (about 1.24 kg/L) that swimmers float without effort, and the surface keeps dropping about a metre per year as inflow shrinks.

  • Toggle the sea-level plane to compare the lake surface with 0 m elevation
  • Toggle the salt crust to see the shoreline terraces
  • Read elevation, salinity, density, and depth figures in the panel
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

The whole Dead Sea basin - the banded terrain relief, the salt terraces, and the translucent sea-level plane that shows how far the surface sits below 0 m - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to swing around the depression, and zoom in to read where the shoreline drops toward the lowest land on Earth. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the basin profile, terraces, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
