Frequently Asked Questions

What does the Local Bubble Interstellar Cavity 3D Explorer show?

The Local Bubble - a real, roughly 1,000-light-year-wide cavity in space that surrounds the Sun today, carved out by about 15 supernovae over the last 14 million years. Scrub the timeline or press Play to watch it grow, and click the region markers or the Sun to read the real published figures in the facts panel.

Is the Sun always inside the Local Bubble?

No. The Sun was more than 1,000 light-years away from the bubble's birthplace when the first supernovae went off about 14 million years ago, and its path through the galaxy only carried it into the bubble about 5 million years ago. The timeline's status line marks the moment it crosses in.

Which star-forming regions sit on the Local Bubble's surface?

Seven, per the published research (Zucker et al. 2022, Nature): Ophiuchus, Lupus, Pipe, Chamaeleon, and Musca (all part of the Scorpius-Centaurus association), plus Corona Australis and the Taurus molecular cloud. Click any of the seven blue markers on the scene for its published distance and role.

Are Perseus and Orion on the Local Bubble too?

No - despite being two of the most famous nearby star-forming regions, neither sits on this bubble's surface. Perseus sits on the far side of a separate, related structure (the Perseus-Taurus Shell), with Taurus on its near side. Orion sits at the intersection of two different superbubbles entirely (the Orion Shell and the larger Orion-Eridanus superbubble). Press "Show Perseus & Orion" to see both marked and read the sourced explanation.

How dense is the gas inside the Local Bubble?

Very thin. Published estimates for the hot interior gas range from about 0.001 to 0.07 neutral-hydrogen atoms per cubic centimeter depending on the source and which gas phase is measured - all sources agree it is at least about 10 times thinner than the Milky Way's typical interstellar gas, and roughly 1,000,000 K.

Is this a physics simulation of the real bubble?

No. The bubble's growth curve, the Sun's straight-line entry path, and the supernova counter are illustrative animations paced to match the real cited totals (the 14-million-year formation window, about 15 supernovae, 5 million years since the Sun's entry) - not a precise dynamical simulation or a chronology of individually dated events. The figures in the facts panel and the table on the main page are the real published values.
