How to Use the Kreutz Sungrazing Comets 3D Explorer - Step by Step

Last reviewed: 2026-08-14

Open the Kreutz Sungrazing Comets 3D Explorer, pick a real historical comet or the typical unnamed fragment, sweep it toward the Sun with the slider or "Play sweep", and watch what happens at perihelion.


Pick a comet preset

Press "Typical family member", "Great Comet of 1843", "Great Comet of 1882", or "Ikeya-Seki (1965)" to load that comet's real perihelion distance and facts into the scene and the facts panel.


Sweep or play toward perihelion

Drag the "Sweep" slider to jump to any point on the orbit, or press "Play sweep" to move automatically from far out, through perihelion, and back - watch the ion (blue) and dust (yellow) tails lengthen as the nucleus nears the Sun.


Watch what happens at perihelion

For the typical family member, the nucleus and tails fade to nothing just past perihelion - most Kreutz fragments vaporize into dust at or before closest approach. The three named historical comets stay visible the whole way through, because they are the rare survivors.


Read the family fan

The faint lines fanning out around the bright orbit represent sibling family members grouped on nearly the same path - an illustration of the family, not measured data for specific objects.


Drag to orbit and use fullscreen

Drag to orbit the camera, scroll or pinch to zoom, and use the Fullscreen button to expand the whole wrapper for a clearer view.
