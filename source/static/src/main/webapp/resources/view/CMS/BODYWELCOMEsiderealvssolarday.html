A mean sidereal day is about 23h 56m 04.09s (86164.0905 s) while a mean solar day is 24h (86400 s) - a gap of about 3m 56s (~235.91 s) - because Earth spins against a distant star while also orbiting the Sun.

Drag to orbit the view, play or step through one solar day, and toggle both / star-only / Sun-only sightlines. The orange mark is a teaching observer on Earth.

The facts panel cites published mean day lengths so the about 3m 56s difference stays the reliable takeaway - orbit scale is compressed for readability.

Walking the controls, one solar day at a time

Five moves cover the whole scene. Work them in order the first time and the ~3m 56s gap lands without any narration.

  1. Open the explorer and wait for Earth, the Sun, and the distant star to paint.
  2. Drag to orbit the view and keep the facts panel visible so both mean day lengths stay on screen.
  3. Use Pause and +5% day to scrub through one solar day of teaching time.
  4. Press Star only, then Sun only, then Both lines to isolate each definition.
  5. Press Reset and repeat, or press Fullscreen for a classroom projector.

Where the scene stops

Everything renders on your device with WebGL. The 3D engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is cached; no scene data is sent to a server.

The scene teaches mean day lengths only - it does not model the equation of time or eccentricity seasonality of the true solar day, and it is not a chronometer or full ephemeris. For axial-tilt seasons, use Seasons Earth 3D Explorer instead.
