Live AQI Checker by City

Last reviewed: 2026-08-31

Check the current air quality (US AQI) for any city, right in the browser.

How air-quality data reaches this page

Data comes live from Open-Meteo's free air-quality API - nothing is uploaded and no account is required; the city search uses Open-Meteo's geocoding API.

Location access is optional and only requested after you press Use my location; the browser asks for permission each time you use it, and typing a city name instead needs no permissions at all.

A 3-day peak-AQI forecast table below the result answers "will it improve tomorrow" for wildfire-smoke or pollution spikes.

These are the same six bands the tool's own bands table and the color badge next to your result use, so a reading of 62 always maps to "Moderate" whether you check New York, your own city, or your current location.

Why the 3-day forecast can jump even on a mostly-calm day

The forecast row for each of the next 3 days is not an average of that day's hourly readings - it is the single highest hourly US AQI value Open-Meteo predicts for that day. So if a wildfire-smoke plume is expected to pass through for just a couple of hours in the afternoon, tomorrow's row on Live AQI Checker by City will show that spike's category, even though most of the day is forecast to stay clean. Read the forecast row as "the worst it gets that day," not "the typical hour that day."

The tool also asks Open-Meteo specifically for the US AQI parameter, not the EU AQI parameter the same API can return. The two scales use different math and different pollutant weighting, so comparing a reading here against another app that reports the EU scale will not line up, even for the exact same city at the exact same moment.
