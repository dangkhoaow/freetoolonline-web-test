How to Estimate Trip Fuel Cost - Step by Step

Last reviewed: 2026-09-27

The fuel cost calculator needs three numbers - distance, fuel efficiency and fuel price - and shows the fuel a trip needs and what it costs, live as you type. Nothing is installed and nothing you enter leaves the browser. Here is each step with a worked example.


Step 1 - open the tool and pick your units

Go to the fuel cost calculator. The units dropdown offers US (miles, MPG, price per gallon) and metric (km, L/100km, price per liter). If you switch later, the values you already typed are converted in place - the result stays equivalent.


Step 2 - enter the distance

Type the one-way distance from your route planner. For there-and-back, tick the round-trip checkbox instead of doubling by hand - the tool doubles the distance for you and says so in the result line.


Step 3 - enter your vehicle's efficiency

Use the figure from your car's trip display or the owner's manual: MPG in US units, or liters per 100 km in metric. A mid-size gasoline car is typically around 30 MPG (about 7.8 L/100km); if in doubt, enter a slightly worse figure so the estimate errs on the safe side.


Step 4 - enter the fuel price and travelers

Type the pump price you actually pay, in your own currency - the tool never fetches prices, so the output is a plain number without a currency symbol. If several people split the cost, set the travelers field and a per-person line appears.


Step 5 - read the result

Three outputs update on every keystroke: fuel needed (gallons or liters), total fuel cost, and cost per mile or km. The worked examples:
