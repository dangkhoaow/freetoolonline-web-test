Photo Translator - Translate Text in a Photo, On-Device

Last reviewed: 2026-08-26

Point this page at a photo, a screenshot, or a scan with printed English text - a menu, a sign, a package label, a slide, an error dialog - and it reads the text out of the image and translates it, entirely in your browser. The photo is never uploaded: the reading runs on your device with an engine served from this site, and the translation runs on your device too, either with Chrome's built-in translation or with a small model you download once and your browser keeps.

The flow is two visible steps. Step 1 reads the text: pick the image, click the button, and the recognized text appears in an editable box - so when the reader trips over a blurry word you can correct it before translating, instead of translating the mistake. Step 2 translates that text into your pick of Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Hindi, Chinese, Russian or Arabic. If you skip Step 2 entirely, Step 1 is still a complete result: copyable text pulled out of an image with nothing downloaded beyond the small reader.

Two boundaries are worth knowing before you rely on it. The reader is built for clear printed text and reads English; handwriting belongs on the handwriting to text page, which runs a model trained for it. And the translation quality is small-model: solid for everyday sentences, weaker on idioms and names, so review anything important before you act on it.

If you already have the text as text, skip the photo step and use the AI translator directly - it adds auto language detection and more direction choices. If you want the text out of an image with no translation at all, the image to text OCR page does exactly that one job.
