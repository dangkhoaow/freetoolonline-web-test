Photo Translator - Translate Text in a Photo, On-Device

Last reviewed: 2026-08-26

Point this page at a photo, a screenshot, or a scan with printed English text - a menu, a sign, a package label, a slide, an error dialog - and it reads the text out of the image and translates it, entirely in your browser. The photo is never uploaded.

The flow is two visible steps. Step 1 reads the text: pick the image, click the button, and the recognized text appears in an editable box. Step 2 translates that text into the language you pick. If you skip Step 2, Step 1 is still a complete result on its own.


Key features

  • Reads printed English text out of a photo or screenshot on your device - the photo is never uploaded, because the reading engine is served from this site and runs in your browser.
  • The recognized text lands in an editable box, so when the reader trips over a blurry word you can correct it before translating, instead of translating the mistake.
  • Translates the recognized text into your pick of Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Hindi, Chinese, Russian or Arabic.
  • On Chrome 138+ the translation can run with the browser's built-in translation engine, with no download from this page.
  • Otherwise the on-device translation model - about 115 MB per language pair - downloads only after you click the load button, shows progress, and is kept by your browser afterwards.
  • Without any translation engine the recognized text itself is a complete, copyable result: reading a photo needs only the small reader, about 5 MB served from this site.

When to use it

Reach for this when the text you need is stuck inside an image - a photographed menu or sign, a screenshot of a slide, a scanned package label, an error dialog you cannot copy from. Read it, correct any slip in the box, then translate it into a language you understand.


Reading and translation quality

The reader is built for clear printed text and reads English; it works best on sharp, well-lit, straight-on text, while blur, glare and steep angles cause slips. Handwriting belongs on the handwriting to text page, which runs a model trained for it. Translation quality is small-model: solid for everyday sentences, weaker on idioms and names, so review anything important before you act on it.


Related tools

If you already have the text as text, skip the photo step and use the AI translator directly - it adds auto language detection and more direction choices. If you want the text out of an image with no translation at all, the image to text OCR page does exactly that one job.
