How the reading works

The reading takes a single upload through the HTTPS processing service: the JPG header is parsed server-side to read the recorded quality marker, the value is returned to the page, and the upload copy is rotated out after the retention window. The tool reads the marker only - it does not change or re-save the file - so the next decision (keep, re-compress for delivery, or hand off to a compress tool) gets made on real numbers.

There is no batch mode: the page reports one reading per upload and does not iterate a folder, so checking several JPGs means deciding on the file in front of you, then re-uploading the next one.

The accepted inputs are .jpeg and .jpg only, and both the upload filter and the server-side service reject any other extension before the read is attempted. PNG and GIF use lossless representations; HEIC, AVIF, and WebP have no equivalent quality scalar - so a non-JPEG file has to be converted to JPG first.


Reading the quality bands

The recorded number separates archive copies from delivery copies, which is what no screen-side visual check can tell you. Three bands cover the decision, and each one points at a different next step.

Recorded qualityWhat the file isNext step
High band, around 85 and aboveThe source for further workKeep it as the archive, re-save from it for new delivery targets, and take it into compress image to hit a smaller target with predictable loss. If 80 is the delivery target, the archive belongs above that, not at the same level.
Mid band, roughly 65 to 85Already re-saved once; treat as a delivery copyBranch the next save from a separate higher-quality original rather than from the JPG you just read, and do not feed it back through compress unless you have a higher-band archive.
Low band, below 65At the bottom of its quality budgetStop re-compressing and go back to the original capture for a fresh master. A file flagged at quality 50 re-saved at 70 only bakes artefacts in.

Reading the number first is what prevents the silent quality-floor problem, where every later save runs at or below an already-low starting point and the original detail is no longer recoverable.


Auditing a compression pass in two numbers

After re-saving a JPG through a separate compress tool, read the output back here as a second upload. The second reading reports the quality marker recorded by the compressor, making the round-trip auditable in two numbers - input quality and output quality - rather than a screen-side guess.
