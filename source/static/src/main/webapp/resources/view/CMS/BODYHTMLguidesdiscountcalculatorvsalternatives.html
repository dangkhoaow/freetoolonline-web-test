Against mental math

The 10% anchor trick (take 10%, scale up) handles a single clean discount well: 20% off 50 is two tens, 10 off. It breaks on stacked deals - adding 25% and 10% to get 35% overstates the discount, because the coupon applies to the reduced price; the true total is 32.5%. The calculator states that combined figure explicitly, plus the savings amount mental math usually skips.


Against the phone's calculator app

Multiplying by 0.75 gets a 25%-off sale price in any calculator app. What it does not give you: the amount saved, the effective total of a stacked coupon, the tax row, or the breakdown table - each is another manual chain with a chance to slip a step. Here they render together, live, from the same three or four numbers.


Against a spreadsheet

A spreadsheet reaches the same arithmetic once the formulas are written, and it wins when you want a saved record - a price-watch list across weeks, say. For a one-tag decision in a store aisle, the tool is faster, needs nothing installed, and cannot slip a cell reference. Nothing you type is stored or sent anywhere.
