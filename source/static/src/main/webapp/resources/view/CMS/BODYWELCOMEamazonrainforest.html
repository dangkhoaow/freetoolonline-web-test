Amazon Rainforest 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-29

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Amazon Rainforest as a canopy-top relief: a near-continuous green ceiling banded from understory-to-mid canopy up to the densest upper canopy, scattered emergent trees breaking through the top, and the sediment-brown Amazon River cutting a light-gap corridor across the basin.

The rainforest's core spreads across about 5.5 million sq km and nine countries, with roughly 60% inside Brazil. Its main canopy typically stands 30-45 m tall, while emergent trees push through to about 55-60 m - the tallest Amazon tree measured to date, a Dinizia excelsa recorded in 2019, reached about 88 m. The Amazon River itself runs about 6,400 km and carries an average discharge of about 209,000 cubic meters per second, roughly a fifth of all river water reaching the world's oceans. The basin is estimated to hold one in ten of the world's known species.

  • Toggle the emergent-tree markers that break through the main canopy
  • Toggle the Amazon River ribbon cutting across the basin
  • Read area, canopy-height, river, and biodiversity figures in the panel
  • See the canopy bands from understory-to-mid green up to bright emergent
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload
  • Switch the View button to the Meeting of Waters confluence for real measured terrain and satellite imagery

Because the full 5.5 million sq km rainforest cannot fit inside any single terrain window, the explorer's Confluence view frames one real, named reach: the Encontro das Aguas (Meeting of Waters) near Manaus, where the black-water Rio Negro meets the sediment-pale Solimoes. That view is built from real elevation tiles (Mapzen/AWS Open Data Terrarium, derived from NASA/USGS DEMs) and Sentinel-2 satellite imagery, decoding the confluence itself to about 7 m above sea level - matching the published figure for the site. The canopy-relief scene above stays a schematic teaching cue, not a survey map.
