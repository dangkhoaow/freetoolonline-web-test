The vehicle recall lookup, the official nhtsa.gov search, a dealer phone call, and paid VIN history reports can all answer "does this vehicle have an open recall". They differ in cost, speed, and what exactly comes back. The table below is the short answer.

When this page wins

Speed and breadth: you want the model's whole campaign list in seconds - the number, report date, affected component, problem, risk, and fix per card, newest first, with do-not-drive, park-outside, and over-the-air badges rendered straight from the NHTSA data. It also takes a year, make, and model when you do not have the VIN at hand, which the official VIN search cannot. Your query goes only to the official US-government NHTSA API - never to this site's servers.


When an alternative wins

Proof that one specific car was already repaired: the nhtsa.gov VIN search or the dealer - this page lists model-level campaigns, not per-car completion status. A purchase decision needing accident and title history too: a paid VIN report bundles those. Anything outside the US market: none of the NHTSA channels cover other countries' recall systems - use the local authority's own lookup.


Same data, different packaging

The free channels all read NHTSA's campaign records, so the campaigns themselves should agree everywhere - what you are choosing is packaging: instant model-level cards here, per-VIN completion status on nhtsa.gov, and a person on the phone at the dealer.
