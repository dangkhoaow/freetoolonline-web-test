Why do the pillars look so different in visible versus infrared light?

In visible light the dense gas and dust block starlight, making the pillars appear as dark silhouettes against a bright nebula - the classic 1995 Hubble image. In infrared light, longer wavelengths penetrate the dust, revealing young stars embedded inside the pillars that are completely hidden in visible light - this is what James Webb showed in 2022. The 3D explorer lets you toggle between both views on the same structure.
