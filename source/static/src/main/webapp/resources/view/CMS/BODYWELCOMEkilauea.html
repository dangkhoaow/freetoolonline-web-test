Kilauea 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Kilauea, on the southeast flank of Mauna Loa on Hawaii Island - one of the most active volcanoes on Earth. Where Vesuvius and Etna rise as steep or four-cratered cones, Kilauea spreads into a much lower, far broader shield, gently sloped enough that a person standing on its flank might not immediately recognize it as a volcano at all.

At Kilauea's summit sits a large collapsed caldera about 4 km by 3.2 km across. Inside that caldera, the smaller Halema'uma'u crater - traditionally regarded as the home of the volcano goddess Pele - has held an active lava lake during several eruptive episodes since 2008. Two rift zones radiate from the summit: the long East Rift Zone, which fed decades of flank eruptions including the Pu'u 'O'o vent (1983-2018) and the destructive 2018 lower Puna eruption, and the shorter Southwest Rift Zone.

  • Toggle the lava lake glow to see Halema'uma'u's crater floor light up orange-red
  • Toggle the rift-zone ridges to see the East Rift Zone and Southwest Rift Zone traced outward from the caldera
  • Read elevation, caldera size, and heritage-status figures in the info panel
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom

For other volcanoes, explore the Mount Etna 3D Explorer for a broad four-crater shield-strato profile, or the Mount Vesuvius 3D Explorer for a single steep cone inside an ancient caldera ridge - both dramatically steeper and narrower than Kilauea's low shield.

Kilauea's caldera, lava lake, and rift-zone ridges are drawn right in your browser through WebGL, so the whole volcano stays on your own device. The three.js engine behind the scene downloads a single time (about 0.7 MB), then is cached, so returning to spin the scene again is instant.

This is an educational approximation - the terrain, caldera, lava-lake glow, and rift-zone ridges are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
