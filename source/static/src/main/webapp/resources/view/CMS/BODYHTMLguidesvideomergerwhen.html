When to merge clips with Video Merger - and when not to

Last reviewed: 2026-10-02

Use Video Merger when two or more clips should play back-to-back as one file: a day of phone clips, lecture parts, dashcam segments, or a short vertical edit with music. Pick another tool for titles or effects, for cutting one video into pieces, or for converting a single file.


Frame shape, engine and speed by scenario

Video Merger picks its engine automatically and shows it as a badge while merging: "Lossless join - no re-encode" when every clip matches and nothing is edited, "Encoding in your browser (WebCodecs)" for everything else, and "Compatibility mode (slower)" when the browser lacks WebCodecs, when a clip cannot be decoded or read, or as a fallback after an encoder failure.

A day of phone clips into one video

Video Merger can turn ten or twenty clips from one phone into a single file. If every clip has the same codec settings, resolution, rotation and audio format, and you change nothing - no "Trim", no "Mute", no transition, no music, "Frame shape" on Original, "Quality" on Auto - the clips are copied with no quality loss.

One mismatched clip or one edit means a re-encode: lossy at a high quality setting, not bit-identical. The cards show each clip's resolution and orientation, so a lone 4K or portrait clip stands out. Many iPhones record HEVC, and a re-encode of those in Chrome without a hardware HEVC decoder runs in "Compatibility mode (slower)".


Landscape and portrait clips in one frame

Video Merger fits every clip into one frame, set under "Frame shape": Original (the first clip's shape), 16:9 (landscape), 9:16 (vertical), 1:1 (square) or 4:5 (portrait). On Original, a portrait first clip makes the whole video portrait.

Under "Fit", "Letterbox (show the whole clip)" adds black bars: a portrait clip in a 16:9 frame fills about a third of the width. "Crop to fill" covers the frame and trims the overflow from the center, so that clip keeps only the middle third of its height. Pick Letterbox when faces or text sit near the edges.

Rotation flags are applied, so portrait clips stand upright; "Try two sample clips" loads a landscape and a portrait demo to test both fits.


Vertical edits for short-video feeds (9:16)

For a vertical post, set "Frame shape" in Video Merger to 9:16 (vertical). Portrait phone clips usually fill it as they are; a landscape clip on "Crop to fill" loses both sides, so keep the action centered.

Each card's "Trim" sliders move in 0.1 s steps, and "Preview" plays only the trimmed range. With "Quality" on Auto and 1080p or 4K sources, a browser encode outputs 1080 x 1920. The Output line usually reads "Output: MP4 (H.264 + AAC)"; a browser that cannot encode H.264 produces WebM instead.

For a 16:9 version as well, press "Merge again" after downloading - clips and settings stay - and switch "Frame shape". Each run makes one file.


Screen recordings, lectures and meeting parts

Video Merger also suits recordings that stopped and restarted, such as a lecture in three parts or a meeting split by a reconnect. Parts from one app at one setting often match, and in a lossless join the output container follows the codecs: WebM stays WebM, and H.264 or HEVC comes out as MP4.

Trimming the wait before a meeting starts forces a re-encode. On "Quality" Auto with Original shape, that output is capped at 1080 pixels on the short side - a 1440p capture becomes 1080p - so fine text like code stays sharpest in an untrimmed join. If a reconnect changed the window size, Letterbox keeps all shared content in view.


Travel montage with music and crossfades

A travel montage uses Video Merger's transitions and music. Between cards, pick "No transition", "Crossfade" or "Fade through black"; choose one under "Every gap" and press "Apply to all" to set them all at once. "Transition length" (0.5 s, 1 s or 1.5 s) covers all gaps, shortened automatically for clips too short to hold it.

"Crossfade" overlaps neighboring clips, so 15 clips with 14 one-second crossfades run 14 seconds shorter than their combined length; "Fade through black" keeps the total length.

"Add music" takes any audio file the browser can decode, such as MP3, M4A/AAC, WAV, OGG, Opus or FLAC. "Music volume" starts at 80 %, "Clip audio while music plays" defaults to Lower (25 %), and looping plus a 2 s fade-out at the end are on by default. Clips have no individual volume, only "Mute". Re-encoded HDR clips come out as standard 8-bit (SDR), so colors can shift from what the phone shows.


Dashcam and security camera segments

Dashcams and many security cameras save fixed-length segments, a good fit for Video Merger's lossless join: segments from one camera at one setting usually match. The match breaks if some segments lack an audio track or the resolution changed, and AVI exports always run in "Compatibility mode (slower)".

Such footage often shows license plates, faces and front doors. Video Merger reads the files in the browser and uploads nothing, which you can check in the browser developer tools Network tab. It needs no account, adds no watermark and keeps nothing after the tab closes. Device memory, not a server cap, limits size, so merge a long drive one trip at a time. Front and rear camera files join in sequence only; there is no side-by-side layout.


When something else fits better

Video Merger is built for joining clips in sequence. Other jobs belong elsewhere:

  • Titles, captions, effects or keyframes - the Video Editor.
  • One file into pieces - the Video Splitter.
  • One range from one video - the Video Trimmer; "Merge videos" needs at least two clips.
  • A new format for one file - the in-browser HD Video Converter; the merger has no format menu, bitrate setting or file-size target.
  • Speed changes or GIF output - the Video Speed Changer or Video to GIF; per-clip rotate or crop and photos as clips are not available in the merger.
  • Long or 4K footage on an older phone - expect a slow encode, and keep the tab open in the foreground: closing it stops the merge. The tool warns before merging when the estimated output is large (over about 1.5 GB in a browser encode); if the time-left estimate looks long, press "Cancel" and use a desktop browser.

Frequently asked questions

What happens to clips that have no sound?

They add silence for their length, keeping picture and sound in sync; muted clips do the same. On a re-encode, all audio becomes 48 kHz stereo, so a mono microphone plays in both ears and surround tracks are mixed down.

Do clips from two different phones qualify for the lossless join?

Only when nothing differs, and different phone models often record with different codec settings or audio formats. The summary line on the result screen names the engine that ran.

Which phone browsers can run a merge?

Current Chrome and Edge, and Safari 16.4 or later; on Safari before version 26, Video Merger loads its own AAC encoder, a download of about 1 MB. Firefox on Android has no WebCodecs and runs merges in "Compatibility mode (slower)".
