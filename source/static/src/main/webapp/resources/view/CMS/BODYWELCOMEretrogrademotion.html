Retrograde Motion 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-31

Mars appears to loop on the sky when Earth overtakes on a faster inside orbit - the yellow line of sight sweeps, the sky trail draws the loop near opposition, and the facts panel lists Mars synodic period about 779.9 d (~26 months) with a typical retrograde arc about 72 d.

Drag to orbit the view, scroll or pinch to zoom, and press Play. Toggle Sky trail and Line of sight, jump Near opposition, and scrub Day across one synodic cycle. The Retrograde / Direct badge follows finite-difference sky elongation.

  • Sun + Earth + Mars on compressed coplanar orbits
  • Yellow Earth-to-Mars line of sight
  • Sky trail showing the apparent loop shape
  • Play / Pause, Near opposition jump, day scrub
  • Published synodic and arc figures in the facts panel
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - renders on your device with WebGL, no account, no upload; the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached, and no scene data is sent to a server

Reading the scene in three steps

Find the two worlds by color, jump to opposition with the sky trail on, then scrub Day across the synodic cycle to watch direct motion return.

  1. Find the overtakes: Earth is the blue inner world, Mars the red outer one, and the yellow line is the Earth-to-Mars line of sight.
  2. Click Near opposition and leave Sky trail on, so the purple path draws the loop stretch while the badge flips to Retrograde.
  3. Drag Day across hundreds of days to see direct motion return. Fullscreen targets the scene wrapper, so the controls stay visible while you orbit the view.

Teachers use it to demystify retrograde as a viewpoint effect, and students scrub through opposition to catch the reverse stretch.

Solar System 3D Explorer is a heliocentric neighborhood tour of all the planets; Retrograde Motion 3D Explorer isolates the viewpoint illusion that a quiet top-down planet parade hides. A textbook prints one static zig-zag, while this page adds play, line of sight, and a live sky trail in a browser-only 3D view. Kepler Orbits 3D Explorer teaches the ellipse laws rather than Earth-Mars overtakes.

The scene is an educational visualization of relative geometry - orbit sizes and trail angles are compressed. It is not a precision ephemeris, not a dated opposition calendar, not a constellation chart for tonight, and not an N-body gravity model.
