This page renders Dacentrurus as a 3D model you can spin in the browser - drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, toggle a 1.8 m person beside it for scale, and click the head, a back plate, the tail, or a leg to read a fact about that part.

Dacentrurus lived in the Late Jurassic, roughly 154 to 140 million years ago, and its fossils come from England, France, Portugal, and Spain - making it one of the few stegosaurs known from mainland Europe. It was the first stegosaur ever described from a European skeleton: Richard Owen named the holotype Omosaurus armatus in 1875 from a find near Swindon, England, and Frederick Augustus Lucas renamed the genus Dacentrurus in 1902 after discovering the name Omosaurus was already used for a different fossil reptile.

Published size estimates disagree. Commonly cited figures put adults around 8-9 m long and 5-7.4 tonnes, with a hip height of about 1.8 m, while the holotype itself measured only about 7 m and some estimates range up to 10 m. A pelvis roughly 1.5 m wide has been used to argue Dacentrurus was among the largest known stegosaurs, despite older texts describing it as small. Like Stegosaurus, it carried plates along its back and paired spikes near the tip of its tail (a thagomizer) - a broader, classic stegosaur build rather than the long neck of its closer relative Miragaia.
