Are Flags at Half-Mast Today? (US Federal Status)

Last reviewed: 2026-08-31

Five recurring US federal observances put the flag at half-staff every year: Peace Officers Memorial Day (May 15), Memorial Day (last Monday in May, until noon), Patriot Day (September 11), National POW/MIA Recognition Day (third Friday in September), and Pearl Harbor Remembrance Day (December 7). This half-mast checker compares today's date against those five rules in your browser.

Everything computes locally in the browser from durable calendar rules - no upload, no account, and no network request.

The full-year table below covers this year and next year, so you can plan ahead around a known, recurring observance - useful for schools, government offices, or veterans' organizations that lower flags on these dates every year.

Two of the five follow an nth-weekday-of-month rule instead of a fixed date - Memorial Day is always the last Monday in May, and National POW/MIA Recognition Day is always the third Friday in September - so this half-mast checker computes the exact date fresh for whichever year it loads in, rather than reading from a hard-coded per-year list.

Why can't a specific person's flag order be predicted in advance?

The five recurring dates above are set under the US Flag Code (4 U.S.C. Sec 7) and long-standing annual proclamations, so this half-mast checker can compute the same rule every year without looking anything up. A half-staff order tied to one specific death or tragedy instead comes from an individual proclamation, issued by the President for the national flag or by a state governor for state and local flags - one event at a time, with no calendar rule to compute from in advance.
