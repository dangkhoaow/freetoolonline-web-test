Solar Flare Classification 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-25

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer visualizes the GOES X-ray flare classification scale - the letter-and-number system NOAA uses to rate how strong a solar flare is, and the matching NOAA Radio Blackout (R) scale that describes what a flare of that strength does to high-frequency radio on Earth.

GOES satellites measure the Sun's peak soft X-ray output in the 1-8 angstrom band and sort each flare into a letter class, each one a power-of-ten step: A is below 1e-7 W/m^2, B is 1e-7 to below 1e-6 W/m^2, C is 1e-6 to below 1e-5 W/m^2, M is 1e-5 to below 1e-4 W/m^2, and X is 1e-4 W/m^2 or higher, with no formal upper cap. Each letter subdivides linearly from 1.0 to 9.9 - an M5 flare is 5x10^-5 W/m^2, an X1 flare is 1x10^-4 W/m^2. NOAA's 5-level Radio Blackout scale ties directly to these classes: R1 (Minor) starts at M1, R2 (Moderate) at M5 - the level at which NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center issues an alert - R3 (Strong) at X1, R4 (Severe) at X10, and R5 (Extreme) at X20. The largest confirmed flare on record is the X28 flare of 4 November 2003, part of the "Halloween storms" - the GOES sensor saturated near its peak, so the true value may have been even higher.

  • Drag the flare-class slider to step through A, B, C, M1, M5, X1, X10, and X20
  • Watch the flare loop brighten and the particle burst intensify as the class rises
  • Watch the red reach arc on the Earth companion grow once a class reaches M1/R1 or higher - classes A through C carry no NOAA Radio Blackout rating at all
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

For the plasma cloud a strong flare can throw outward, open the Coronal Mass Ejection 3D Explorer; for the 11-year rise and fall in sunspot counts this activity rides on top of, open the Sunspot Activity 3D Explorer.

Everything renders on your device with WebGL. The 3D engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is cached.

This is an educational approximation, not a physical simulation. Flare brightness, loop size, particle-burst intensity, and the Earth companion's reach arc are scaled for visibility and are not a rendered radiative-transfer or magnetic-reconnection model, and the reach arc is not a precise blackout footprint map. The flux ranges and R-scale figures above are the real NOAA SWPC ones.
