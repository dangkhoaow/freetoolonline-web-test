Spinosaurus Size Comparison

Last reviewed: 2026-08-13

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

The Spinosaurus 3D Viewer shows a sail-backed theropod at its published figures: length about 14 m, hip height about 2.1 m, weight about 7.4 tonnes. Turn on Size vs human and a 1.8 m person appears at the true length ratio, so the theropod scale gap is readable at a glance.

What the Spinosaurus facts panel lists

The facts panel beside the canvas carries exactly five rows: Length 14 m, Weight 7.4 t, When it lived 99-94 Mya (Late Cretaceous), Diet Carnivore (mainly fish), and Described 1912, Egypt (named by Stromer, 1915). Hip height is not one of those rows - the about 2.1 m figure is a page measure used in the table above, not a panel row. Under the table the panel adds a species note (the sail of elongated neural spines, the crocodile-like snout and conical teeth) and a disclosure line naming the model and its license.


The model that loads on the Spinosaurus page

The page paints a shaped Spinosaurus immediately so nothing waits on a download, then quietly upgrades it. After that first paint the viewer fetches a real, free-licensed 3D model for the species, checks it against a published checksum, caches it in the browser, and swaps it in - at which point the status line changes to "Showing a real 3D model of this species." The current model is credited to Tuomo Untinen under CC-BY-SA 3.0 via OpenGameArt, and the facts panel names that credit once the swap lands. If the download or the graphics step fails for any reason, the first-paint model simply stays on screen, so the page never breaks while waiting.


How the Size vs human toggle scales the person

The Spinosaurus model is drawn to a fixed on-screen length so the whole animal fits the canvas: 12 world units stand for the published 14 m. The scale figure is then sized by that same units-per-metre factor, which is why the comparison is honest - the person and the animal share one ruler derived from LENGTH, not from hip height. Press Size vs human under the canvas to show the figure; the button flips to Hide human to remove it again. The comparison is proportional, not a decorative sticker.


The four Spinosaurus controls under the canvas

Drag with the pointer to orbit the Spinosaurus, and scroll or pinch with two fingers to zoom. When the scene is ready the status line reads "Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, click a body part for a fact." Four buttons sit under the canvas:

  • Fullscreen - expands the viewer wrapper so the scene grows while the buttons stay visible
  • Stop rotation - freezes the slow auto-spin; the label flips to Auto-rotate to resume it
  • Idle motion - adds subtle breathing and leg sway; the label flips to Stand still
  • Size vs human - shows the 1.8 m scale figure; the label flips to Hide human

Clicking a body part opens a part note

A short click swaps the species sheet for a note about the part under the pointer. The viewer separates a click from a drag by distance: if the pointer moved less than 8 pixels it counts as a click, so a slow orbit never fires a part note by accident. On this theropod the clickable parts are the head, neck, body, arm, leg and tail, each with its own note - the head note says the skull shape follows the fossil while the fleshy profile is an artist reconstruction. Click empty space to return to the fact sheet.


What a person would see beside a Spinosaurus

A person standing alongside would meet a hip line just above their own head: at about 2.1 m at the hip, the Spinosaurus hip sits a little over a 1.8 m adult. From snout to tail the animal spans about 14 m, roughly 7.8 times that person end to end. Turn on the scale figure and orbit beside a hind leg to see how the long narrow snout and the dorsal sail tower past it.

When the browser viewer fits and when it does not

Pick the browser viewer when you want drag-orbit on a sail-backed theropod, the scale figure at the honest length ratio, and part notes on the head, a leg or the tail without installing anything. Nothing about the visit is sent to a server, which suits a classroom laptop or a quick desktop check of the Late Cretaceous numbers. A phone AR app wins when you need the silhouette on your own floor - this viewer never claims room placement. A museum wins for walking beside mounted bone at hall scale. A static image wins only when one shareable frame is enough.


What the Spinosaurus size numbers are not

These numbers are published estimates with an honest length range, not settled science: earlier work put the animal nearer 15 m, and mass estimates move with the skeleton reconstruction used. Skin and sail color are an artistic reconstruction, because fossils do not preserve soft tissue or color. Whether Spinosaurus was fully aquatic is still debated, so the viewer describes a semi-aquatic lifestyle as argued rather than proven. The page is a viewer and a size sheet - it does not place the model in your room, does not claim colour accuracy, and carries no score or win state.
