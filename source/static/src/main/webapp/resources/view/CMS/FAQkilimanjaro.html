Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Kilimanjaro have glaciers near the equator?

Elevation is the key: at 5,895 m, the summit is high enough that the temperature stays below freezing year-round, despite the equatorial location. The thin atmosphere at this altitude, combined with high wind speeds, preserves the ice.

How much glacier has Kilimanjaro lost?

The snowcap has shrunk from about 12 square km in 1912 to about 1.76 square km in 2026 - an 85% loss over 114 years. Scientists attribute this to climate warming and reduced cloud moisture reaching the summit.

Is Kilimanjaro an active volcano?

No - it is dormant (technically, not yet extinct). The last eruption was about 360,000 years ago. The three volcanic cones (Kibo, Mawenzi, Shira) show a progression of activity from oldest to youngest.

How many people climb Kilimanjaro each year?

About 50,000 people attempt the summit annually; roughly 65% succeed. Most use the Marangu (tourist) route, which takes 5-6 days to ascend and acclimatize.

What countries does Kilimanjaro border?

The mountain is located in northeastern Tanzania, near the border with Kenya. It stands alone on the African continent - the closest other major mountain range (the Rwenzori, in Uganda/DRC) is over 1,000 km away.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the cone profile, elevation colors, and snowcap outlines are schematic teaching cues, not survey data or satellite imagery.
