Bryce Canyon 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-05

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Bryce Canyon as a horseshoe amphitheater carved into the rim of the Paunsaugunt Plateau, packed with thousands of hoodoo spires banded from rose-pink at the bowl floor up through orange and pale pink to cream near the rim.

Despite the name, Bryce Canyon is not a true canyon: frost-wedging and rain carved it into a series of amphitheaters along the plateau edge, not a river. The rim sits about 2,400-2,778 m above sea level, and the hoodoos below it typically run 1.5-45 m tall, cut from the pink, orange, and white Claron Formation limestone laid down roughly 30-40 million years ago. Bryce Canyon National Park was established in 1928 to protect the amphitheaters.

For other rock formations in this cluster, open the Grand Canyon 3D Explorer (a river-carved gorge with stacked layers) or the Stone Forest (Shilin) 3D Explorer (a dense karst pinnacle field).

The amphitheater, its packed hoodoo spires, and the color banding are all drawn on your own device with WebGL, so orbiting and zooming across the bowl stay smooth with nothing sent to a server. The three.js engine that renders the scene downloads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached, so a second visit to Bryce Canyon reuses it and starts faster.

This is an educational approximation - hoodoo placement, spacing, and color bands are procedural teaching cues, not survey geology.
