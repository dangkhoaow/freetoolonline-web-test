Okavango Delta 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Okavango Delta as an inland fan: a single trunk enters Botswana through the Okavango Panhandle, fans into a network of braided channels across a flat plain, then shrinks and dries away completely into Kalahari sand - no coastline, no river mouth, no ocean.

The water starts as rain falling in the Angolan highlands, feeding the Cubango and Cuito Rivers that join to form the Okavango River. That rain falls roughly October to April, but the floodwater takes about four months to travel the roughly 1,200 km downstream, so the delta's own peak flood usually arrives June to August - during Botswana's dry winter, a mismatch that shapes the whole ecosystem. In most years almost none of that water reaches any sea: it disappears through evaporation, plant transpiration, and seepage into the sand.

  • Toggle the Chief's Island marker for the delta's largest island, largely inside Moremi Game Reserve

The Okavango Delta scene assembles entirely inside your browser using the site-vendored three.js WebGL build, so the braided channel network, the flood-pulse recolor, and the Chief's Island marker all redraw on your own device. The engine downloads once (about 0.7 MB), is cached for return visits, and nothing about your session - no account, no upload - is ever sent to a server.

This is an educational approximation - the channel network, plain profile, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey hydrology.
