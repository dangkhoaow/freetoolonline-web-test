Frequently Asked Questions

How long is the Aletsch Glacier?

About 23 km, making it the longest glacier in the Alps. It covers roughly 80 sq km of ice with a volume of about 15 cubic km.

What are the dark stripes on the ice?

Medial moraines - long bands of rock debris carried along where the Grosser Aletschfirn, Jungfraufirn, and Ewigschneefeld glaciers merge at Concordia Place and flow together down the valley.

What is Concordia Place?

The wide confluence area near the head of the glacier where its three tributary glaciers meet. Ice there was historically up to about 800-900 m thick, though it has thinned as the glacier retreats.

Has the Aletsch Glacier been shrinking?

Yes. It has retreated roughly 3 km since the mid-1800s, documented in photographs since 1856, with the pace of retreat accelerating in recent decades.

Is the Aletsch Glacier protected?

Yes. It lies within the Swiss Alps Jungfrau-Aletsch UNESCO World Heritage Site, listed since 2001.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the valley curve, ice surface, moraine stripes, and historic-edge marker are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
