When to Use the Voyager Interstellar Position 3D Explorer - Best Sessions and Limits

Last reviewed: 2026-07-25

Open Voyager Interstellar Position 3D Explorer when you want to see how far Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 have really traveled since their 1977 launches and when each one crossed into interstellar space - not to check today's exact live distance or run a to-scale orbital-dynamics projection of either probe's real early trajectory.


A launch-to-heliopause lesson

A lesson starts both probes at the Sun in 1977, scrubs the mission year forward, and watches Voyager 1 cross the heliopause first in 2012 while Voyager 2 - despite launching slightly earlier but visiting all four giant planets along the way - does not cross until 2018, six years later.


Sessions this explorer does not fit

Sessions this page does not fit are checking the single 1990 photograph Voyager 1 took looking back at Earth - use the Pale Blue Dot 3D Explorer for that - or exploring the belt of icy bodies and the far larger Oort Cloud on their own terms - use the Kuiper Belt and Oort Cloud 3D Explorer.
