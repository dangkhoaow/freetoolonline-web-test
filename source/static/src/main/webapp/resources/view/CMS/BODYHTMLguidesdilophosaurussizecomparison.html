What recent research adds about Dilophosaurus

Fossils preserve the twin crests' bone shape but not their outward covering, so the most likely material is keratin - the same protein that forms claws, beaks, and the cranial ornaments of several other dinosaurs. At 195-184 million years ago (often cited around 193 Mya), Dilophosaurus is also the geologically oldest theropod in this size-comparison collection and one of the smallest full-grown theropods shipped so far, well below the later, larger Tyrannosaurus rex, Carnotaurus, Allosaurus, and Giganotosaurus viewers in the same collection. A 2020 reanalysis by Marsh and Rowe reassessed the animal's ecological role: rather than treating it as a marginal scavenger, their study supports Dilophosaurus as an apex predator within its own Early Jurassic ecosystem, with some anatomical evidence consistent with piscivory (fish-eating) alongside general predation.

Frequently asked questions

What does the name "Dilophosaurus" mean?

Two-crested lizard. Samuel Welles gave Dilophosaurus its own genus name in 1970, after first describing the fossil as a Megalosaurus species in 1954, naming it directly for the paired head crests the model renders.

What were the twin crests likely covered in?

Most likely keratin - the fossils preserve the crests' bone shape but not their outward covering, so researchers infer a keratin sheath similar to claws, beaks, and other dinosaurs' cranial ornaments.

Was Dilophosaurus an apex predator or a scavenger?

A 2020 reanalysis by Marsh and Rowe supports Dilophosaurus as an apex predator in its own Early Jurassic ecosystem, with some anatomical evidence consistent with piscivory (fish-eating) alongside general predation, rather than a marginal scavenger.
