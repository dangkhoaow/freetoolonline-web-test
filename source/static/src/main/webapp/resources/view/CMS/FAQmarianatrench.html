Frequently Asked Questions

How deep is the Mariana Trench?

About 10,935 m below sea level at Challenger Deep, its deepest known point - though different sonar surveys report values roughly 100 m apart, from about 10,924 m to about 10,994 m.

Where is the Mariana Trench?

In the western Pacific Ocean, east of the Mariana Islands, where the Pacific plate subducts beneath the smaller Mariana plate.

How much pressure is at the bottom?

About 1,086 bar - roughly 1,000 times the atmospheric pressure at sea level.

Who has reached the bottom?

Trieste carried Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh to the bottom in 1960; the unmanned Kaiko reached it in 1995; James Cameron piloted Deepsea Challenger solo in 2012; the Five Deeps expedition returned in 2019.

Would Mount Everest fit in the trench?

Yes, with room to spare. Everest is about 8,849 m tall; the trench is about 10,935 m deep, so Everest's peak would still sit roughly 2 km underwater.

Is this a scientific bathymetry model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the trench profile, plate wedge, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey bathymetry.
