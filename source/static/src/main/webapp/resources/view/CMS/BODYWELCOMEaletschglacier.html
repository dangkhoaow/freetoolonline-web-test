Aletsch Glacier 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-28

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Aletsch Glacier as a curving river of ice descending through the Bernese Alps below the Jungfrau, formed where three tributary glaciers merge at Concordia Place. Toggle the dark medial moraine stripes left by the merging ice streams, and the marker showing how far the terminus has retreated since the 1860s.

Aletsch is the longest glacier in the Alps, stretching about 23 km and covering roughly 80 sq km of ice, with a volume of about 15 cubic km. It forms at Concordia Place, where the Grosser Aletschfirn, Jungfraufirn, and Ewigschneefeld glaciers converge and leave behind long dark stripes of rock debris - medial moraines - running the length of the ice below. Ice near Concordia Place was historically up to about 800-900 m thick, though it has thinned as the glacier retreats. Like most Alpine glaciers, Aletsch has been retreating since photographic records began in 1856, losing roughly 3 km in length; the terrain beyond today's ice edge, once covered by ice, is now bare moraine-strewn rock.

The whole scene - the curving valley, the ice surface, the medial moraines, and the historic edge marker - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to trace the moraine stripes from Concordia Place down to today's ice edge, and toggle the 1860s marker to see the retreat distance. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the valley curve, ice surface, moraine stripes, and historic-edge marker are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
