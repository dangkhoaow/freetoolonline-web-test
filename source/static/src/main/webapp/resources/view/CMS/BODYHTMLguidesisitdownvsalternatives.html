Down-for-Me Check vs Outage Sites and Ping - Compared

Last reviewed: 2026-08-21

The Is It Down checker, crowd-report outage sites, and a terminal ping answer three different questions. The checker probes a site directly from your own device in the browser; outage sites aggregate other people's reports; ping tests the network path from a command line. Here is an honest comparison so you reach for the right one.

When the device-local check wins

It is the fastest honest first move: one direct request from your browser to the site, a Reachable card with a response time in milliseconds, or a Not reachable card that names the three possible causes - the site is down, your network, DNS or ad-blocker blocks it, or the site refuses cross-site checks. Seven popular sites are pre-checked on load, and no third-party service ever receives the address you type.


When an outage site or ping wins

Crowd-report sites are the only way to confirm "down for everyone" - a claim this checker deliberately never makes, because it has exactly one vantage point. Terminal ping remains useful for network-path detail like packet loss, but many sites block ICMP, a terminal is not always at hand, and a blocked ping proves little about a website's front door. In practice: check here first, settle "just me" with a second network, and reach for a crowd site only when you need global confirmation.
