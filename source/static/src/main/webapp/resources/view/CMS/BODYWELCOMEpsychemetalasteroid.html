Psyche Metal Asteroid 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-02

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer replays NASA's real Psyche mission to metal-rich asteroid 16 Psyche, one independently published milestone at a time, from its 2023 launch through its 2026 Mars flyby to its planned 2029 orbit arrival and 2031 mission end.

Psyche launched on a Falcon Heavy from NASA's Kennedy Space Center on 2023-10-13 and cruises on a solar-electric propulsion system - four SPT-140 Hall-effect thrusters drawing up to 4.5 kW, carrying about 922 kg of xenon propellant. On 2026-05-15 it flew past Mars at a closest approach of about 4,609 km (2,864 mi), a gravity assist that added about 1,000 mph to its speed and bent its orbital plane about 1 degree relative to the Sun, without spending any propellant. After a roughly six-year, about 2.2-billion-mile (3.6-billion-km) cruise, Psyche's own gravity is expected to capture the spacecraft in late July 2029, with science operations beginning in August 2029. Asteroid 16 Psyche measures about 278 by 232 by 164 km (mean diameter about 222 km) and rotates once roughly every 4.2 hours; its bulk density, about 3.98 g/cm3, is well below solid iron-nickel metal (about 8 g/cm3), one reason scientists think Psyche is a porous mix of metal and rock rather than one solid metal ball. Over at least 26 months, the spacecraft moves through a series of progressively different science orbits, mapping the asteroid's shape, gravity field, and surface composition, before the mission's planned end on 2031-10-31. These figures come from NASA and NASA Science mission updates, the JPL Psyche press kit, and the peer-reviewed studies Vernazza et al. 2021, Ferrais et al. 2020, and Shepard et al. 2017/2021 (via JPL's Small-Body Database and Wikipedia's cited "16 Psyche" figures).

  • Milestone slider (5 stops) moves the spacecraft along a procedural cruise route, then into a stylized orbit ring around a noise-displaced Psyche body
  • Each stop shows its real date, name, and mission fact
  • A running table lists all 5 milestones at once, with the active one highlighted
  • A reddish Mars marker sits along the cruise route at the flyby point
  • The orbit-ring radius shrinks with each later milestone, standing in for the real multi-orbit science campaign
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

The whole Psyche milestone scene - the noise-displaced asteroid body, the shrinking stylized orbit-tier rings, and the drag-to-orbit camera - is drawn locally in your browser with WebGL, so scrubbing the milestone slider never uploads anything. The vendored three.js engine downloads once (about 0.7 MB) and then stays cached for your next visit.

This is an educational approximation - Psyche's body shape and the spacecraft's cruise route and orbit rings are procedural, not a precise shape model or a physically simulated flight path or orbit, and the scene does not interpolate a continuous position between milestones or claim a live spacecraft feed. Each milestone's date and description are real and independently published by NASA, NASA Science, the JPL Psyche press kit, and the cited peer-reviewed studies.
