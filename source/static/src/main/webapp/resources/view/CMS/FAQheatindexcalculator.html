Frequently Asked Questions

What does the heat index calculator do?

It takes the air temperature and relative humidity you enter and computes the heat index (the apparent, feels-like temperature) using the National Weather Service's Rothfusz-regression formula, then shows the matching NWS risk category.

Why does humidity change how hot it feels?

High humidity slows evaporative cooling from sweat, so the body cannot shed heat as efficiently. The same air temperature can feel noticeably hotter at 70% humidity than at 20% humidity - the heat index number captures that difference.

What do the risk categories (Caution, Extreme Caution, Danger, Extreme Danger) mean?

They are the National Weather Service's official heat-index bands: Caution (80-90F), Extreme Caution (90-103F), Danger (103-124F), and Extreme Danger (125F and above), each with an increasing likelihood of heat-related illness with prolonged exposure or activity.

What does the calculator show below 80 degrees apparent temperature?

Below 80F, the NWS regression formula the calculator otherwise uses is not defined, so Heat Index Calculator falls back to the simpler temperature-humidity average the National Weather Service itself uses at lower readings. The result switches to the full regression, with its correction terms, only once the apparent temperature reaches 80F.

Does the heat index shown here account for direct sunlight?

No. Heat Index Calculator reports the standard shade-value heat index published by the NWS. Full sun exposure can add up to 15F to the effective heat index per NWS guidance, so conditions in direct sun can feel meaningfully hotter than the number shown here.

Should I rely on this instead of an official NWS heat advisory?

No. This calculator is a general estimate for understanding how temperature and humidity combine, not a substitute for an official National Weather Service heat advisory or warning - always follow local NWS alerts for your area.
