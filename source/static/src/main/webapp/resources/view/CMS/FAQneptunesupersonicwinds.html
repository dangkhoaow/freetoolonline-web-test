Frequently Asked Questions

How fast are Neptune's winds?

Peak measured winds near the Great Dark Spot reached roughly 2,000-2,400 km/h (about 1,200-1,500 mph) - the fastest winds ever recorded on any planet in the solar system.

Why does Neptune have the strongest winds despite so little sunlight?

Neptune receives about 900 times less sunlight than Earth because it orbits roughly 30 times farther from the Sun. Why it still has the strongest winds is an open research question tied to internal heat, fast rotation, and low atmospheric drag.

What was the Great Dark Spot?

An anticyclonic storm roughly the size of Earth, discovered by NASA's Voyager 2 in 1989, centered around 20 degrees south latitude in Neptune's southern hemisphere.

Is the Great Dark Spot still there?

No. Hubble Space Telescope observations in the mid-1990s found no trace of it - unlike Jupiter's Great Red Spot, which has been observed continuously since at least 1831, it was not a permanent feature.

How fast does Neptune rotate?

About 16.1 hours on average. Neptune is not a rigid body, so different latitudes rotate at different rates - about 18 hours at the equator and about 12 hours near the poles, based on Voyager 2 data.

How does Neptune's wind speed compare to Saturn and Jupiter?

Neptune's peak measured winds (about 2,000-2,400 km/h) are the fastest known. Saturn's equatorial jet reaches about 1,800 km/h, and Jupiter's winds typically run 320-640 km/h with polar jets recorded up to about 1,450 km/h.

How big is Neptune compared to Earth?

Neptune's equatorial diameter is about 49,528 km, compared to Earth's about 12,742 km - roughly 3.9 times wider.

Is the on-screen storm shape to scale?

No. Neptune's cloud bands are drawn as flat artistic color stripes, not a photographic texture, and the storm's oval shape and on-screen size are stylized. Whether the storm appears at all in each era, and its approximate 20 degree south location, reflect the real published observations.

Where do these figures come from?

NASA's Neptune Facts page, the NASA NSSDC Planetary Fact Sheet, NASA/JPL's Great Dark Spot image sequence (PIA00045), and NASA's reporting on Hubble's storm-lifecycle observations.
