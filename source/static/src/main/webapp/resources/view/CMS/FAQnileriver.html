Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the Nile called the world's longest river?

The Nile River is approximately 6,650 kilometers long, making it longer than any other river on Earth. It flows from the highlands of East Africa (Lake Victoria region) northward across Sudan and Egypt to the Mediterranean Sea, traversing diverse landscapes including rainforests, highlands, and deserts.

Which countries does the Nile flow through?

The Nile passes through 11 countries: Uganda, Sudan, Egypt, Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania, Burundi, Rwanda, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Eritrea, and South Sudan. The primary sources are in East Africa, and the main delta flows through Egypt.

What is the ancient Egypt civilization connection to the Nile?

Ancient Egyptian civilization (roughly 3100 BCE to 30 BCE) developed entirely along the Nile River valley. The annual summer flood (June to September) deposited nutrient-rich sediment, creating a fertile corridor through the desert. This predictable inundation cycle enabled agriculture, supported a population of millions, and allowed the rise of one of history's greatest civilizations.

What is the Aswan High Dam and how did it change the Nile?

The Aswan High Dam, completed in 1970, transformed the Nile from a seasonal to a year-round controlled water system. It created Lake Nasser (one of the world's largest artificial lakes), enabled electricity generation, and allowed irrigation independent of the flood cycle. However, it also changed sediment flow and altered ecosystems downstream.

How much water does the Nile discharge into the Mediterranean?

Before the Aswan High Dam, the Nile discharged approximately 2,800 cubic meters of water per second on average. Modern discharge is lower due to irrigation demands and dam regulation, but the river remains a critical water source for the region.
