Frequently Asked Questions

Why doesn't the Okavango Delta flow to the sea?

It is an inland (endorheic) delta - the water fans out across a flat basin in the Kalahari and, in most years, disappears entirely through evaporation, plant transpiration, and seepage into the sand before it could ever reach an ocean.

Why does the flood arrive in the dry season?

The rain that feeds the delta falls in the Angolan highlands roughly October to April, but that floodwater takes about four months to travel the roughly 1,200 km downstream. By the time it arrives, Botswana's own rainy season has ended - so the delta's peak flood usually lands in the dry winter months, June to August.

How did the delta's thousands of islands form?

Many started as termite mounds. Sediment collects around the mound, trees take root, and over centuries the mound grows into a low, wooded island standing above the surrounding channels and floodplain.

What is Chief's Island?

The delta's largest island, commonly cited at about 70 km long, sitting largely inside Moremi Game Reserve - one of the richest wildlife areas within the delta.

Is the Okavango Delta a UNESCO World Heritage Site?

Yes - it was inscribed in 2014, and is often noted as the 1,000th site added to the World Heritage List.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the channel network, plain profile, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey hydrology.
