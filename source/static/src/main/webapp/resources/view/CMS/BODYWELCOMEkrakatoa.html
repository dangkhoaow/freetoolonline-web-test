Krakatoa 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-28

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Krakatoa as a collapsed island caldera in the Sunda Strait: three surviving fragments of the original island ringing a submerged basin, and Anak Krakatau - the young volcano growing back from the sea near the middle of that basin.

The 1883 eruption destroyed roughly two-thirds of the original island of Krakatoa, collapsing most of it below sea level in one of the most violent eruptions in recorded history. The blast is commonly cited as one of the loudest sounds ever recorded, reportedly heard about 4,800 km away, and the tsunamis it triggered killed on the order of 36,000 people around the Sunda Strait. What remained above water split into three fragments: Rakata, the largest, with a dramatic sheer cliff rising roughly 700-800 m; and the smaller Sertung and Panjang. Since 1927, a new volcano - Anak Krakatau, "Child of Krakatoa" - has been rebuilding near the middle of the flooded caldera, with most of its bulk still hidden below the waterline. A large flank collapse in December 2018 stripped away much of the young cone and triggered another deadly tsunami; the volcano has kept rebuilding since.

The whole Krakatoa scene - the submerged caldera floor, the three remnant islands, the regrowing Anak Krakatau cone, and the sea-level and 1883-outline toggles - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to swing around the caldera, and zoom in to compare the surviving islands against the translucent outline of the much larger island that stood there before 1883. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation - the caldera shape, remnant islands, cone, and 1883 outline are schematic teaching cues, not survey terrain.
