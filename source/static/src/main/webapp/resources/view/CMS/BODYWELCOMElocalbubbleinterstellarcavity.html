Local Bubble Interstellar Cavity 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-14

The Local Bubble is a cavity in interstellar space roughly 1,000 light-years across, and the Sun sits inside it today. A chain of supernovae beginning about 14 million years ago blew that cavity open in the gas between the stars.

Drag to orbit the view, scroll or pinch to zoom, and press Play or Pause to animate the timeline. Drag the slider to jump to any point in the bubble's 14-million-year history, click the Sun or any of the seven blue region markers to refresh the facts panel, and press "Show Perseus & Orion" to reveal two well-known regions that are commonly assumed to sit on this bubble's surface but, per the published research, actually belong to two separate structures.

Inside, the cavity is close to empty: gas at roughly 1,000,000 K and at least 10 times thinner than the Milky Way's typical interstellar gas, still pushing outward at about 4 miles (about 6 km) per second.

  • A 14-million-year timeline showing the Local Bubble's growth from its first supernova to today, with a live supernova counter and radius readout
  • Seven clickable star-forming-region markers on the bubble's surface, each opening its own published figures
  • The Sun's own drift into the bubble, from more than 1,000 light-years away to inside it about 5 million years ago

Readers who assume every nearby star-forming region sits inside the Local Bubble get a direct, sourced correction here: Perseus and Orion, two of the most famous nearby star-nurseries, are NOT on this bubble's surface. Students and teachers use the timeline to see how a chain of supernovae millions of years apart can carve out a structure a thousand light-years wide, and where the Sun itself fits into that history.

The whole scene renders on your device through WebGL: the vendored three.js engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and then stays cached, so nothing you scrub or click is sent to a server. No account, no upload, and the page reloads offline once the engine is cached.

The scene is an educational approximation of a real astrophysical structure, not a physics simulation. The bubble's growth curve, the Sun's straight-line entry path, and the supernova counter are illustrative, paced to the real cited totals above rather than a precise chronology of individually dated events - and the seven on-surface markers sit at fixed, evenly-spread illustrative directions, not each region's real reconstructed galactic coordinates.
