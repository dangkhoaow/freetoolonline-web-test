Recent Earthquakes Near Me (USGS Live Feed)

Last reviewed: 2026-08-04

Check the US Geological Survey's live earthquake feed for the past hour, day, or week, filtered by a minimum magnitude.

The page loads a default "past day, all magnitudes" lookup on open, so results are visible before you touch a control. Pick a time window and a magnitude floor, then press "Check earthquakes" to load matching events from the live feed.

Key features

  • Each result shows magnitude (color-banded by severity), place, time-ago, and depth, and the place name links to the official USGS event page.
  • Press "Sort by distance from me" for a browser-location lookup: results then sort nearest-first with a distance column in km and miles, computed in your browser. Without location, they stay most-recent-first.
  • A static magnitude-scale reference table (micro through great, with typical effects) stays on the page at all times, even before you run a lookup.
  • When nothing matches your magnitude floor, you get a clear "No earthquakes at or above that magnitude" message instead of a blank panel.

When to use it

Use it to see what has shaken your area recently, or raise the magnitude floor to track the largest events. It is a lookup, not an early-warning or alert system - data appears only after USGS processes an event, so follow local official guidance during an active emergency.

What each magnitude tier means

The tracker's on-page reference table breaks earthquakes into six magnitude bands, each with a plain-language description of the effect that size actually has. Magnitude 1.0-2.9 (micro/minor) is usually not felt at all and shows up only on instruments. Magnitude 3.0-3.9 (minor) is often felt but rarely causes damage. Magnitude 4.0-4.9 (light) produces noticeable shaking of objects indoors, with minor damage possible. Magnitude 5.0-5.9 (moderate) can damage poorly built structures over a small area. Magnitude 6.0-6.9 (strong) can be destructive across an area roughly 160 km wide. Magnitude 7.0 and above (major/great) can cause serious damage across whole regions. The magnitude-floor dropdown mirrors this same scale: choose "All magnitudes" to see everything the feed reports, or step up to M1.0+, M2.5+, or M4.5+ to drop the smaller, rarely-felt events and focus on the ones people are more likely to notice.

Your location stays in your browser

The tool calls the public USGS feed directly from your browser, so no server of ours is in the request path. It reads your device location only through the browser Geolocation API when you press the distance button, with a graceful fallback if you deny it - there is no address search, and it does not store which locations you check. Coverage is strongest for the US and networked regions, so small quakes elsewhere may be missing.
