Tyrannosaurus rex 3D Viewer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-17

This page renders a Tyrannosaurus rex as a 3D model you can spin in the browser: drag to rotate, pinch to zoom, add a 1.8 m person for scale, and click a body part for a fact.

Tyrannosaurus rex lived in the Late Cretaceous, roughly 68-66 million years ago, in western North America - about 12.3 m long, 3.7 m tall at the hips, weighing near 8.9 tonnes, with around 60 serrated teeth up to 20 cm long.

The first named skeleton was described in 1902 from a Montana find by Barnum Brown. The model shows its short two-fingered arms and heavy, head-balancing tail.

Everything runs on your device with WebGL - no account, nothing sent to a server. Skin tone and pattern are an artistic reconstruction, since fossils preserve no color or soft tissue; the numbers above are real published values.

What the model does when you touch it

Clicking the model is part-specific: tap the head, neck, body, tail, or a leg and the facts panel swaps to a short note for that part rather than showing one static blurb, so a click on the tail explains the heavy counterweight while a click on the head reads the roughly 60 serrated teeth up to 20 cm long. The Size vs human toggle does not drop in a fixed silhouette - it places a 1.8 m person and scales it to the honest length ratio against the reconstructed 12.3 m body, so the person reads correctly against the hips at about 3.7 m. Three more controls sit under the canvas: Stop rotation / Auto-rotate freezes or resumes the turntable spin, Idle motion adds a slow sway on the hind legs so a paused model still looks alive, and Fullscreen hands the wrapper the whole screen for a phone in portrait. The geometry is procedural and drawn with WebGL through a site-vendored three.js build that lazy-loads after the page paints and is then cached, so the first spin may wait a beat on a cold load but repeat visits start immediately. Nothing is uploaded and no account is needed - the trade-off against a phone AR app is that you lose room-scale placement but keep a labeled, measured model that works on a laptop with no install.
