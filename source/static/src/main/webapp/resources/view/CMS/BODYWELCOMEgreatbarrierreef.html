Great Barrier Reef 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-07-28

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows the Great Barrier Reef the way it actually forms - not a single wall of coral, but a dense, patchy field of individual reefs and channels running along the edge of the Queensland continental shelf. A shallow lagoon separates the reef from the mainland coast, and beyond the reef the shelf drops steeply into the open Coral Sea. Toggle the coral bleaching view to see how reef color shifts under heat stress, or switch on the Heart Reef marker over a well-known individual reef near the Whitsunday Islands.

The reef system runs about 2,300 km along the Queensland coast and covers roughly 344,400 sq km - large enough that it is often described as the only living structure visible from space. It is made up of more than 2,900 separate reefs and over 900 islands and cays, not one continuous barrier, which is why the scene shows open channels between reef patches rather than a solid ridge. Rising ocean temperatures have triggered several mass coral bleaching events on the reef, documented in 2016, 2017, 2020, and 2022.

  • Toggle the coral bleaching view to compare healthy reef coloring with a pale, bleached look
  • Toggle the Heart Reef marker to highlight a well-known individual reef
  • Read length, area, reef count, and lagoon depth figures in the panel
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

For another shallow, life-dense water body with a very different profile, open the Lake Victoria 3D Explorer, or compare a marine basin under environmental stress in the Black Sea 3D Explorer.

The reef scene is drawn right in your browser using WebGL, so orbiting the patchy reef field and toggling the bleaching view never sends anything about your visit to a server. The vendored three.js engine that builds the geometry downloads a single time (roughly 0.7 MB) and then stays cached, so returning to explore the lagoon and Coral Sea drop-off reloads instantly. Should your device lack WebGL support, the page says so plainly and still shows the reef facts panel.

This is an educational approximation - the reef pattern, lagoon, and vertical scale are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
