Touchscreen Test

Last reviewed: 2026-07-21

Check single and multi-touch contacts on a local test pad in the browser.


Key features

  • Enable touch listening arms the pad; the first touch or click on the pad also arms it.
  • touchstart, touchmove, and touchend update up to five contact dots and the status text.
  • A pointer fallback mirrors contacts for desktop browsers without a touchscreen, so a mouse works too.
  • Reset counters clears touches, moves, and maxFingers and hides the dots.
  • A status line is visible before your first touch, so you can confirm the pad is ready.

How to run the test

Click Enable touch listening, then touch or click the pad to see contact dots and counters. On a desktop without a touchscreen, click and drag with the mouse - the pointer fallback updates the same readout.


Privacy and data handling

Reset counters anytime; nothing is uploaded and no account is required. No camera or microphone permission is requested, and coordinates stay in this browser tab.

Reading the touch readout to spot a hardware fault

The pad tracks up to five contact points at once, so it doubles as a quick check that a phone or tablet digitizer registers every simultaneous finger the panel claims to support: press five fingers down together and watch whether five dots appear or one is missing. The maxFingers figure holds the highest number of contacts seen in a session, so it is the value to note when you are confirming five-point versus single-touch support. Drag one finger slowly across the glass and the moving dot should follow without stalling or jumping; a dot that freezes over one patch points to a dead zone, and a dot that surfaces with no finger on the screen points to a ghost or stuck contact. On a laptop with no touchscreen the pointer fallback mirrors the same readout from a mouse or trackpad, so you can rehearse the check before testing on the touch device itself. Clear the counters between runs to start a fresh count after wiping the glass, and the status line above the pad confirms the test is armed before your first touch.
