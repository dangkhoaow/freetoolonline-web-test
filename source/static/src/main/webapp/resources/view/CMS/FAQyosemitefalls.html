Frequently Asked Questions

How tall is Yosemite Falls?

About 739 m (2,425 ft) in total across three tiers, making it the tallest waterfall in North America: Upper Yosemite Fall (about 436 m), the Middle Cascades (about 206 m), and Lower Yosemite Fall (about 98 m).

Why does it drop in three tiers instead of one?

The granite cliff in Yosemite Valley has two natural benches partway down. Yosemite Creek falls freely off the top bench as Upper Yosemite Fall, crosses a bench and continues through the Middle Cascades, then makes a final drop as Lower Yosemite Fall to the valley floor.

Why does the flow change so much by season?

Yosemite Creek is fed almost entirely by high-country snowmelt, not a glacier or large lake. Flow typically peaks in May or June as the snowpack melts, then drops through the summer and can shrink to a trickle - sometimes running nearly dry - by August or September.

Where is Yosemite Falls?

In Yosemite Valley, within Yosemite National Park, California, USA - a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1984.

Is Yosemite Falls the tallest waterfall in the world?

No. It is the tallest in North America. Taller waterfalls exist elsewhere, including Angel Falls in Venezuela, which drops about 979 m in total with a single 807 m free-fall segment.

Is this a scientific hydrological model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the cliff shape, bench profile, and water columns are schematic teaching cues, not a survey-grade model.
