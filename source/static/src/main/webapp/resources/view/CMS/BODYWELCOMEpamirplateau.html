The Pamir Plateau is the world's highest mountain knot - a massive confluence where five major ranges (Tian Shan, Hindu Kush, Karakoram, Kunlun, and Hindu Raj) converge across Tajikistan, Afghanistan, Kyrgyzstan, Pakistan, and Xinjiang. Explore Pik Ismail Samani, the 7,495-meter sovereign peak at the Pamir's heart.

Indigenous name Bam-i Dunya (Roof of the World)

Geologic context: The Pamir formed through the collision of the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates, beginning about 40-50 million years ago. The plateau remains tectonically active, with ongoing uplift sustaining its extreme elevation. Alpine tundra vegetation and permanent glaciers cover much of the high terrain. Access to the Pamir is restricted by extreme altitude, harsh climate, and international borders - the region remains one of Earth's most remote mountain plateaus.
