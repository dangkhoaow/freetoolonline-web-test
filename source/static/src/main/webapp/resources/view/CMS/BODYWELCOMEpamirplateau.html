Last reviewed: 2026-07-31 | Byline: freetoolonline editorial

The Pamir Plateau is the world's highest and largest plateau, a mountainous expanse where six major mountain ranges converge - the Hindu Kush, Karakoram, Tian Shan, Kunlun, and the plateau's own ridges. This intersection, sometimes called "the knot of the world's valleys," creates a dramatic tapestry of peaks, valleys, and passes. The plateau rises above 4,000 meters over much of its 386,400 square kilometers, with numerous peaks exceeding 7,000 meters. Peak Communism (historically Pik Stalin), the plateau's highest point at about 7,495 meters, stands as the third-highest mountain in Asia after Everest and K2. The thin air, extreme climate, and rugged terrain make the Pamir one of Earth's most remote and least populated major plateaus, though it has been a crossroads for trade routes for millennia.
