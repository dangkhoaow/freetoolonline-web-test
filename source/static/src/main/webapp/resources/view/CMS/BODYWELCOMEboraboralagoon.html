Bora Bora Lagoon 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Bora Bora as a cross-section: two rocky peaks, Mount Otemanu and Mount Pahia, rising from a shallow turquoise lagoon that is bounded by a raised barrier reef carrying small sand-and-coral motu, with darker open ocean beyond the reef.

Bora Bora is what is left of a much larger shield volcano. Millions of years ago the volcano was a tall, active island; as it went extinct and the seafloor beneath it slowly subsided, the outer slopes eroded away and sank below the waterline while a fringing coral reef kept growing upward at roughly the pace of the sinking, staying near the surface. What remains today is the eroded volcanic core (the twin peaks) standing inside a wide lagoon, ringed by the reef that grew around the original island's edge - a textbook example of the "almost atoll" stage of volcanic island subsidence that geologists have documented across many Pacific islands.

The twin-peak relief and its reef-ring motu markers are built and rendered on your own device with WebGL, so orbiting the lagoon and flipping the toggles stays smooth after the vendored three.js engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is cached for return visits.

Reading the reef and the peaks

Two toggles carry most of the teaching value here. The lagoon depth-band coloring maps the color-coded shift from shallow turquoise water near the reef to deeper blue water closer to the peaks, so the lagoon's shallow, gentle basin reads at a glance instead of as a flat description, and the motu markers pick out the small sand-and-coral islets that sit on top of the barrier reef ring, visible evidence of the reef's own growth around the sinking volcanic island. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, and your toggle choices are saved in browser storage so the scene reopens the way you last left it.

This is an educational approximation - the peak proportions, reef shape, and motu placement are schematic teaching cues, not a survey-grade bathymetric model.
