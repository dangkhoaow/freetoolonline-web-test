When to Use the Olbers' Paradox 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-03

Open the Olbers' Paradox 3D Explorer when you want to see why the night sky stays dark instead of blazing bright, by toggling a naive infinite-universe model against the real, finite-age expanding universe.


Sessions this explorer fits

Students asking why an infinite universe full of stars would not make the whole sky glow, teachers walking through a centuries-old puzzle with Kepler, Olbers, Poe, and Kelvin's real dates attached, and curious readers who want a single-page answer to why the night sky is dark instead of piecing it together from several sources all get a direct answer here.


Sessions this explorer does not fit

Sessions this page does not fit are studying the mechanics of cosmic expansion itself, which is on the Expanding Universe 3D Explorer; seeing what today's leftover redshifted radiation actually looks like across the sky, which is on the Cosmic Microwave Background 3D Explorer; or working through a real photometric brightness calculation, since the shell layout and sky-coverage readout here are an illustrative teaching device, not a radiative-transfer computation.
