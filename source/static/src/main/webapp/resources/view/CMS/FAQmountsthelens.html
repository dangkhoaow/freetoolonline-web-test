Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Mount St. Helens crater shaped like a horseshoe?

The May 18, 1980 eruption began with a massive debris avalanche that stripped away the north flank, then a lateral blast tore the crater open toward the north - leaving a horseshoe-shaped amphitheater instead of a closed ring.

How much of the mountain did the 1980 eruption remove?

About 400 m of elevation - the pre-1980 summit stood about 2,950 m; today's summit is about 2,549 m.

What is the lava dome inside the crater?

A mound of hardened lava that has rebuilt episodically inside the crater since 1980, most recently during a 2004-2008 eruptive phase, partially refilling the space the 1980 blast opened up.

Is Mount St. Helens still active?

Yes - it is an active stratovolcano in the Cascade Range, part of the Pacific Ring of Fire, and is intensively monitored for renewed activity.

Where is Mount St. Helens?

In the Cascade Range in Skamania County, Washington State, USA.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the crater shape, lava dome, and pre-1980 outline are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
