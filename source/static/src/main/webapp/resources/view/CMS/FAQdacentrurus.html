Frequently Asked Questions

How big was Dacentrurus?

Commonly cited figures put adults around 8-9 m long and 5-7.4 tonnes, with a hip height of about 1.8 m. The holotype itself measured only about 7 m, and some estimates range up to about 10 m - sources disagree on the size of this genus. Turn on the human figure in the viewer to see the scale against a 1.8 m person.

Was Dacentrurus really one of the largest stegosaurs?

A pelvis roughly 1.5 m wide has been used to argue Dacentrurus was among the largest known stegosaurs, overturning older texts that described it as a smaller animal. This model shows the commonly cited length and mass range rather than a single invented number, because published estimates still disagree.

Where and when did Dacentrurus live?

In the Late Jurassic, roughly 154 to 140 million years ago (mainly the Kimmeridgian stage). Fossils have been found in England, France, Portugal, and Spain, making it one of the few stegosaurs known from mainland Europe.

Why is it called Dacentrurus instead of Omosaurus?

Richard Owen first named the holotype Omosaurus armatus in 1875 from a skeleton found near Swindon, England - the first stegosaur ever described from a European specimen. In 1902, Frederick Augustus Lucas renamed the genus Dacentrurus after finding that Omosaurus had already been used for a different, unrelated fossil reptile.

Is the model scientifically accurate?

The proportions follow published figures, but the skin color and soft-tissue outline are an artistic reconstruction - fossils preserve bone, not soft tissue or color. This model is not a fossil-accurate skeleton. The length, weight, and age figures shown are real published values, with ranges cited where sources disagree.

Do I need to install anything to view it?

No. The model renders in your browser with WebGL - no app, no account, and nothing about your visit is sent to a server. The 3D engine loads once and is then cached. When available, a free-licensed glTF model may swap in after first paint.
