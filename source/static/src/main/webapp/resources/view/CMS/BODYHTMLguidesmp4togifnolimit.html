What "no limit" means here (and what it does not mean)

The trim sliders on GIF Maker snap to the length of the video you actually uploaded - if the source clip is 40 seconds long, the duration slider can be set anywhere inside that 40 seconds; the tool does not clamp it down to a fixed short window the way some converters cap free use at 3 or 5 seconds. Why the trim window matters more than a marketing "unlimited" label

A converter that advertises "unlimited" but still asks for an account is trading one limit for another - the length cap disappears, but a sign-up wall replaces it. GIF Maker's actual constraint is simpler and stated up front on the page itself: the start-point and duration sliders can only select time that exists inside the video you uploaded, and there is a recommendation (not an enforced cap) to keep the trimmed segment to 3-10 seconds so the resulting GIF stays a reasonable size for chat apps and forums. Trimming longer than that is still possible - the tradeoff is simply a bigger file.

Because the width, height, and frame-rate controls are the same on every visit, and no account tracks how many times the page has been used, there is nothing to hit a quota against between one conversion and the next.

How the sliders actually work

After uploading, the start-point slider marks where in the clip the trim begins and the duration slider sets how long the trimmed segment runs - both bound by the uploaded video's own length, not a separate tool-imposed ceiling. Width and height inputs sit below the trim sliders (checking AutoW or AutoH scales the other side proportionally), and the frame-rate slider runs from 1 to 60 fps. None of these controls change or disappear depending on how long the source clip is or how many times the tool has already been used in the same session.

FAQ

Do I need to create an account to convert more than one video?

No. GIF Maker does not require a sign-up, an email address, or a login for the first conversion or any conversion after it.

Is there a maximum GIF length I can create?

The duration slider is bound by the length of the video you upload, not by a separate fixed cap. The page recommends trimming to 3-10 seconds for smaller, faster-loading files in chat apps, but that is a suggestion shown on the page, not a limit the slider enforces.

Does the free version watermark the GIF or restrict the frame rate?

The frame-rate slider runs the same 1-to-60 fps range on every use, and the width/height controls are available every time - none of the controls change based on a paid-vs-free tier.

What is the tradeoff if I trim a much longer segment than 3-10 seconds?

A longer trimmed segment produces a larger GIF file, which loads and previews more slowly in chat apps and forums - that is the reason for the on-page recommendation, not a rule the tool enforces.
