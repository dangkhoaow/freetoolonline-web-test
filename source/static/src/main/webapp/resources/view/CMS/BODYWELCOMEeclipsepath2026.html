2026 Total Solar Eclipse Path 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-05

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

On August 12, 2026 the Moon's shadow sweeps a real, published path across Earth - Siberia, Greenland, Iceland, the North Atlantic, and Spain. Scrub the path slider from 0 to 100 percent and watch a shadow marker move along that route while the panel names the region underneath it and reads out published local totality times.

This is the first total solar eclipse to cross mainland Europe since 1999. NASA and ESA maps place the path over Greenland's ice sheet, the west coast of Iceland near Reykjavik, open North Atlantic water, and northern Spain before it ends near the Mediterranean coast and a small corner of Portugal, just before local sunset.

  • Read published local totality times for Reykjavik and Leon as the shadow passes each region

Most places along the path see under two minutes of totality; only locations very near the center of the path in Greenland, northern Russia, or the North Atlantic get a little longer, though still under two and a half minutes. Alaska, Canada, and the northern United States see only a partial eclipse - a small bite out of the Sun's disk, not full darkness.

For the general mechanism behind any solar eclipse - why the Sun and Moon look almost the same size in the sky, how the umbra and penumbra work, and the Saros repeat cycle - see the Solar Eclipse 3D Explorer. That page teaches the physics with an illustrative, undated shadow sweep; this page shows the real geography and published local times for this one specific, dated event.

The globe, the yellow path line, and the moving shadow marker all draw on your device with WebGL, so scrubbing the path slider and orbiting the Earth stay smooth with no round trip to a server. The three.js engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is cached, so the next time you open the 2026 eclipse path it comes back instantly - and once loaded, the path sweep and the Iceland and Spain quick jumps keep working even if your connection drops. Nothing about the eclipse route or the totality times you view is uploaded; there is no account and no file to send.

This is an educational approximation, not NASA's precise eclipse-prediction map - the yellow path line connects eight published waypoint regions with straight spherical segments between them, not the exact center-line coordinates NASA computes from orbital mechanics. For exact local circumstances at a specific address, use NASA's or timeanddate.com's official interactive eclipse maps.
