How the Pteranodon size-vs-human toggle stays honest

The Pteranodon model is drawn to a fixed on-screen wingspan so it fits the canvas; the Size vs human control then places a 1.8 m person at the true wingspan ratio used by the viewer (about 6.5 m). Tap Size vs human under the canvas to show or hide the scale figure - the comparison is proportional, not a decorative sticker. Published adult-male wingspan still spans about 5.6-7.6 m, so the toggle picks one clear scale inside that disclosed range.


What a person would see beside a Pteranodon

What a person would see is a crested flying reptile whose tip-to-tip wingspan at about 6.5 m is roughly several person-lengths across, with membrane wings and a backward crest - turn on the person in the viewer and orbit beside a wing to feel how the span sits relative to the scale figure. Skin and crest color are an artistic reconstruction, not a fossil-accurate skeleton. The page states plainly that Pteranodon was a pterosaur, not a dinosaur.


What the Pteranodon size numbers are not

What these size numbers are not is a claim of one precise wingspan or weight: the Pteranodon size comparison discloses the literature ranges and uses about 6.5 m only for the Size vs human scale; it does not call Pteranodon a dinosaur, no free-licensed Pteranodon glTF is re-hosted yet so the viewer stays procedural, and this is not a win/lose game. Frequently asked questions

Can you rotate and zoom the Pteranodon model yourself, or does it just auto-play?

Drag to orbit the Pteranodon and use the scroll wheel or a pinch gesture to zoom; a separate Stop rotation toggle turns the auto-spin on or off, an Idle motion toggle adds or removes subtle breathing and leg movement, and Fullscreen expands the canvas - all independent of the Size vs human control described above.

What happens when you click the head, a leg, or another body part?

A short click swaps the species fact sheet for a note about the part under the pointer - the head, neck, body, a leg, and the tail each carry their own note. The viewer tells a click from a drag by distance: moving the pointer less than 8 pixels counts as a click, so a slow orbit will not misfire a part note. Click empty space to bring the fact sheet back.

Does viewing the Pteranodon model send anything to a server?

No - the viewer renders entirely in the browser using WebGL. Orbiting, zooming, toggling Size vs human, and clicking parts all happen client-side; there is no account, no upload, and nothing about the visit is sent to a server.

What did Pteranodon eat, and how does the model show that?

Pteranodon was a carnivore that fed mainly on fish, matched on the model by a long toothless beak built for catching fish rather than for chewing - a different head shape from a tooth-bearing land predator.

Where was the Pteranodon fossil found, and who named it?

Othniel Charles Marsh named Pteranodon longiceps in 1876 from a specimen recovered in the Niobrara chalk formation of Kansas - one of the earliest pterosaurs described from North America.

Does the Pteranodon viewer ever swap in a real 3D scan, like some other species pages do?

Not yet. No free-licensed Pteranodon model has been re-hosted for this page, so the viewer stays on its procedural reconstruction; a handful of sibling species pages do swap in a real scanned model once one clears the site's licence check.
