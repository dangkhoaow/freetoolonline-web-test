Son Doong Cave 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-29

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer has two views. The Surface view renders the karst massif directly above and around Son Doong from real measured elevation (Mapzen/AWS Open Data Terrarium terrain tiles, sourced from NASA/USGS DEMs) and real satellite imagery (Copernicus Sentinel-2), so the jagged limestone cone-karst hills the park is famous for are true measured terrain, not a procedural guess. The Cave interior view stays a schematic cutaway - a giant winding passage that widens into a vaulted chamber, with two doline openings where the ceiling has collapsed and let sunlight and jungle pour in, plus the river channel that cuts across the cave floor - because satellites cannot see inside the mountain; the passage itself has never been laser-scanned into an open dataset. Switch between them with the View button.

Son Doong sits in Phong Nha-Ke Bang National Park, Quang Binh Province, Vietnam. A local farmer found the entrance in 1990, but the passage was not surveyed until the British Cave Research Association mapped it in 2009 and confirmed it as the largest known cave passage on Earth by volume. Sections of the main chamber run up to about 200 m high and 150 m wide, and the two dolines are large enough to hold their own patch of rainforest growing on the cave floor below the light shaft.

  • Switch the View button between the real Surface terrain and the schematic Cave interior
  • Toggle the doline sunlight to show or hide the two jungle-lit skylight openings (interior view)
  • Toggle the river highlight to trace the Rao Thuong river channel (interior view)
  • Read passage length, chamber height and width figures in the panel
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

Everything renders on your device with WebGL. The 3D engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is cached.

Terrain: Mapzen/AWS Open Data terrain tiles (NASA/USGS DEMs). Imagery: contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2026. This is an educational approximation - the Surface view is real measured terrain shown at true 1:1 scale, but the Cave interior view's passage shape, doline openings, and vertical scale remain schematic teaching cues, not survey cave geology.
