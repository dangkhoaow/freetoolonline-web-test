This browser explorer shows a meteor shower radiant: meteors in one stream travel on nearly parallel paths, but perspective makes them appear to radiate from one sky point. The page compares the Perseids and Leonids.

Switch showers, scrub speed, and lengthen the streaks. Earth 3D Globe owns the planet view; this page owns the sky-geometry explanation for why a radiant appears.

  • Perseids and Leonids toggle
  • Parallel meteoroid streaks on a local sky dome
  • Radiant marker showing the apparent source point
  • Trail-length and speed scrubbers with play/pause
  • Perseids peak Aug 12-13, ZHR ~100, parent 109P/Swift-Tuttle
  • Leonids parent 55P/Tempel-Tuttle

Walking the controls, move by move

Five moves cover the whole scene. Run them in order and the radiant marker, the streak geometry, and the parent-comet contrast each land on their own.

  1. Open the explorer and wait for the local sky dome and the radiant marker to paint.
  2. Read the facts panel: Perseids peak Aug 12-13, ZHR ~100, parent 109P/Swift-Tuttle.
  3. Set Shower to Leonids to swap the parent stream to 55P/Tempel-Tuttle, then back to Perseids.
  4. Scrub Speed and Trail, and press Pause streaks to freeze the sky mid-flight.
  5. Drag to orbit and scroll or pinch to zoom - from a new angle the streaks still point back to the same marker, which is what proves the radiant is a direction rather than a place. Press Fullscreen for a projector.

Where the scene stops

Everything renders on your device with WebGL. The 3D engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is cached; no scene data is sent to a server.

This is an educational perspective-geometry sky scene - not a real-time meteor forecast or orbit integrator. For tonight's cloud cover, moonlight, or local visibility window, use a live observing app instead.
