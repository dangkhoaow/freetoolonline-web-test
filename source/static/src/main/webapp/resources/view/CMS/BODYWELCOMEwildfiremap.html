Live Wildfire Map - Active US Fire Incidents

Last reviewed: 2026-08-08

The map above shows every active US wildfire the federal interagency system (NIFC WFIGS) is currently reporting. Each dot is one incident: its size tracks the fire's acreage, its color tracks how contained the fire is. The list under the map ranks the same fires largest-first with acres, percent contained, county, and discovery date - loaded directly in your browser, no app, no account.

How to read the map

The map is a coordinate plot of incident locations on a longitude and latitude grid - deliberately not a street or satellite basemap, so nothing external loads and nothing about you is sent anywhere. Red dots are fires with little or no containment, orange and yellow mean crews have a partial control line, green means the fire is 90 percent contained or better. Grey dots are prescribed burns - planned, controlled fires that land managers set on purpose. Hover any dot to see the fire's name, county, acreage, and containment without leaving the map.

Filter to the fires you care about

The state menu narrows the map and the list to one state, and it shows a live incident count next to each state so you can see at a glance where activity is concentrated. The size menu hides the small stuff - show only fires over 100, 1,000, or 10,000 acres. By default you see wildfires only; the checkbox adds prescribed burns to the same view. If you want to know what is burning near you, the distance button asks for your location once, then re-sorts the list nearest-first with a distance column in kilometers and miles. The permission is optional, used only in this tab, and never stored.

Where the data comes from - and its limits

The incident list is the National Interagency Fire Center's WFIGS feed, the same reporting system US federal and state fire agencies file into. It covers US incidents only, and entries appear on the agencies' reporting cadence - a new fire can take hours to show up. That makes this page the right tool for situational awareness and the wrong tool for evacuation decisions: if a fire is near you, follow local officials, not any map. The Refresh button re-pulls the latest list whenever you need it.

Check smoke and weather alongside

A fire's location is only part of the picture. For smoke, check the live air quality checker - wildfire smoke moves hundreds of miles past the perimeter. For watches and warnings tied to fire weather (wind shifts, red flag conditions), the storm and weather alerts tracker reads the National Weather Service feed. The three pages cover three distinct questions: where the fires are, what the air is like, and what the weather service is warning about.
