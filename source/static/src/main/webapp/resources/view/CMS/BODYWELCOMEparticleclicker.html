Particle Clicker is a free browser particle-physics incremental from CERN Webfest 2014. Press Play, click the detector for data, research discoveries along a historic timeline, hire lab workers for automatic data, and buy upgrades with funding.

How to play

Click the detector to collect particle events and turn them into data. Spend that data on research discoveries, then hire lab workers who keep collecting data while you plan your next move. Funding upgrades speed up how fast the detector and your team gather data, so the lab keeps growing between clicks as you walk the historic particle timeline.

Key features

  • Press Play once to load the lab (about 2 MB, then cached), so there is no install and no account.
  • Your progress stays under ftol:particleclicker keys on this device, nothing is uploaded, and it never clears other games' saves.
  • A fullscreen button hands the detector the whole screen on desktop or phone when you want to focus.
  • The game works with a mouse or touch, and landscape mode works best on phones.
  • This build is adapted from the open source particle-clicker project (MIT, CERN Webfest 2014), and the in-game About panel still credits the original authors by name.

Depth: how the one-time 2 MB load and the Play now button actually work

Nothing downloads when the page first opens. The launch panel shows a Play now button, and only when you click it does the wrapper fetch the roughly 2 MB game and inject it as a cross-origin frame into the #pcStage area, removing the launch overlay. While that fetch runs, the #pcStatus line reads a loading state and then flips to a loaded message that tells you to click the detector for data, then research, hire, and upgrade. That single fetch is cached by the browser, so returning to the tab or reopening the game later skips the download rather than pulling the 2 MB again. The tradeoff is a short wait on the very first Play in exchange for no installer, no sign-in, and no background loading before you have decided to play. Because upload is disabled at the wrapper and the frame runs entirely in your browser, that one initial transfer is the only network cost the lab ever asks of you.
