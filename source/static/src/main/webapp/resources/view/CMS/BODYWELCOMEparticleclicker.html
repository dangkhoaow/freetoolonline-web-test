Welcome to Particle Clicker - a free browser particle-physics incremental from CERN Webfest 2014. Press Play, click the detector for data, research discoveries along a historic timeline, hire lab workers for automatic data, and buy upgrades with funding. About ~2 MB loads after you press Play. Progress stays under ftol:particleclicker keys on this device. Nothing is uploaded and no account is required. The workers you hire keep collecting data while you plan your next move, and the funding upgrades you buy speed up how fast the detector and your team gather it, so the lab keeps growing between clicks. A fullscreen button hands the detector the whole screen when you want to focus, and the game works with a mouse or touch. Landscape mode works best on phones. The lab itself traces back to CERN Webfest 2014, where a small team built the original incremental as an open source project and released it under the MIT license, and the About panel inside the game still credits those original authors by name. Because the license is permissive, this build keeps the same detector-and-research loop while running the whole thing locally in your browser, with no server, no runtime analytics, and no third party script pulled in after the one-time load.

Depth: how the one-time 2 MB load and the Play now button actually work

Nothing downloads when the page first opens. The launch panel shows a Play now button, and only when you click it does the wrapper fetch the roughly 2 MB game and inject it as a cross-origin frame into the #pcStage area, removing the launch overlay. While that fetch runs, the #pcStatus line reads a loading state and then flips to a loaded message that tells you to click the detector for data, then research, hire, and upgrade. That single fetch is cached by the browser, so returning to the tab or reopening the game later skips the download rather than pulling the 2 MB again. The tradeoff is a short wait on the very first Play in exchange for no installer, no sign-in, and no background loading before you have decided to play. Because upload is disabled at the wrapper and the frame runs entirely in your browser, that one initial transfer is the only network cost the lab ever asks of you.
