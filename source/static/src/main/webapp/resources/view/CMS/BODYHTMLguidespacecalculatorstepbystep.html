How to Use the Pace Calculator - Step by Step

Last reviewed: 2026-10-03

The pace calculator turns any two of distance, time and pace into the third, live as you type. These are the exact inputs, what each accepts, and how to read the result and the split table.


Step 1 - open the tool

Open the calculator and a full result is already on screen: the form loads pre-filled with a 5K in 25:00, so the required pace - 5:00 min/km, 8:03 min/mile - the speed in km/h and mph, and the checkpoint table are visible before you type anything.


Step 2 - pick what to solve for

The first select offers three questions: pace from distance and time (the default), finish time from distance and pace, and distance from time and pace. The input group being solved is hidden, so the form only ever asks for the two numbers it needs.


Step 3 - set the distance

Type any distance, or pick a race preset - 5K, 10K, half marathon or marathon - and the field fills with the exact figure (21.0975 km for the half, 42.195 km for the full) in whichever unit is selected.


Step 4 - enter the time or the pace

Time is entered as hours, minutes and seconds; pace as minutes and seconds per kilometer or per mile. Every change recalculates immediately - there is no submit button - and a zero or missing value shows a plain prompt naming what to enter instead of a broken result.


Step 5 - switch units if you need to

The unit select flips between kilometers (pace in min/km) and miles (pace in min/mile). Switching converts the distance and pace you already entered in place - a 5 km / 5:00 min/km plan becomes 3.11 miles at 8:03 min/mile - so the result stays equivalent instead of resetting.


Step 6 - read the result and the split table

The answer line is followed by the pace in both min/km and min/mile and the speed in both km/h and mph, so one entry covers a race plan, a treadmill setting and a watch reading. Below it, an even-pace table projects cumulative times at 1 km, 1 mile, 5K, 10K, 15K, half marathon, 30K and the marathon. The result block itself says what the numbers are: even-pace planning estimates, not a coaching, training or medical tool. Nothing you type is stored or sent anywhere, so note down the plan before closing the tab.
