AI Translator - Private, In Your Browser

Last reviewed: 2026-08-09

The AI Translator converts text between English and eleven other languages entirely on your device, so what you type is never uploaded to a server. It translates with Chrome's built-in engine when your browser has one, or with a neural translation model you download once and your browser keeps. A built-in phrasebook covers everyday phrases with no download at all.

Three ways the page can translate

The tool always tells you which engine it used, in the line under the output panel. On Chrome 138 and newer, the browser ships its own on-device translation engine: the page detects it, and when the language pair is available you can translate immediately with nothing to download from this site. In every other modern browser, one click fetches an open MarianMT translation model for your chosen language pair - about 115 MB, downloaded once and stored by the browser, so later visits translate without fetching it again. If you want nothing downloaded at all, the phrasebook at the bottom of the page answers the most common traveller phrases instantly in seven languages.

What stays private

All three paths run inside the browser. The text you type is processed by an engine on your own machine - whether that is Chrome's built-in translator, the downloaded model, or the phrasebook lookup - and this site has no translation server that could receive it. That matters when the text is a contract clause, a medical note, or a message you would not paste into a cloud translator. Once a language pair is stored, it keeps translating even with the connection off.

Languages and limits, stated plainly

The downloadable models pair English with Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Hindi, Chinese, Russian and Arabic - English must be one side of the pair. Chrome's built-in engine may cover additional pairs; the page checks each pair you pick and says so. Input is capped at 4,000 characters per run so the browser stays responsive - translate longer documents in parts. The on-device model is small (about 78 million parameters): everyday sentences come out solid, but idioms, slang and rare language pairs land below what large cloud services produce, so review anything important before you send it.

When another tool is the better fit

If you were looking for dots and dashes rather than human languages, the Morse code translator is the page you want - it encodes and decodes Morse instantly without any model. And when the goal is to shorten a text rather than change its language, the text summarizer runs the same kind of private, on-device processing to condense long passages.
