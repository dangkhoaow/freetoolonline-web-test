Old Faithful 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-27

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Old Faithful as a low sinter cone at the center of Yellowstone's Upper Geyser Basin, surrounded by runoff channels and a scatter of smaller steam vents. Toggle the timed eruption cycle to watch a sped-up plume rise from the vent, and toggle the runoff channel colors to see the thermophile-tinted bands that radiate outward.

Old Faithful is not Yellowstone's tallest or most regular geyser, but it is by far the most famous, thanks to eruptions predictable enough that park rangers post forecasts. Eruptions typically shoot about 106 to 185 ft (32 to 56 m) into the air, averaging around 130 ft, and last 1.5 to 5 minutes. The interval between eruptions ranges from about 44 to 125 minutes, averaging around 90 minutes - though that average has lengthened somewhat over recent decades, a shift NPS and USGS scientists partly link to earthquake-related changes deep in the underground plumbing. The geyser sits in the Upper Geyser Basin, home to the highest concentration of geysers anywhere on Earth: over 150 within about one square mile.

The whole scene - the basin flat, the sinter cone, the scattered steam vents, and the runoff channels - is built and drawn on your own device with WebGL through the site-vendored three.js engine. Orbit the scene to see the cone and channels, and toggle the eruption cycle to watch a sped-up plume rise from the vent. Nothing about your visit is uploaded to a server, and the engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is then cached.

This is an educational approximation, sped up for demonstration - the basin relief, sinter cone, and eruption timing are schematic teaching cues, not survey data or a real-time forecast.
