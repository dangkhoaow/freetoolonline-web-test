Mount Etna 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-01

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer shows Mount Etna on the east coast of Sicily, Italy - Europe's tallest active volcano. Where Vesuvius and Fuji rise as one steep symmetrical cone, Etna spreads into a much wider shield-to-strato profile, with four separate summit craters clustered near its top instead of a single vent.

Etna has been continuously active for at least 2,700 years, making it one of the most closely studied volcanoes on Earth. Its broad flanks are marked by dark lava-flow fans from centuries of eruptions, some of which have reached toward the city of Catania at the mountain's base. UNESCO recognized Etna as a World Heritage Site in 2013 for its exceptional record of ongoing volcanic activity.

  • Toggle the summit craters to see the four named vents clustered near the top (Voragine, Bocca Nuova, Northeast Crater, Southeast Crater)
  • Toggle the lava-flow fans to see schematic flow paths etched down the flanks
  • Read height, crater count, and heritage-status figures in the info panel
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

For other volcanoes, explore the Mount Vesuvius 3D Explorer for a single steep cone inside an ancient caldera ridge, or Mount Fuji 3D Explorer for a symmetrical stratovolcano cone.

Etna's craters, flanks, and lava-flow fans are drawn right in your browser through WebGL, so the whole volcano stays on your own device. The three.js engine behind the scene downloads a single time (about 0.7 MB), then is cached, so returning to spin Etna again is instant.

This is an educational approximation - the terrain, crater markers, and lava-flow fans are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
