Frequently Asked Questions

Where is Zhangye Danxia?

In Gansu Province, China, in the foothills of the Qilian Mountains. The geopark covers roughly 300 sq km across two main scenic areas at elevations around 2,000-3,800 m.

Why are the hills so colorful?

The rock layers contain different minerals and oxidation states - iron oxide produces red and orange bands, while other minerals produce yellow, green, and grey-purple bands, deposited over roughly 24 million years.

Why do the stripes cut across the hills at an angle?

Because the colored bands follow the original tilted sedimentary layers, not the modern hillside shape. Tectonic uplift tilted the layers after they formed, so the stripes run diagonally across today's rounded terrain.

Is Zhangye Danxia a UNESCO site?

Yes. It is part of the "China Danxia" UNESCO World Heritage Site, a joint listing of six Danxia landform areas in China, inscribed in 2010.

When are the colors most vivid?

Near sunrise and sunset, when low sun angles deepen the tones, and often right after rain when the wet rock surface increases color saturation.

What are the wooden walkways in the park for?

Elevated boardwalks and viewing platforms let visitors see the formations up close while protecting the fragile rock surface from foot-traffic erosion.

Is this a scientific terrain model?

No. It is an educational explorer using published figures - the hill shapes, stripe placement, lighting toggle, and boardwalk markers are schematic teaching cues, not survey data.
