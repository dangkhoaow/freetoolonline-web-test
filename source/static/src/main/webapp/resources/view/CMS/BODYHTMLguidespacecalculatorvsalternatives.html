Against mental math

Dividing 25 minutes by 5 km is easy; dividing 3:52:00 by 42.195 km is not, and converting the answer to min/mile means multiplying by 1.609344 without slipping a second. Here the division, the unit twin and the speed equivalents compute together, and the split table extends the one answer to eight checkpoints.


Against a GPS watch or running app

A watch measures the run you are on - live pace, distance, history, and it wins at recording what actually happened. This page answers the planning question before the run: what pace does the goal need, what does that pace project at each checkpoint. It is also the opposite trade on data: nothing you enter leaves the browser and no account is needed, but nothing is tracked or saved either.


Against a printed pace chart

A pace band or wall chart is fast and works mid-race with no battery, but it only lists the rows someone printed - round paces at standard distances, in one unit. The calculator takes any combination, shows both units at once, and re-renders the checkpoint table for exactly your pace rather than the nearest printed row.


Against a spreadsheet

A spreadsheet reaches the same arithmetic once the time-parsing and unit-conversion formulas are written, and it wins when you want a saved training log across weeks. For tonight's answer the tool is faster, needs nothing installed, and cannot slip a cell reference - at the cost that nothing is stored, so copy the plan out before closing the tab.
