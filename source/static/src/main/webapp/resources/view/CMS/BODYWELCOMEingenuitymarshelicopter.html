Ingenuity Mars Helicopter 3D Explorer

Last reviewed: 2026-08-12

Reviewed by FreetoolOnline editorial team

This browser explorer replays NASA's Ingenuity helicopter through 5 real flight milestones on Mars, and lets you compare its rotor speed side by side with a typical Earth helicopter.

Ingenuity landed attached to the belly of the Perseverance rover inside Jezero Crater on 2021-02-18. On 2021-04-19 it made history's first powered, controlled flight by an aircraft on another planet, climbing about 3 m (10 ft) and hovering about 30 seconds. Because Mars's atmosphere is about 1% as dense as Earth's at the surface, Ingenuity's four carbon-fiber blades, arranged into two counter-rotating rotors 1.2 m (4 ft) across, had to spin far faster than any Earth helicopter's rotor - roughly 2,400 rpm by design, later raised to about 2,700 rpm as the atmosphere thinned seasonally (it was ground-tested, not flown, to 2,800 rpm). A typical Earth helicopter's main rotor spins around 400-500 rpm - Ingenuity's rotors turn roughly five times faster to generate enough lift in Mars's thin air. Originally planned for just 5 flights over 30 Martian days, Ingenuity ended up flying 72 times, logging more than 2 hours of total flight time over roughly 1,000 Martian days, before its final flight on 2024-01-18: it reached about 12 m (40 ft), hovered about 4.5 seconds, then lost contact with Perseverance about 1 m above the ground while descending. Images downlinked afterward showed rotor-blade damage that ended its flying career, and NASA declared the mission over on 2024-01-25. These figures come from NASA JPL's Ingenuity mission pages and NASA's published mission-status updates.

  • Flight-milestone slider (5 stops) moves the helicopter to a real, published flight or mission-status milestone
  • Each stop shows its real flight number (where applicable), date, name, and altitude (where officially reported)
  • A running table lists all 5 milestones at once, with the active one highlighted
  • An Earth-comparison toggle reveals a second, smaller helicopter spinning at roughly one-fifth the visual rate, with both rpm figures shown side by side
  • Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom
  • Runs fully in the browser with the vendored three.js engine - no account, no upload

For the rover that carried Ingenuity to Mars and still serves as its data relay, see the Curiosity Rover Traverse 3D Explorer - a different rover on a different mission. For the terrain Ingenuity flew over, see the Mars Terrain 3D Explorer, built from real NASA elevation data.

Everything renders on your device with WebGL. The 3D engine loads once (about 0.7 MB) and is cached.

This is an educational approximation - the terrain, rover, and helicopter models are procedural shapes, not a terrain-accurate reconstruction of Jezero Crater, and rotor spin on screen is slowed for visibility rather than matching the real rpm. Every flight number, date, altitude, and rpm figure in the facts panel is real and independently published by NASA JPL.
