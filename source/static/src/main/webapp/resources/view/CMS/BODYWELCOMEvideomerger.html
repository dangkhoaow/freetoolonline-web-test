Merge Videos in Your Browser - Join Clips, Add Music and Transitions

Last reviewed: 2026-10-02
freetoolonline.com editorial team

To merge videos, add two or more clips to Video Merger, drag them into order, trim or mute any of them, choose transitions, music and a frame shape, then press "Merge videos". You get a playable preview, a summary of clip count, duration, resolution, size and engine, and one MP4 or WebM file to download.


Clip cards, trims and previews

Every clip gets a card in play order with a thumbnail, file name, trimmed duration, resolution and a landscape, portrait or square hint. "Trim" shows start and end sliders in 0.1-second steps, and the card's duration updates as you drag. "Preview" plays only the trimmed range; if the browser cannot play that codec, it says so, and the clip is still merged. Audio-only files are refused as clips with a message, though "Add music" accepts them.


Mixed clips in one frame shape

A merged video has one frame, so "Frame shape" sets it for the whole result. Original copies the first clip's shape - drag the clip you want to match to the front. "Quality" sets the short side: 1080p or 720p, plus 4K on desktop browsers whose encoder reports it can encode 4K. Auto keeps the first clip's resolution with Original, never above 1080 on the short side; with a fixed shape it takes the largest clip's short side, capped at 1080.

"Fit" handles clips whose shape differs from the frame. "Letterbox (show the whole clip)" scales a clip to fit inside and fills the empty area with black bars, so nothing is cut off. "Crop to fill" scales it until it covers the frame, keeps the center and trims the overflow at the edges - no bars, but a landscape clip in a 9:16 frame keeps only its middle. "Try two sample clips" loads one landscape and one portrait demo clip to compare both fits.


Transitions and background music

"Crossfade" overlaps two neighboring clips: the picture dissolves from one into the next while the two soundtracks crossfade, so each crossfaded gap makes the merged video shorter by the transition length. "Fade through black" fades the first clip's picture and sound to black and silence, then fades the next clip in, keeping the total length. The "Transition length" is shortened automatically when a clip is too short to hold it.

"Add music" lays an audio track - MP3, M4A/AAC, WAV, OGG, Opus, FLAC or another format the browser can decode - under the merged video, and the "Music volume" slider sets its level from 0 to 100% (80% by default). "Clip audio while music plays" controls the sound recorded with the clips:

  • "Keep" leaves clip sound at 100%, mixed with the music.
  • "Lower" turns clip sound down to 25% under the music - the default once a track is added.
  • "Mute" drops clip sound to 0%, so the music becomes the only soundtrack.

Two checkboxes start switched on: "Loop music if it is shorter than the video" repeats a short track to the end, and "Fade music out at the end" fades it over the last 2 seconds. A clip with no audio track, or one muted on its card, contributes silence, which keeps picture and sound in sync.

Check for yourself that nothing is uploaded

Open the browser's developer tools, switch to the Network tab and run a merge. The only requests the tool itself makes are for its own code files from freetoolonline.com: Mediabunny (about 0.7 MB), an AAC audio encoder (about 1 MB, only in browsers without a built-in one, such as Safari versions before 26) and FFmpeg.wasm (about 31 MB, only in compatibility mode). The browser caches each one after its first download, and your own files never appear in that list.


Merge, split, trim, edit or convert

Video Merger joins clips; it does not add text, captions or effects, or split one clip into pieces. Other video tools cover those jobs.
